## Pronoun — Clue Card · `C1B06-CC`

*(Bangla glosses permitted on the card; student-facing worksheet instructions English-only.)*

**A pronoun takes the place of a naming word (noun).**

| Naming word | Pronoun |
|---|---|
| a boy / Yusuf / father / brother | **he** |
| a girl / Aisha / mother / sister | **she** |
| a thing / an animal (cat, bird, book, tree) | **it** |
| two or more (boys, girls, birds, Yusuf and Nusair) | **they** |
| I + others (**Yusuf and I**, **Aisha and I**) | **we** |

*Person-shift note (teacher):* when **I am in the group**, we say **we** — "Yusuf and I read" → "**We** read." (Recognition only; students are not asked to *write* a "we" sentence.)

# Sunday — Block 5 reteach: text-based demonstratives

**Aim today:** move students from **picture-based** recognition (W5) to **text-based** understanding. The child now decides the demonstrative from a written hint — *(near, one)*, *(far, many)* — with no picture. All frame nouns are held (W1–W6).

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine (never cut) |
| 2 | 5–9 | Re-walk: naming word; recall last week's pointing words (fast bridge) |
| 3 | 9–19 | Re-teach **this/that/these/those** from the **text clue** (near/far · one/many), no pictures |
| 4 | 19–23 | Model the four frames on the board with written hints |
| 5 | 23–33 | **CW-1 done in class** (~10 min) |
| 6 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)

The fixed oral opener (never cut). Alphabet spot-check · numbers · greetings · one classroom command · one rhyme, as every session.

### Step 2 — Re-walk: naming word + last week's pointing words (5–9 min)

Fast bridge, not re-teaching. Under four minutes — wake the nouns and remind the class what *this/that/these/those* are before switching to the text method.

**Say (word-for-word):**
> *"Last week we learned four **pointing words**. Who remembers one?"*
> *(Take them as they come: this · that · these · those.)*
> *"Good. Last week the **picture** told us near or far, one or many. Today there is **no picture** — the **words** tell us. Listen carefully to the words."*

**Worked bridge (say and hold nothing up — this is the point):**
- *"If I say a thing is **near** and there is **one** — which word? … **This.**"*
- *"If I say **far** and **many** — which word? … **Those.**"*

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child still reaches for a picture / points at an object: *"No picture today. Listen to the words — near or far? one or many? Then choose."*
- Child gives a naming word instead of a pointing word: *"Book is a naming word. The pointing word points **at** the book — this, that, these, those."*

### Step 3 — Re-teach this/that/these/those from the text clue (9–19 min)

The heart of the reteach. Teach the **two questions** that decode any hint: **near or far?** and **one or many?** Build the four-cell answer from those two questions — spoken, then written as a chart, never as a picture.

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *"Two questions decide every pointing word. Question one: **near or far?** Question two: **one or many?**"*
> *(Write the empty four-cell chart on the board.)*
>
> |  | one | many |
> |---|---|---|
> | **near** | this | these |
> | **far** | that | those |
>
> *"Near and one — **this**. Near and many — **these**. Far and one — **that**. Far and many — **those**. Say them with me."*

**Do at least six spoken decodes — read the hint aloud, class answers before you fill the word:**
- *"near, one"* → class: *this*
- *"far, many"* → class: *those*
- *"near, many"* → class: *these*
- *"far, one"* → class: *that*
- *"near, one"* → *this*
- *"far, many"* → *those*

**The key move — hold the noun fixed, change only the hint, to prove it's the hint that decides:**
> *"'book'. Near, one — **This is a book.** Now same book — far, one — **That is a book.** Now many, near — **These are books.** Now many, far — **Those are books.** The book did not move on the page — the **words** changed the pointing word."*

*Bangla support (oral only):* কাছে + একটা → **this**; দূরে + একটা → **that**; কাছে + অনেক → **these**; দূরে + অনেক → **those**।

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child answers from only one question (*"far"* → says *that* when the hint is *far, many*): *"Far — good, that's one question. Now the second: one or many? … Many. Far **and** many is **those**."*
- Child gives singular word for a plural hint (*this* for *near, many*): point at the chart — *"Many things — we use **these**, not this."*
- Child forgets the frame (says only *these*): *"Yes — now the whole sentence: **These are books.** Many, so 'are'."*

### Step 4 — Model the four frames (19–23 min)

Fix the four sentence shapes on the board beside the chart, each tagged with its written hint — so the class connects **hint → frame**.

Write on the board:
> **This is a ___.**  (near, one)
> **That is a ___.**  (far, one)
> **These are ___.**  (near, many)
> **Those are ___.**  (far, many)

**Say (word-for-word):**
> *"Four sentences. One thing near — **This is a ___.** One thing far — **That is a ___.** Many near — **These are ___.** Many far — **Those are ___.** The blank is our naming word."*
> *(Read a hint, fill the frame aloud.)* *"'near, one — pen'. **This is a pen.** 'far, many — trees'. **Those are trees.**"*

**Worked examples — read the hint, class reads the finished sentence:**
- (near, one) chair → *"This is a chair."*
- (far, many) cows → *"Those are cows."*
- (near, many) pens → *"These are pens."*
- (far, one) house → *"That is a house."*

**One number note, lightly:** singular frames use *is a ___*; plural frames use *are ___* (no "a"). Keep it a passing correction — *"Many, so **are**, and no 'a'."*

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child writes *"These is books"*: *"Many — **These are**. 'are' for many."*
- Child writes *"Those are a trees"*: *"Many things — no 'a'. **Those are trees.**"*
- Child mixes near/far word with correct number (*these* for *far, many*): back to the chart, two questions.

### Step 5 — CW-1 done in class (23–33 min)

Distribute **CW-1** (`C1B06-CW1`). No pictures — each item is a sentence with a blank and a written hint.

**Model item 1 aloud before they start (word-for-word):**
> *"Number 1: '______ is a book. (near, one)'. Two questions — near or far? Near. One or many? One. Near and one is — **This.** Write **This**: This is a book."*
> *"Number 2: '______ is a cow. (far, one)'. Far, one — **That.** That is a cow."*

Then let them work. Move around and prompt with the **two questions only**, never the answer:
> *"Near or far?" … "One or many?" … "So which pointing word?"*

Ten items — students complete **CW-1 in class** in the ~10 min. **HW-1** goes home as separate independent practice.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child reads only the noun and guesses: *"Read the hint in the brackets — near or far? one or many?"*
- Child writes the right word but drops the frame: accept the demonstrative for the mark; nudge the full sentence for neatness.
- Child finishes fast: whisper-read all ten back, checking each starts with a capital and ends with a full stop.

### Step 6 — Exit Check (33–35 min)

One prompt per student (roll = 7); teacher **says a hint aloud** (no picture), child says the full sentence. Prompts: *near/one book · far/many trees · near/many pens · far/one house · near/one chair · far/many cows · near/many birds.* Answer alone suffices.

# Monday — Pronoun intro + match

**Aim today:** a **pronoun** takes the place of a naming word. Teach he/she/it/they, then **we** (person-shift: *X and I → we*). Recognition/matching only — no rewriting yet.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine |
| 2 | 5–9 | Re-walk: naming word (pronoun replaces a naming word) |
| 3 | 9–19 | Introduce he/she/it/they, then **we**; match to naming words |
| 4 | 19–23 | Model the matching on the board |
| 5 | 23–33 | **CW-2 done in class** |
| 6 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)

The fixed oral opener.

### Step 2 — Re-walk: naming word (5–9 min)

**Say (word-for-word):**
> *"A **naming word** names a person or a thing. Give me one."*
> *(Take four or five: Yusuf · Aisha · cat · book · tree.)*
> *"Today, a new kind of word — a **pronoun**. A pronoun **takes the place** of a naming word, so we don't say the name again and again."*

**Worked bridge:**
> *"Listen: 'Yusuf reads. Yusuf prays. Yusuf plays.' Too many 'Yusuf'! Instead: 'Yusuf reads. **He** prays. **He** plays.' **He** takes the place of Yusuf."*

### Step 3 — Introduce he/she/it/they, then we (9–19 min)

Teach the five in order, each anchored to a naming word the class can see or name.

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *"**He** — for one boy or man. Yusuf → **he**. Father → **he**."*
> *"**She** — for one girl or woman. Aisha → **she**. Mother → **she**."*
> *"**It** — for one thing or animal. A book → **it**. A cat → **it**. A tree → **it**."*
> *"**They** — for two or more. Yusuf and Nusair → **they**. The birds → **they**."*

**Then the person-shift — teach we carefully:**
> *"Last one — **we**. When **I am in the group**, we say **we**. 'Yusuf and I' — I am there too — so **we**. 'Aisha and I read' → **We** read."*
> *(Point to yourself and a child.)* *"You and I — **we**."*

**Do at least six spoken matches, class answers before you confirm:**
- Maryam → *she* · a pen → *it* · Abdullah → *he* · the cows → *they* · Nusair and I → *we* · a bird → *it*

*Bangla support (oral only):* একজন ছেলে → he; একজন মেয়ে → she; একটা জিনিস/প্রাণী → it; অনেকজন → they; আমি + অন্যরা → we।

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child says *he* for a thing (*"a book → he"*): *"A book is a **thing**, not a boy. A thing is **it**."*
- Child says *they* for one: *"One bird — **it**. Many birds — **they**."*
- Child says *they* for "X and I": *"Am I in the group? 'Yusuf **and I**' — I am there. When I am in it, we say **we**."*

### Step 4 — Model the matching (19–23 min)

Write two columns on the board — naming words on the left, pronouns on the right — and draw the lines with the class.

> Yusuf —— he
> Aisha —— she
> a cat —— it
> the birds —— they
> Fatima and I —— we

**Say:** *"Draw a line from the naming word to the pronoun that takes its place."* Model two, then invite children to the board for two more.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Line drawn to the wrong number (a cat → they): *"One cat — is it many? No. One thing is **it**."*
- Child matches by first letter or guesswork: back to the meaning — boy/man, girl/woman, thing, many, or "I + others".

### Step 5 — CW-2 done in class (23–33 min)

Distribute **CW-2** (`C1B06-CW2`). Each item is a naming word; the child writes the pronoun that takes its place.

**Model item 1 aloud:**
> *"Number 1: 'Yusuf'. One boy — **he**. Write **he**."*
> *"Number 5: 'Yusuf and I'. Am I in the group? Yes. So — **we**."*

Then they work. Prompt only with the meaning question — *"one boy? one girl? a thing? many? or you and I?"* — never the answer.

Ten items — complete **CW-2 in class**. **HW-2** goes home.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child writes *he* for *teacher* without thinking: acceptable — the class teacher (Ustaz) is male; if a child asks about a female teacher, *"a woman teacher is **she**"* (oral note only; graded item is male).
- Child writes a name instead of a pronoun: *"The pronoun takes the **place** of the name — which small word? he, she, it, they, or we?"*

### Step 6 — Exit Check (33–35 min)

One prompt per student (roll = 7); teacher says a naming word, child says the pronoun. Prompts: *Aisha · a book · Yusuf and Nusair · father · Maryam and I · the cats · Abdullah.*

# Tuesday — Choose the pronoun

**Aim today:** the child now meets the pronoun **inside a sentence** — the naming word is underlined, and they choose the pronoun that would replace it. This is the last step before writing the rewrite.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine |
| 2 | 5–10 | Re-walk: yesterday's five pronouns (fast) |
| 3 | 10–18 | Whole-class: read a sentence, find the naming word, choose its pronoun |
| 4 | 18–30 | **CW-3 done in class** |
| 5 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)

The fixed oral opener.

### Step 2 — Re-walk: the five pronouns (5–10 min)

Quick recall, chart on the board: he (one boy/man) · she (one girl/woman) · it (one thing/animal) · they (many) · we (I + others). Fire six quick naming words, class calls the pronoun.

### Step 3 — Whole-class: choose the pronoun in a sentence (10–18 min)

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *"Now the naming word lives **inside a sentence**, and it is underlined. Find it, then choose its pronoun."*
> *(Write:)* *"**Abdullah** reads a book. — The underlined word is Abdullah. One boy — **He.**"*
> *"**Aisha** has a pen. — Aisha, one girl — **She.**"*
> *"The **cat** is on the mat. — cat, one thing — **It.**"*
> *"**Yusuf and Nusair** play in the field. — two boys — **They.**"*
> *"**Nusair and I** read the Quran. — I am in it — **We.**"*

Do two or three more on the board with the class calling the answer.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child chooses the pronoun for the wrong word (picks *book* not *Abdullah*): *"Which word is **underlined**? That's the one we replace."*
- Child says *they* for "X and I": *"I am in the group — **we**."*

### Step 4 — CW-3 done in class (18–30 min)

Distribute **CW-3** (`C1B06-CW3`). Each sentence has the naming word underlined; the child writes the pronoun (He/She/It/They/We).

**Model item 1 aloud:**
> *"Number 1: 'Abdullah reads a book.' Underlined — Abdullah. One boy — **He.**"*

Then they work. Prompt with two questions only — *"Which word is underlined? What is it — one boy, one girl, a thing, many, or you and I?"*

Ten items — complete **CW-3 in class**. **HW-3** goes home.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child writes lowercase mid-sentence answers on a blank: fine here (they write only the pronoun); remind capital when it starts the rewrite tomorrow.
- Child replaces with a name or repeats the noun: back to "which small word takes its place?"

### Step 5 — Exit Check (30–35 min)

One prompt per student (roll = 7); teacher reads a short sentence, names the underlined word, child gives the pronoun. Prompts: *Raima has a bag (Raima) · The cow is in the forest (cow) · Fatima and Raima are sisters · Nusair sits (Nusair) · Abdullah and I look (Abdullah and I) · The birds fly (birds) · Aisha reads (Aisha).*

# Wednesday — Full rewrite (S3) + ungraded rehearsal

**Aim today:** the child writes the **whole sentence again**, replacing the naming word with its pronoun (PD-039, S3 — no word box). This is exactly the Annual Q3 format. Targets he/she/it/they.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine |
| 2 | 5–10 | Re-walk: choose the pronoun (yesterday) → now write the whole sentence |
| 3 | 10–22 | **CW-4 done in class** — full rewrite |
| 4 | 22–31 | **Ungraded S3 rehearsal** for tomorrow's PT (fresh sentences) |
| 5 | 31–33 | Collect; remind about Thursday PT |
| 6 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)

The fixed oral opener.

### Step 2 — Re-walk: from choosing to writing (5–10 min)

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *"Yesterday you **chose** the pronoun. Today you **write the whole sentence** with it. Watch."*
> *(Write:)* *"'Yusuf reads the Quran.' Yusuf → He. Now the whole sentence: **He reads the Quran.**"*
> *"'Aisha has a white cat.' Aisha → She. **She has a white cat.**"*
> *"Start with a **capital** pronoun, keep the rest of the sentence, end with a full stop."*

**Two rules on the board:**
> 1. Change only the **naming word** to its pronoun.
> 2. Keep everything else the same. Capital start · full stop end.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child rewrites the whole sentence in new words: *"Only change the naming word. Keep the rest."*
- Child drops the capital (*"he reads…"*): *"The sentence starts here — capital **He**."*

### Step 3 — CW-4 done in class (10–22 min)

Distribute **CW-4** (`C1B06-CW4`). Each item is a full sentence; the child rewrites it using a pronoun. No word box.

**Model item 1 aloud:**
> *"Number 1: 'Yusuf reads the Quran.' Yusuf is one boy — He. Write the whole sentence: **He reads the Quran.**"*

Then they work. Prompt only: *"What is the naming word? What is its pronoun? Now write the whole sentence."* Never supply the sentence.

Ten items — complete **CW-4 in class**. **HW-4** goes home.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child changes *is/has* or the object too: *"Keep the rest the same — only the naming word changes."*
- Child writes only the pronoun: *"The **whole** sentence, please — He … reads … the Quran."*
- Child uses wrong pronoun number: back to one/many, boy/girl/thing.

### Step 4 — Ungraded S3 rehearsal (22–31 min)

Oral/board rehearsal for tomorrow's PT — **fresh sentences, not on any sheet, and disjoint from the PT** (Run Book §6.5). Three items, whole-class then a few children at the board:

> *"Brother helps father."* → **He** helps father.
> *"Aisha and Maryam read."* → **They** read.
> *"The duck is in the pond."* → **It** is in the pond.

**Tell the class:** *"Tomorrow's test asks the same kind of rewrite, with new sentences. Change the naming word to its pronoun, write the whole sentence."*

### Step 5 — Collect; remind about Thursday PT (31–33 min)

Collect CW-4 and books. *"Thursday: spelling and Bengali meanings, a pointing-word part, and a rewrite part — the same rewrite you did today."*

### Step 6 — Exit Check (33–35 min)

One prompt per student (roll = 7); teacher reads a sentence, child says the rewritten sentence aloud. Prompts (all fresh, disjoint from CW/HW/PT): *Yusuf helps mother · The cat drinks milk · Fatima and Raima walk · Father reads the paper · The tree is tall · Nusair and Abdullah run · Maryam reads a book.*
