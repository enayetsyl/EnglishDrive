## Word-Role Clue Card — Pronoun row added this week

> ### WHAT ROLE DOES THE WORD PLAY? — **কোন পদ?**
> *(In the exam this is called the **part of speech**. Role and part of speech mean the same thing.)*
>
> | Ask | Role | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | Does it **name** a person, place or thing? *(নাম বোঝায়?)* | **Noun** *(বিশেষ্য)* | teacher · mosque · water |
> | Does it show an **action**? *(কাজ বোঝায়?)* | **Verb** *(ক্রিয়া)* | play · help · write |
> | Does it **describe** a noun — *what kind / which / whose*? *(noun-কে বর্ণনা করে — কেমন / কোনটা / কার?)* | **Adjective** *(বিশেষণ)* | tall · kind · **my** book · **this** tree |
> | Does it **stand instead of** a noun? *(noun-এর বদলে বসে?)* | **Pronoun** *(সর্বনাম)* | he · she · it · **them** · **us** |
>
> **The boundary to remember — মনে রাখার সীমারেখা:**
> *my · his · her · our · their · this · that* **before a noun** are **Adjectives** (they describe *which/whose*).
> *(noun-এর **আগে** বসলে এগুলো **বিশেষণ** — কোনটা / কার তা বলে দেয়।)*
> *he · she · it · me · him · us · them* **standing alone** are **Pronouns** (they replace the noun).
> *(একা বসলে — noun-এর জায়গায় — এগুলো **সর্বনাম**, noun-এর বদলে বসে।)*
>
> **Placement —** what-kind words (*tall, kind*) go **before** the noun or after *is/are*. Which/whose words (*this, my*) go **only before** the noun.
> *(what-kind: noun-এর আগে বা *is/are*-এর পরে; which/whose: শুধু noun-এর আগে।)*

# Class 1 (Sunday) — Adjectives: describing words (*what kind / which*)

**Objective.** Students recognise an adjective as a word that describes a noun (*what kind* / *which one*), find the adjective in a sentence, and use one to describe.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–6 | Re-walk: the four word-classes |
| 2 | 6–13 | What an adjective does |
| 3 | 13–20 | Find the adjective |
| 4 | 20–27 | Describe with an adjective |
| 5 | 27–29 | Clue Card — describing row |
| 6 | 29–32 | CW-1 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Re-walk the four word-classes (0–6 min)

Write on the board: `teacher · play · tall · mosque`

> **Say:** "Before we start something new, let us remember what we already know from Block 1. Four of these words — look at them. Which word **names** a person or a place?"

**Expect:** "teacher" and "mosque."

> **Say:** "Good. A naming word is a…?" → **"Noun."** "And which word here shows an **action** — something you do?"

**Expect:** "play."

> **Say:** "Yes — *play* is a **Verb**, a doing word. We know Noun and Verb well. That leaves one: *tall*. It does not name and it does not act. Today we look closely at what it does — the **describing** word."

> **If a student already says "adjective":** "You are ahead of us — yes. Let us find out exactly what an adjective does."

**Teacher note — say both terms in class.** This week we call a word's job its **role** (noun, verb, adjective, pronoun) because it is the plainer word for the children. **But the exam question uses the words "part of speech."** Tell the class directly, and repeat it through the week: *"The job — the **role** — of a word is what the exam calls its **part of speech**. They mean the same thing. When the exam says 'identify the part of speech,' it is asking for the word's role."* Both terms should be familiar by Thursday, so no child meets "part of speech" for the first time in the exam.

### Step 2 — What an adjective does (6–13 min)

Write and read, one pair under the other:

```
a tree          a tall tree
a bird          a small green bird
```

> **Say:** "Read the first pair with me. *A tree.* … *A tall tree.* What has the word *tall* added? What does it tell us?"

**Expect:** "how tall it is" / "the size" / "what kind of tree."

> **Say:** "Yes — *tall* tells us **what kind** *(কেমন)* of tree. *Tree* names the thing; *tall* describes it. A word that describes a noun — that tells us *what kind* or *which one* — is an **Adjective**."

> **Say:** "Look at the second pair. In *a small green bird*, how many describing words are there?"

**Expect:** "two — *small* and *green*."

> **Say:** "Where does the adjective sit: **before** the noun, or **after** it?"

**Expect:** "Before."

> **Say:** "Most often, yes — right before the noun. *A **tall** tree.* *A **kind** teacher.* But an adjective can also come **after** the noun, when we use a joining word like *is* or *are*. Listen: *The tree **is tall**.* *Our uniforms **are white**.* Same describing word, same job — telling us what kind — but now it sits after the noun."

> **Say:** "So do not decide by asking only 'is it before the noun?' Ask the real question: **does this word describe the noun — tell us what kind, which, or whose?** If it describes, it is an adjective, whether it sits before the noun or after *is/are*."

**The placement rule (state it once, plainly).** A **what-kind** word (*tall, kind, cold*) can sit **before** the noun (*a tall tree*) **or after** *is/are* (*the tree is tall*). A **which** word (*this, that*) and a **whose** word (*my, his, her, our, their*) sit **only before** the noun — we say *this book* and *my book*, never *the book is this* or *the book is my*.

**Teacher note.** The two positions have names you do **not** need to teach the children: an adjective *before* its noun (*a tall tree*) is **attributive**; an adjective *after is/are* (*the tree is tall*) is **predicative**. Both are fully within this block — the binding papers use both (*"The tiger is a **strong** animal"*, *"Our uniforms **are white**"*). Keep the children's rule simple — *"a describing word is an adjective wherever it sits"* — and do not drill position. Position is **not** the test; the describing job is.

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student points to the verb as the describing word** (e.g. thinks *play* describes): "Careful — *play* is a doing word, an action. Describing is not doing. Ask: does this word tell me **what kind**? If yes, adjective. If it tells me an **action**, it is a verb."

### Step 3 — Find the adjective (13–20 min)

Write each phrase; read aloud; class names the describing word:

`The kind teacher · a healthy meal · the dirty spoon · The water is cold · a colourful flower · The boy is strong`

> **Say (item 1):** "*The kind teacher.* Which word describes the teacher — tells us what kind?"

**Expect:** "kind."

> **Say:** "Yes. And here *kind* sits **before** *teacher* — the most common place for an adjective. Good."

> **Say (predicative item):** "*The water is cold* — which word describes the water?" → **"cold."** → "Yes — and here it sits **after** *is*, not before the noun. Still an adjective, because it still describes."

Continue through each, always asking: *which word describes the noun?*

> **Say (item 4):** "*A colourful flower* — the adjective?" → **"colourful."** "It describes the…?" → **"flower."**

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student names the noun instead of the adjective** (says "teacher"): "That is the naming word — the noun. I want the word that **describes** it. What kind of teacher? → *kind*. That is the adjective."

### Step 4 — Describe with an adjective (20–27 min)

> **Say:** "Now you add the describing word. I will say a noun; you put an adjective **before** it and say the whole phrase."

Write: `___ tree · ___ meal · ___ letter · ___ friend`

> **Say:** "*Tree.* Give me an adjective before it."

**Expect (accept any held adjective that fits):** "tall tree," "big tree," "green tree," "beautiful tree."

> **Say:** "All good — each one describes the tree. Now *meal*."

**Expect:** "healthy meal," "fresh meal," "hot meal."

Take several answers for each noun. Praise correct placement specifically:

> **Say:** "Notice — you put the describing word right where it belongs, so each phrase sounds correct. *Tall tree* works; *the tree is tall* works too. What does not work is *tree tall* with nothing joining them."

**Teacher note.** Keep both the noun and the adjective from words the class already holds — the child is practising **using a describing word correctly**, not learning new vocabulary here. Accept the adjective either before the noun (*a tall tree*) or after *is/are* (*the tree is tall*); both are correct.

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student says a bare "tree tall":** "Nearly — but *tree tall* is not complete. You have two good ways to fix it. Put the describing word **before** the noun — *a **tall** tree* — or add *is* and put it **after** — *the tree **is tall***. Say both with me." *(Class repeats both.)*

### Step 5 — Clue Card — describing row (27–29 min)

> **Say:** "Look at our Word-Role card. The **describing** row is already there from Block 1. Let us read it."

Read the **Adjective** row and its Bangla gloss aloud with the class:

> **Adjective** *(বিশেষণ)* — describes a noun: *what kind / which / whose* — tall · kind · **my** book · **this** tree

### Step 6 — CW-1 (29–32 min)

Distribute CW-1. Model item 1 on the board:

> **Say:** "Item one — *The small bird sat on the leaf.* The underlined word is *small*. Does it name, act, or describe?" → **"Describe."** → "What class?" → **"Adjective."** "Write it. Continue on your own; what you do not finish goes home with HW-1."

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

Teacher reads each phrase; one student per prompt names the underlined word's role.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | The **tall** tree | Adjective |
| 2 | a **healthy** meal | Adjective |
| 3 | the **dirty** spoon | Adjective |
| 4 | a **colourful** flower | Adjective |
| 5 | the **kind** teacher | Adjective |
| 6 | the **big** mountain | Adjective |
| 7 | a **short** letter | Adjective |

# Class 2 (Monday) — *my / his / this* before a noun (still adjectives)

**Objective.** Students recognise that *my/his/her/our/their* and *this/that* **before a noun** describe *whose* or *which* — so they are adjectives — and identify them in a passage.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Day 1 |
| 2 | 4–12 | *whose* and *which* are still describing |
| 3 | 12–20 | Find them in sentences |
| 4 | 20–25 | Passage identify |
| 5 | 25–28 | Clue Card — the boundary line |
| 6 | 28–32 | CW-2 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)

Books closed. Write: `the tall tree · a healthy meal · the kind teacher · a colourful flower`

> **Say:** "Yesterday we learned the describing word — the adjective. In *the tall tree*, name the adjective."

**Expect:** "tall." Continue quickly through the four; class calls out the adjective each time.

> **Say:** "And where does the adjective usually sit?" → **"Before the noun."** "Usually before — or after *is/are*, as we saw. Hold that. Today we meet some words you might **not** expect to be adjectives — but they are, for exactly that reason."

### Step 2 — *whose* (কার) and *which* (কোনটা) are still describing (4–12 min)

Write, one column beside the other:

```
a book          my book          this book
a tree          his tree         that tree
```

> **Say:** "Read across the first row. *A book. My book. This book.* In *my book* — whose book is it? *(কার বই?)*"

**Expect:** "mine" / "yours."

> **Say:** "It tells us **whose**. And *this book* — **which** book?" → **"This one."** → "It tells us **which**. Now — do both of these words come **before** the noun *book*?"

**Expect:** "Yes."

> **Say:** "They sit before the noun, and they tell us something about it — **whose**, **which**. That is exactly what a describing word does. So *my, this, his, that* — before a noun — are still **Adjectives**."

> **Say:** "Remember our describing question from yesterday: *what kind, which, or whose?* Words like *tall* answer **what kind**. Words like *my* and *this* answer **whose** and **which**. All of them describe. All of them are adjectives."

**Anticipated error & correction — the key moment of the day.**
> **When a student says "*my* is a pronoun":** "I understand completely — it feels like one, and later this week you will meet words that really are pronouns. But look at **where *my* sits**. Is there a noun right after it?" → **"Yes — *book*."** → "Then it is doing an adjective's job: sitting before the noun, telling us **whose**. The **place** decides the class. Before a noun and describing it → adjective."

> **Say (reinforce):** "Say the rule with me: *my, his, this, that — before a noun — adjective.*" *(Class repeats once.)*

**Teacher note.** Unlike describing words such as *tall* (which can sit after *is/are* — *the tree is tall*), the possessives *my/his/her/our/their* only sit **before** the noun. We do not say *"the book is my."* To say it after the noun, English uses a different word — *the book is **mine*** — but *mine/yours/hers* are pronouns, taught later, not this block. Keep the children on *my/his* **before a noun**.

> **Say:** "Remember our placement rule: *what-kind* words like *tall* can go before the noun or after *is*. But *whose* and *which* words — *my, his, this, that* — go **only before** the noun."

### Step 3 — Find them in sentences (12–20 min)

Read each aloud; class names the describing word (the one before the noun):

`my uncle · his sister · our school · their village · this bird · that mountain · your teacher`

> **Say (item 1):** "*My uncle.* The describing word?" → **"my."** → "It tells us…?" → **"whose uncle."**

> **Say (item 5):** "*This bird.* Describing word?" → **"this."** → "It tells us…?" → **"which bird."**

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student skips *my/his/this* looking only for a "normal" adjective like *tall*:** "There is no *tall* or *kind* here — but there is still a describing word. What sits before *uncle* and tells us whose? → *my*. That is today's adjective."

### Step 4 — Passage identify (20–25 min)

Write on the board and read aloud slowly:

```
My kind teacher gave us a healthy meal. His small bird sat in the tall tree.
This green leaf fell on our clean table.
```

> **Say:** "Find **every** describing word — the plain ones like *kind*, and the whose/which ones like *My* and *This*. Call them out; I will underline."

Underline as they find them: **My, kind, healthy, His, small, tall, This, green, our, clean** (10).

> **Say:** "Ten describing words. Notice we have both kinds together — *kind, healthy, small, tall, green, clean* tell us **what kind**; *My, His, This, our* tell us **whose** or **which**. Every one is an adjective, because every one describes its noun — here each one happens to sit right before it."

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student calls *gave* or *fell* an adjective:** "Test it — does *gave* describe a noun, or show an **action**? An action → that is a **verb**, not an adjective."

### Step 5 — Clue Card — the boundary line (25–28 min)

> **Say:** "Let us add today's rule to our card."

Add, with the class, and read the Bangla gloss:

> *my · his · her · our · their · this · that* **before a noun** → **Adjective** (whose / which)
> *(noun-এর আগে বসলে এগুলো বিশেষণ — কোনটা / কার তা বলে দেয়।)*

### Step 6 — CW-2 (28–32 min)

Distribute CW-2. Model item 1:

> **Say:** "Item one — look at the underlined word. Is it before a noun? Then what class?" *(Take the answer, then:)* "Continue on your own; the rest is HW-2 if you do not finish."

# Class 3 (Tuesday) — Pronouns: words that stand *instead of* a noun

**Objective.** Students recognise a personal pronoun as a word that **replaces** a noun (subject: I/he/she/it/we/they · object: me/him/her/us/them), and tell it apart from an adjective that sits before a noun.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Day 2 boundary |
| 2 | 4–12 | Replacing a noun |
| 3 | 12–20 | Subject and object pronouns |
| 4 | 20–26 | The boundary sort |
| 5 | 26–28 | Clue Card — Pronoun row |
| 6 | 28–32 | CW-3 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall the boundary (0–4 min)

Books closed. Write on the board: `my book · this tree · his sister`

> **Say:** "Yesterday we learned that some small words are adjectives. Look at these three. In *my book* — what role does *my* play?"

**Expect:** "Adjective." *(Some may hesitate or say "pronoun" — that is the whole point of this week; do not be discouraged.)*

> **If a student says "pronoun":** "I understand why — it feels like a pronoun. But look at **where it sits**. Is there a noun right after it?" → **"Yes — *book*."** → "Then it is describing that noun — telling us **whose** book. Before a noun, it does an adjective's job."

> **Say:** "So *my, this, his* — before a noun — are all…?" → **"Adjectives."** "Good. Hold that firmly, because today we meet words that do the **opposite**. They do not sit before a noun and describe it. They stand **instead of** the noun — in its place."

### Step 2 — Replacing a noun (4–12 min)

Write both lines, one under the other:

```
Yusuf helps his mother.        He helps his mother.
The teacher likes the boy.     The teacher likes him.
```

> **Say:** "Read the first pair. *Yusuf helps his mother.* Who helps his mother?" → **"Yusuf."**

> **Say:** "Now the second sentence — *He helps his mother.* Do we still know who?" → **"Yusuf."** → "Yes — *He* stands **in the place of** Yusuf. We took out the name and put *He* there instead."

> **Say:** "*Yusuf* is a naming word — a noun. *He* is not naming anyone new; it is **standing instead of** the noun. A word that stands in the place of a noun is a **Pronoun**."

Underline *He* and draw a small arrow back to *Yusuf*.

> **Say:** "Second pair. *The teacher likes the boy.* Now — *The teacher likes him.* What did *him* replace?" → **"The boy."**

> **Say:** "See the difference from yesterday. *My* book — *my* sits **before** the noun and describes it: adjective. *He* — *He* sits **in the place of** the noun: pronoun. One describes the noun. The other **takes its place**."

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student says the pronoun "describes" the noun:** "Let us test it. Does *He* tell us **what kind** of Yusuf, or **which** Yusuf? No — there is no Yusuf left in the sentence at all. *He* has **replaced** him. Describing needs a noun to describe; replacing removes the noun. That is the difference."

### Step 3 — Subject and object pronouns (12–20 min)

> **Say:** "Pronouns come in two groups. Watch where they sit in the sentence."

Build this table with the class, writing each column as you say it:

| Subject (does the action) — **কর্তা** | Object (receives the action) — **কর্ম** |
|---|---|
| I · he · she · it · we · they | me · him · her · us · them |

> **Say:** "The **subject** pronoun *(কর্তা)* does the action — it usually comes first. *She helps her mother.* Who does the helping? *She*. Subject."

> **Say:** "The **object** pronoun *(কর্ম)* receives the action — it usually comes after the verb. *The teacher likes him.* Who is liked? *him*. Object."

Read each aloud, class repeats, then individuals:
`She helps her mother. · The teacher likes him. · We play in the park. · My father met them. · They live in the village. · Aisha gave the picture to me.`

> **Say (point to each):** "In *We play* — is *We* doing or receiving?" → **"Doing — subject."** "In *met them* — is *them* doing or receiving?" → **"Receiving — object."**

**Teacher note (this/that light dual-use).** *This* and *that* can also stand alone — *"This is my book"* — where they act as pronouns rather than adjectives. Note it **once, lightly** (Drive Plan §2 row 6); the graded work stays on the clear personal pronouns. Do not drill it.

> **Say (the light note, once):** "One small thing. Yesterday *this* sat before a noun — *this book* — and was an adjective. But *This is my book* — here *this* stands alone, so it is acting as a pronoun. We will not test this today; just notice it can do both, like *my* and *his*."

### Step 4 — The boundary sort (20–26 min)

The heart of the day — the Analyze step. **Slow down here.**

> **Say:** "Now the real skill. The same little word can be an adjective **or** a pronoun. The **place** decides. I will read a sentence; you tell me the role of the underlined word, and **why**."

Work through each, aloud, asking "before a noun, or standing alone?" every time:

| Sentence | Underlined word | Role | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **His** sister is a doctor. | His | Adjective | before the noun *sister* |
| The teacher likes **him**. | him | Pronoun | stands instead of the boy |
| **This** bird is small. | This | Adjective | before the noun *bird* |
| **They** live in the village. | They | Pronoun | stands instead of the people |
| **Her** brother helped us. | Her | Adjective | before the noun *brother* |
| My father met **her** at the shop. | her | Pronoun | stands instead of the person |

> **Say (item 1):** "*His sister is a doctor.* Is there a noun right after *His*?" → **"Yes — sister."** → "So *His* is describing whose sister. Role?" → **"Adjective."**

> **Say (item 2):** "*The teacher likes him.* Is there a noun right after *him*?" → **"No."** → "*him* is standing where a name would go — it replaced *the boy*. Role?" → **"Pronoun."**

**The critical pair — items 5 and 6.** These are the same word, *her*, in its two jobs. Do not rush.

> **Say:** "Now watch carefully. Item five — *Her brother helped us.* Item six — *My father met her at the shop.* Same word, *her*, both times. Are they the same class?"

**Anticipated split answer:** some say "yes, same word, same class." This is the exact error to correct.

> **Correction:** "No — and this is the most important idea of the week. In *Her brother*, is there a noun right after *her*?" → **"Yes — brother."** → "So it describes **whose** brother: **adjective**. In *met her*, is there a noun after *her*?" → **"No."** → "It stands in the place of a person: **pronoun**. Same word. Different **place**. Different class."

> **Say:** "And notice — *my, his, this* only ever sit **before** a noun; they never come after *is*. That is one more way they differ from a pronoun, which stands alone."

> **Say (close the step):** "So never ask only 'what is the word?' Ask 'what is it **doing here** — sitting before a noun, or standing in its place?' Before a noun → adjective. Standing alone → pronoun."

### Step 5 — Clue Card — Pronoun row (26–28 min)

> **Say:** "Let us add the Pronoun row to our card." *(Write it with the class.)*

Add the **Pronoun** row (subject + object lists) and read the Bangla gloss aloud:

> **Pronoun** *(সর্বনাম)* — stands instead of a noun · Subject: I/he/she/it/we/they · Object: me/him/her/us/them
> *(একা বসলে — noun-এর জায়গায় — এগুলো সর্বনাম।)*

### Step 6 — CW-3 (28–32 min)

Distribute CW-3. Model item 1 on the board:

> **Say:** "Item one — *She helps her mother.* The underlined word is *She*. Before a noun, or standing alone?" → **"Standing alone."** → "So we write…?" → **"Pronoun."** "Write it on the line. Continue on your own. What you do not finish goes home as HW-3."

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

Teacher reads the sentence; one student per prompt names the underlined word's role. Reasons in two or three words.

| # | Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **She** helps her mother. | Pronoun |
| 2 | **my** uncle | Adjective |
| 3 | The teacher likes **him**. | Pronoun |
| 4 | **this** bird | Adjective |
| 5 | **We** play in the park. | Pronoun |
| 6 | **their** village | Adjective |
| 7 | My father met **them**. | Pronoun |
| 8 | **his** sister | Adjective |
| 9 | **They** live in the village. | Pronoun |
| 10 | Aisha gave it to **me**. | Pronoun |
| 11 | **our** school | Adjective |
| 12 | The doctor helped **us**. | Pronoun |

Prompts alternate the two classes so the check tests the boundary, not a run of one answer.

# Class 4 (Wednesday) — Mixed identify, error-find, Self-try demonstration

**Objective.** Students identify Noun / Verb / Adjective / Pronoun in mixed sentences, find and fix a wrong label, and rehearse the graded Self-try format.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|

| 2 | 4–12 | Mixed identify (all four) |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)

Clue Card visible.

> **Say:** "Four classes on our card. **Noun** — names. **Verb** — acts. **Adjective** — describes: *what kind, which, whose*. **Pronoun** — stands instead of a noun. And the boundary we worked on: *my, this, his* **before a noun** → adjective; *he, him, them* **alone** → pronoun."

> **Say:** "One placement reminder: *what-kind* adjectives can sit before the noun or after *is/are*; *whose* and *which* adjectives sit only before the noun."

> **Say:** "Today we mix all four together — exactly like your exam."

### Step 2 — Mixed identify (4–12 min)

Read each aloud; class names the underlined word's role, then says **why**:

`The **tall** tree · **She** helps her mother · My mother **washes** the spoon · **our** school · a **doctor** helped the child · The teacher likes **him**`

> **Say (item 1):** "*The tall tree* — *tall*?" → **"Adjective — describes the tree."**

> **Say (item 2):** "*She helps her mother* — *She*?" → **"Pronoun — stands instead of a name."**

> **Say (item 3):** "*My mother washes the spoon* — *washes*?" → **"Verb — an action."**

> **Say (item 4):** "*our school* — *our*?" → **"Adjective — before the noun, tells whose."**

> **Say (item 6):** "*The teacher likes him* — *him*?" → **"Pronoun — stands instead of the boy."**

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student calls *our* a pronoun:** "Where does *our* sit?" → **"Before *school*."** → "Before a noun, telling us whose → adjective. Standing alone would make it a pronoun, but here it is describing the noun."

### Step 3 — CW-4 out (12–13 min)

Distribute CW-4.

> **Say:** "Part A item one — decide the role of the underlined word. Name, act, describe, or replace?" *(Take the first answer, then let them work.)*

**Teacher note.** Today's CW-4 is the exam-shaped task. Remind the class once more: *"On the exam this same question says 'identify the **part of speech**' — it means exactly this, the word's **role**."* Say both terms so no child is thrown by the exam wording on Thursday.

### Step 4 — CW-4 Part A, mixed identify (13–20 min)

Students work; teacher circulates and corrects at the desk. Take answers aloud at the end, asking "why?" for each — **especially the my/his boundary items**.

> **Say (while circulating, to anyone stuck):** "Ask the two questions. Does it sit **before a noun**? Then adjective. Does it **stand alone** in a noun's place? Then pronoun."

### Step 5 — CW-4 Part B, error-find (20–26 min)

> **Say:** "Part B is different. Each sentence already has a label written after it. Some labels are **right** and some are **wrong**. If it is right, write a tick. If it is wrong, cross it out and write the correct role."

Students work; teacher circulates. This is the Analyze step.

> **Say (take answers aloud):** "Number thirteen — *The tall tree gave shade*, labelled *Noun*. Is *tall* a noun?" → **"No — adjective."** → "So we correct it. Good — you are checking the label, not just reading it."

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student ticks a wrong label without checking:** "Read the word, then decide the role **yourself** first. Now compare with the label. Do they match? Only then tick."

### Step 6 — Self-try demonstration (26–33 min)

**Not collected. Not marked.** This is the rehearsal for Thursday's graded Self-try.

**Step 1 — teacher models one.** Write all three lines on the board as you say them:

> **Say:** "Watch me do one. My word is **kind**. Its role — *kind* describes a noun, tells what kind — so **Adjective**. Why: *it describes a noun*. Now my sentence: *A kind boy shared his food.* See — *kind* sits before the noun *boy*, doing an adjective's job."

**Step 2 — students try one.** Each picks a word from the CW-4 box and fills all three lines: word · class · why · sentence.

> **Say:** "Now you. Choose one word from the box. Write its role, write why, then write one sentence using it correctly. Three or four of you will read yours aloud."

Take three or four; give quick feedback **on the reason line**, not just the sentence.

> **Say (feedback model):** "Good sentence. Now your reason — you wrote *'it is a pronoun.'* Tell me **why** it is a pronoun." → *(student:)* "It stands instead of a name." → "That is the reason. Always write the *why*, not just the class."

> **Say (close):** "On Thursday you will do this for marks, with **different** words. Today is practice."

### Step 7 — HW-4 + Exit Check (33–35 min)

Distribute HW-4 (the task line is on the sheet), then run the Exit Check briskly.
