## §0 — The one design problem this block has to solve

Every other C3 block grades a word the child can see. This one grades a word whose correctness depends on a
**different word, in a different sentence**. That single fact drives every decision below.

It has two consequences, and both are failure modes this drive has already met in other forms:

1. **Answerability lives in the antecedent.** *"___ is new."* has no answer. *"Yusuf has a kite. ___ is new."*
   has exactly one. So the antecedent noun is not decoration — it is the item. This is why the `trigger` field is
   honest here rather than a formality (CR-032, ruled Q7 to be applied by hand).
2. **Ambiguity is the default, not the exception.** Add one more noun to the antecedent sentence and the item
   usually breaks. **CR-005** (no unique defensible answer) is therefore the highest-probability defect in this
   block, and it will not be caught by any script.

**The governing rule for every graded item in this block:**

> Exactly one word in the preceding text can be the antecedent for this slot, once number, gender and animacy
> are applied.

**A precise carve-out.** Ambiguity of *referent* is acceptable where it produces no second defensible *answer*.
*"Aisha helps Maryam. ___ is kind."* — a reader cannot tell which girl is kind, but **both readings give `She`**.
The graded act is producing the pronoun, and it has one answer. What is banned is ambiguity that changes the
answer: *"Yusuf has a book and a kite. ___ is new."* (It — but which? and a child may reasonably read either),
or *"The boy read a book. ___ was new."* (`It` for the book, `He` for the boy — both defensible, item dead).

**Practical construction rules that follow.** In any sentence pair carrying a graded blank:

- Never two nouns of the same number **and** animacy in the antecedent sentence.
- Force the choice by **gender** (a roster name), **number** (two names → *they*), or **animacy** (person vs thing).
- For first/second person, the person is set by the **preceding sentence**, exactly as Annual Q3 does:
  *"We were hungry. Mother made breakfast for ___."* → `us`.

## §B — Past mistakes not to repeat (the ones that can actually bite *this* block)

Starter Template Phase 3(B), filtered to live risks rather than recited whole.

**B1 — The ambiguous item (CR-005).** Covered in §0. Highest-probability defect here. **No script will catch it.**
Mitigation: every graded pair is read against the §0 rule by hand before it enters the manifest, and the
naturalness/uniqueness read is logged as a human gate.

**B2 — The rule box that prints the answer above the items.** On C5 Block 6 seven article+noun pairs sat above the
parts grading them: 12 of 38 marks copyable off the sheet, surviving four versions and the block's own audit.
The pronoun equivalent is worse, because a worked pair *is* the whole skill: a box reading
*"Yusuf → **he** · the kite → **it**"* hands over any item with a male name or a thing. **Ruling for this block:**
the rule box prints the **rule** and the **bare closed list** (subject: I, you, he, she, it, we, they; object: me,
you, him, her, us, them) and **no antecedent→pronoun pair at all**. Worked pairs live on the Clue Card and the
wall reference, which §3.17 exempts.

**B3 — Strict alternation in a small answer set (CR-020).** C2 Block 6b ran `has·have·has·have…` through 15 parts —
**108 of 275 marks winnable without applying the rule.** Block 8's set is twelve words, but it is *not* twelve in
practice: a part built from single-character sentences collapses onto `he`/`she`, and a subject/object part
collapses toward a two-value surface. Treated as a near-binary key: **max run ≤2, no strict alternation**,
balance item types *before* shuffling (Starter Template Phase 4), not after.

**B4 — HW key derivable from its CW (CR-006).** Each HW shuffled independently and re-verified; positional
overlap ≤35%.

**B5 — Cross-sheet sentence repetition (CR-009/CR-022/CR-026).** No sentence reused across the block's nine
sheets. Note the C2/C3 distinction the log already draws: repetition of a **rule-derived** item is defensible
(PD-044), repetition of a **recalled pairing** is not. A pronoun item sits closer to rule-derived — but only if
the antecedent is fresh; meeting *"Yusuf has a kite. ___ is new."* twice teaches the child the sentence, not the
rule. Treated as **no repeats**.

**B6 — The within-paper leak, blanks vs filled forms (CR-031).** `It is new.` in an underline part and
`___ is new.` in a cloze part are the same sentence, and the first hands over the second. The two normalise
differently, so the duplicate gate misses it. **Rule: no sentence appears in the block both with its pronoun
printed and with its pronoun blanked.** Checked with blanks normalised away, across parts and across sheets.

**B7 — Stale stated counts (CR-014/CR-025).** Every "[8 × 0.5 = 4]" recomputed from the printed items after every
edit, and blanks-per-item counted from the paper, not from the intent. C3B07 printed *"10 blanks"* over 11.

**B8 — Option list omitting a keyed class (CR-008/CR-017, seven occurrences).** If any instruction lists the
pronouns a pupil may choose from, that list contains every pronoun keyed on that sheet — checked against the
staging table in §E1, not against the full twelve.

**B9 — Clue that discloses the answer (CR-016/CR-019, at 2 — a third makes PATTERN).** Acute here: a Bangla gloss
naming the antecedent is very nearly the answer. **No per-item clues on graded pronoun items.** Bangla support
stays in the Clue Card and the Vocabulary Writing column.

**B10 — Items generated by pairing lists against frames.** Hand-written and read for meaning; a pronoun block is
exactly where frame-crowding produces *"The soil has a bath"*.

## §C — Class-specific limits (restated from Phase 1)

- **Cap, Drive Plan v1.9 §2 row 8 (as amended by PD-052):** personal pronouns — subject *I, **you**, he, she, it,
  we, they*; object *me, **you**, him, her, us, them*; **they replace a noun**. Fill-from-passage (Annual Q3).
- **Guardrail, verbatim:** no *mine/hers/ours/theirs*; *my/his/her/our/their* are **Block 7 adjectives**, not
  taught here; **no relative, reflexive or indefinite pronouns**.
- **Block 7/8 boundary:** before a noun → adjective; replaces a noun → pronoun.
- **this/that (Q3 ruling):** teacher script and Clue Card **only**. Not on any student sheet, not even as carrier
  text. No dual-class AK marking note is therefore needed (CR-015 avoided by design).
- **Bloom:** Understand/Apply dominant, simple Analyze, guided Create at the self-try. Climb within the block.
- **Rubric:** 4 marks, four criteria at 1 each.
- **Vocabulary status:** twelve block-local words (PD-051/052) — counted toward W7 load, **never dictation or
  spelling items**, not added to File 2, not held downstream.
- **Held scope:** the entire 195-word pool. W7 clears it. Antecedent nouns are unconstrained.
- **Tense guard:** present simple only. Present continuous belongs to Block 10 and must not leak into carriers.
- **Roll:** 17.

### Values guards with real bite in this block

- **§H.3 — the sacred-word guard, and this block is where it is easiest to breach.** A pronoun item works by
  referring back. *"Allah is Merciful. ___ created us."* would make a **sacred referent the basis of a graded
  blank** — the guard's core prohibition. **No graded item, on any sheet, in any part, takes Allah, the Qur'an or
  a Prophet as its antecedent.** Reverent teacher prose is untouched. This is stated as a checklist line on the
  master and as an explicit screen in §F.
- **§H.4 — non-mahram.** A pronoun block groups characters constantly (*"Yusuf and Aisha … **they**"*). Plural
  antecedents are built from **same-sex groups** (Yusuf + Abdullah; Aisha + Maryam) or **declared family**
  (brother and sister). Screened at authoring, not after.
- **§H.9 — attribution-in-place, and the W7 batch pushes straight at it.** This week's fresh words are heavily
  nature-lexical: *soil, crop, tree, leaf, field, corn, plant, source, winter, summer, pollution*. A pronoun
  passage about a tree almost writes *"It gives us fruit"* by itself. CR-004/CR-023 stand at **2 occurrences —
  a third makes PATTERN.** Every nature carrier is either plainly descriptive (*"The tree grows in the field"*)
  or attributed in place (*"by Allah's Will"*). No autonomous givers.
- **§H.8** no living-being imagery on graded sheets (text only; the block needs no pictures) · **§H.2** no music,
  no clapping · **PD-021** festival referent rule · **§H.5 (Charter v1.6)** eleven-name roster, now including
  Maryam and Fatima.

**Roster note.** The eleven names matter more here than in any previous block. A pronoun block needs many distinct
third-person referents — one male, one female, one plural-male, one plural-female per item family — and cannot
lean on two or three names without producing exactly the phrase-crowding of B10.

### E3 — De-patterning plan (CR-020 is the live risk)

Balance **before** shuffling, per Starter Template Phase 4:

1. Fix the item-type quota first — e.g. an 8-item subject part: 2 male-singular, 2 female-singular, 2 thing,
   2 plural. That forces `he·she·it·they` to roughly equal frequency **by construction**, before any shuffle.
2. Seeded shuffle, then **regenerate the key from the shuffled body** — never carry the pre-shuffle key.
3. Verify: **max run ≤2, no strict alternation**, on every graded answer set.
4. Verify each HW key against its CW: positional overlap **≤35%**, shuffled independently.
5. On mixed subject/object parts, treat the surface as **near-binary** and apply the alternation test even though
   twelve values nominally exist.

### E6 — Manifest design (CR-028 + CR-032, and the reason this block needs it stated up front)

Three rules specific to this block:

1. **`trigger` = the antecedent noun**, always — the held word that makes the item answerable. Never the pronoun.
   With the whole graded surface block-local (PD-051), this field is the only thing keeping the held-word gate
   from reporting itself vacuous, which is precisely how C3B07 lost sixteen items silently (CR-032).
2. **A passage cloze is itemised per blank** (CR-028 — a part represented by marks alone is unaudited). Each blank
   is one item; `text` is the **sentence carrying the blank**, so the duplication and PT-overlap gates compare
   real sentences. The full passage is carried once at part level for the human read.
3. **`stated_total` is transcribed from the printed sheet**, then recomputed from the key (CR-025/B7).
