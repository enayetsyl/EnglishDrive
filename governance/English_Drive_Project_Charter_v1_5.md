## Section A — Purpose & Root Authority

**A.1** This Charter is the project-wide policy governing the English Skill-Building Drive across Classes 1–5. It states what is and is not permitted; it does not describe how work is executed (Run Book) or what artefacts exist (Architecture document).

**A.2** This Charter is the root authority of the Drive. Where any other Drive document — Drive Plan, Vocabulary Pool or Batch Order, Block-Build Specification, Grammar Block, or Run Book — conflicts with this Charter, this Charter governs.

**A.3** Every Drive Plan applies this Charter to one class's actual book, Teacher's Guide, and binding assessment evidence. A Drive Plan may narrow what this Charter permits for its class; it may not widen it. Widening requires a Principal ruling under the relevant section below.

**A.4** This Charter binds every class equally. A rule stated here without a class-specific exception applies to Classes 1 through 5 without variation.

**A.5** This Charter does not itself specify a class's grammar depth, vocabulary content, or block count. Those are Drive Plan matters, built within the caps and mechanisms this Charter establishes.

## Section B — Governing Hierarchy

**B.1** The Drive's artefacts form a single dependency chain:

> Charter → Drive Plan (File 1) → Vocabulary Pool + Batch Order (File 2) → Block-Build Specification → Grammar Block (File 3)

Each artefact is built against the current version of everything above it in this chain. A new file's header states this build-against chain explicitly.

**B.2** Two categories of document sit outside this chain but govern it:

- **Binding, external:** the NCTB textbook and Teacher's Guide per class, and binding assessment evidence (§J). These are not authored by the Drive but constrain what a Drive Plan may teach and how it must format practice.
- **Build inputs, external:** the Class Skeleton, TG-Reconciliation, and Stability Report per class, plus the shared Bloom's Daily-Use Pocket (REF-18). These are consumed for context and traceability. They are not binding and carry no authority to override a Drive Plan or Block.

**B.3** Cross-class progression maps are informational only and are not required project artefacts. Nothing in this Charter depends on them.

**B.4** Class 3 holds no special policy authority under this Charter. Where Class 3's Block-Build Specification or blocks are used as a working reference by other classes in the absence of their own, that is a Run Book build-order matter (Run Book §4.3), not a Charter-level rule.

**B.5** Where a built artefact surfaces evidence that an upstream document (Drive Plan, or this Charter) needs correction, that correction is routed through a Principal ruling and logged — it is not applied unilaterally, and it does not change this Charter's status as root authority. The mechanism for this is a Run Book matter (Run Book §10, §12); this Charter's role is limited to stating that no downstream artefact may override it without such a ruling.

## Section C — Programme Duration Cap

**C.1** Each class's Drive runs inside a hard ceiling of **three months** from its confirmed start date. This is a maximum, not a target: a class's Drive Plan may run shorter where its spine and age band allow it, but may not exceed three months without a Principal ruling under C.5.

**C.2** The cap governs the full teaching window — grammar weeks, shared or paired weeks, and consolidation weeks alike — regardless of how many blocks a class's spine contains. A shorter spine does not extend the cap; a longer spine does not by itself justify exceeding it.

**C.3** A Drive Plan may draft a proposed schedule that runs longer than the cap as a working draft. Before that class's first week is taught, the schedule must be confirmed or compressed to fit inside the cap. Calendar lock (§M) is the binding gate for this confirmation — no class begins Week 1 with an unresolved cap conflict outstanding.

**C.4** A retention-gate hold (§F) that slows a class's pacing does not itself breach this cap. Where a sustained hold would carry a class's schedule past the three-month ceiling, that is escalated for a Principal ruling under C.5 rather than absorbed silently. *(See front-matter status table: this absorb/escalate threshold is Inferred, not independently sourced.)*

**C.5** Widening the cap for a class — approving a duration longer than three months — is a Principal decision. A Drive Plan may not extend itself past the cap on its own authority.

## Section E — Grammar Spine & Inventory (Map-Then-Decide)

**E.1 Spine order.** Grammar across the Drive follows one fixed, project-wide rung sequence: Word → Sentence → Noun → Article → Adjective → Pronoun → Preposition → Verb → Conjunction → Adverb → Sentence Types → Punctuation → Composition. Every class's grammar track is built along this order; no class may teach a rung out of sequence.

**E.2 Re-walk rule.** Each grammar block opens with a fast revision of the rungs below it before introducing its own. This applies at every rung, in every class, without exception.

**E.3 Book-bounded principle.** A class is taught only the structures its own NCTB book and Teacher's Guide actually use, openly and by name. No structure is taught because it "should" appear at that class's level — it is taught because the book uses it and the school's own papers test it. Teaching stops at the cap; it is never extended past it on the basis of convention, a prior curriculum, or an adjacent class's content.

**E.4 Class-specific derivation.** This Charter does not itself enumerate any class's block count, rung order within a class, or per-rung depth cap. Each class's Drive Plan derives and states its own grammar spine cut — rung by rung, with depth caps — from that class's actual book, TG, and binding assessment evidence, and is version-logged accordingly. Spine length and per-rung depth therefore vary by class; the spine *order* (E.1) and the *principle* governing depth (E.3) do not.

**E.5 Locking and widening authority.** Once a class's depth cap for a rung is set in its Drive Plan, it is locked. Widening a locked cap requires a Principal ruling. No block may be built or delivered against a wider scope than its rung's locked cap without one.

**E.6 Map-then-decide.** Where a binding assessment source (§J) tests a structure the class's book does not teach, the class does not draft teaching material for it on its own initiative. The gap is surfaced and flagged — naming the content, the source paper, and candidate spine placement — and paused for a Principal ruling. Only once ruled on may the cap be widened (or the gap formally accepted) and a block built or revised to match. Procedure for running this path is set out in the Run Book (§10.4); this Charter establishes only that the path exists and that a Principal ruling is the sole route to widening a locked cap.

**E.7 Open item — carried forward, not resolved here.** A live inconsistency exists between a Class 3 Drive Plan revision and this Charter's own record: Drive Plan v1.2 widened Block 10's verb scope to include basic present continuous at recognition level, on book/TG evidence, and flagged that the corresponding Charter §E statement ("Verb — present simple form") required a matching edit. No confirmation survives that this edit was ever made. Beyond closing that specific edit, a broader question remains genuinely unresolved: whether a locked, Principal-ruled per-rung cap is meant to be reconciled against a Charter-held record at all, or whether the Drive Plan's own §2 is the sole authoritative record once a cap is locked. This reconstruction does not resolve either question. Both are carried forward for a Principal ruling; until ruled on, Drive Plan §2 should be treated as the operative record for any rung where the two might disagree.

## Section F — Vocabulary: Book-Bounded Sourcing & Retention Gate

**F.1 Vocabulary-first, grammar-reuse.** Grammar teaching draws its examples and graded practice only from vocabulary students have already been taught and tested on. No grammar block introduces a new word for the purpose of illustrating a grammar point; the word must already be held (F.3) before it can be used that way.

**F.2 Book-bounded sourcing.** A class's vocabulary pool is drawn from that class's own NCTB book — chapter text and activities, unit by unit — and nothing else. Where a book's content is too thin to fill a target batch size, the shortfall is accepted; it is not padded with words the book does not supply.

**F.3 Held words and graded items.** A word is "held" once it has been released in File 2's batch schedule as of the current week. Only held words may appear in a graded item. Content outside a student's held vocabulary may still appear for exposure or context, but is gloss-marked and excluded from grading.

> *Carve-outs (pointer, not an amendment).* Three defined statuses admit a non-held word to a graded item without breaching this clause: **Grammar Exemplars** (Run Book §5.7, PD-009) — sole purpose is to demonstrate a rule, never counted as vocabulary load; **block-local teaching words** (§5.8, PD-012) — taught for the week, gradeable inside their own block only, counted toward load; and **override words** (§5.9, PD-011) — real taught vocabulary, spelling- and dictation-eligible, counted toward load. **PD-020** governs the choice between them and makes week load a decision input rather than an afterthought. None of the three amends File 2; all are build-time admissions, forward-only.

**F.4 Exam-tested, book-absent vocabulary.** Where binding assessment evidence (§J) tests a word the book does not supply, that word may still enter the pool through a defined exception — traced either to the specific exam item it answers, or tagged by source rather than by book unit. Unlike a grammar cap widening under E.6, adding such a word does not require a per-word Principal ruling. It must still pass curation screening against REF-01 (§H.10) and the standard lead-time rule before release. *(See front-matter status table: this asymmetry is Inferred, not independently sourced.)*

**F.5 Retention gate.** The class average on the weekly Performance Test sets the following week's fresh-vocabulary batch size: **≥80% → ramp up; 65–79% → hold; <65% → shrink and re-teach.** This threshold is fixed project-wide and does not vary by class.

**F.6 Retention beats coverage.** Where pacing and retention conflict, retention governs. A batch schedule's planned ramp is a default, not an entitlement — it yields to the retention gate, never the reverse.

**F.7 Pool stability.** A class's vocabulary pool is built once. Weekly batches are drawn from it; the pool itself is not re-tagged or restructured for routine use. Changes to a finalized pool follow a documented, versioned revision, made only on a Principal decision or an approved exam-mapping update (§J).

## Section G — Bloom's Taxonomy Bands

**G.1** Every teaching point and graded item is pitched to a Bloom's cognitive level, using REF-18 (the Daily-Use Pocket) as the operational reference for verbs, stems, and class bands. Where REF-18 and a fuller Bloom's reference disagree on a number, the fuller reference governs; REF-18 restates, it does not set policy.

**G.2** Each class carries a fixed dominant-level band, climbing toward higher-order exposure at its upper end:

| Class | Dominant | Higher-level exposure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remember, Understand | simple Apply, simple Create |
| 2 | Remember, Understand, simple Apply | simple Analyze |
| 3 | Understand, Apply | simple Analyze, guided Create |
| 4 | Understand, Apply, more Analyze than Class 3 | simple Evaluate |
| 5 | Apply, Understand, Analyze | Evaluate, guided Create |

Classes 1–2 stay Remember/Understand-heavy throughout. A class's band is fixed; a Drive Plan applies it, it does not set its own.

**G.3 Climb-within-block.** Within any single block, the cognitive level climbs — earlier sessions sit toward the lower end of the class's band, later sessions reach toward its upper end. A block never opens at its highest permitted level and steps back down.

**G.4 Age rule, both ways.** Do not under-demand an older class by staying at definition-only questions; do not over-demand a younger class by raising the cognitive level past its band. Language and context are kept simple for younger classes without lowering the level itself.

**G.5** Free-thinking and open-ended items follow REF-18's Islamic framing: the student's reasoning is invited, not left open-ended without anchor. The marking rubric for the Drive's own free-thinking task is set separately, at §I (4 marks, four criteria, uniform across classes); REF-18's generic rubric shape is a reference point, not the rubric this Charter binds classes to.

## Section H — Curation & Values Alignment

**H.1 In-house authorship.** All Drive content is authored in-house. Textbook content is used only to anchor and derive scope (§E, §F) — it is not reproduced as passage text. No internet-sourced text is used in student-facing material.

**H.2 Salafi/athari alignment.** Content is tawhid-centred. It excludes shirk- or bid'ah-adjacent material, and excludes other-faith festival or holiday content. *(Clarified by **PD-021**: the exclusion attaches to the **referent**, not the word — festival contexts are admissible where the referent is Islamic, such as Eid or Ramadan.)* It excludes music and instruments — where a device such as a rhyme or song would otherwise be used, choral recitation, spoken repetition, or du'ā/dhikr phrasing is substituted instead. Content stays modest and akhlaq-positive throughout, without exception by class or subject.

**H.3 Sacred-word guard.** Allah and the Qur'an are never used as a grammar-drill target — not as a fill-in-blank answer, not as a classification target (gender, article practice, or similar), and not placed inside any item whose mechanic requires manipulating it for grading. Where a sacred name appears in content, it appears only as a reverent subject, kept outside the graded mechanic itself.

**H.4 Mahram and gender-pairing rule.** No passage, dialogue, or example pairs an unrelated male and female character. Family relationships (e.g. brother–sister, father–mother) are permitted. Same-gender pairings are permitted. No other cross-gender pairing is used, regardless of how incidental it seems to the grammar point being taught.

**H.5 House-style names.** Characters are drawn from a fixed in-house roster — male: Nusair, Yusuf, Abdullah, Abdur Rahim; female: Aisha, Raima, Porshi, Rabab, Jesmin Akter — rotated across material rather than reused identically block to block. Where a binding assessment source (§J) itself supplies a name in an item being mirrored, that name may be admitted alongside the house roster for that item only. *(See front-matter status table: this exception is Inferred, not independently sourced.)*

**H.6 Permitted themes.** Salah, Qur'an, family, cleanliness, care for creation, the days of the week, and comparable Islamic daily-life material are the default well from which examples and passages are drawn. Content that would require non-mahram mixing to depict authentically is excluded regardless of how it is sourced.

**H.7 Greetings and standing phrases.** Islamic greetings and phrases (e.g. Assalamu alaikum, Bismillah, Alhamdulillah, SubhanAllah) may be used naturally where register allows, consistent with H.2.

**H.8 Living-being imagery.** Drive-authored material contains no living-being depictions, per the Curation Policy's faceless / no-living-being rule. Where an activity would conventionally use animal or human imagery (counting pictures, vocabulary illustration, and similar), a compliant alternative is used instead. *(Category evidenced in application to English-class content via the C-05 curation touchpoints in the TG-Reconciliation files.)*
>
> *Forward-only note (per §K.3):* **PD-016** (Principal ruling) admitted **faceless** living-being images as pointed-object picture cues for **Class 1, Block 5 (Demonstrative) only**, as an override of this clause. **As built, the override was not exercised:** during the C1 B5 build the Word Box was removed (PD-023), restricting all graded pictures to taught spellable nouns, and **no living-being imagery appears on any C1 B5 sheet** — so **§H.8 is fully observed in C1 B5 as delivered.** The PD-016 ruling and its **unresolved REF-01 escalation flag** (REF-01, §H.10, owned in Project 02, is the canonical source of the faceless/no-living-being rule and may require owner sign-off) **remain on record for any future block that elects to use faceless imagery.** This clause remains in full force for all classes and blocks, including C1 B5. See Decision Log PD-016 (status update 2026-08-02) and PD-023.

**H.9 Attribution of agency.** Where authored material describes a natural phenomenon acting — the sun giving light, rain falling, a river flowing — the agency is **attributed in place**: *"The sun gives us light **by Allah's Will**."* The alternative of restructuring the sentence to move Allah into the subject position (*"Allah gives us light through the sun"*) is **not** the required form, though plain description carrying no agency at all (*"The sun is very far from us"*) is equally acceptable. Rationale: a child composing freely writes *the sun gives us light*, because that is the word order English supplies; a structural correction is forgotten under exam pressure, whereas a three-word addition is a habit that travels to sentences the teacher did not anticipate. This clause governs **authored** material; in student work, attribution is corrected as a values matter and **never marked down as a language error** (§I.3). It does not disturb §H.3 — sacred names remain absent from graded items, and the attributed form lives in teacher script and board work. *(Ruled **PD-019**, project-wide; forward-only per §K.3 — delivered blocks are not retrofitted.)*

**H.10 Canonical values authority.** The canonical values authority for the school is the LOCKED Islamic Curation Policy (REF-01, per master decision D-020), owned and revised in Project 02. This section states the Drive-local rules most frequently exercised in English content authoring; where §H is silent on a category, REF-01 governs. Vocabulary pool entries are curation-screened against REF-01 at source (Run Book pool-build procedure), with exclusions logged in the Pool's "How to use" sheet. §H does not restate REF-01's full category taxonomy and must not be read as narrowing it.

## Section I — Self-Try / Free-Thinking Rubric

**I.1 Mandatory.** Every grammar block ends with a self-construction (self-try) task. It is never optional, and it is never replaced by a plain classify-or-identify exercise — it is the block's Create-level moment in the Bloom's climb (§G).

**I.2 Anchored framing.** The task is always anchored — to the block's rule, to a word from the student's currently held set, and, from Class 3 onward, to a first step naming that word's class. It is never framed as fully open ("write anything you want").

**I.3 Base rubric shape.** The marking rubric is criteria-based with a fixed point total of **4 marks**, four criteria at **1 mark each**, uniform across every class:

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Content accuracy | 1 |
| Reasoning | 1 |
| Organisation | 1 |
| Correct word-class placement | 1 |
| **Total** | **4** |

Read proportionally: one correct, well-formed sentence earns full marks regardless of length. The criteria are the point, not a raw word count.

**Islamic/value alignment is not a scored criterion.** Charter §H binds all authored material in full — questions, prompts, model answers, worksheets, the Assignment, the Performance Test — and that is unchanged and non-negotiable. It does not extend to scoring student responses: a religiously neutral, on-topic answer earns full marks, value content is required only where the question itself asks for it, and a response that conflicts with §H is flagged and corrected **as a values matter, never marked down as a language or content error** (Run Book §13.1).

**I.4 Class-band scaling.** The **point total and criteria set are identical at every class** — 4 marks, the four criteria at I.3. What scales is the **standard applied within each criterion**, not its mark value:

- **Reasoning** — Class 1: any stated reason accepted, either language. Class 2: one sentence, either language or mixed, tied to the word-job identified. Classes 3–5: evaluated, not merely present, against the word's class, climbing with each class's Bloom band (name the class → find-the-error → dual-job discrimination).
- **Correct word-class placement** — Classes 1–2: the word is used in a position its class allows. Classes 3–5: placement is judged against the class the student named at step one.
- **Content accuracy** and **Organisation** — the same criteria throughout; expected depth is set by the question's own wording, never inferred at marking time (Run Book §13.2).

A criterion is scored at 1 mark or 0. Partial credit within a criterion is not used at this total.

**I.5 Block-type adaptation.** Within the fixed 4-mark total, a specific criterion may be substituted to fit what a block can genuinely assess — a punctuation block trading word-class placement for Accuracy; a composition block trading Reasoning for Sentence variety. **The 4-mark total is fixed and the count of criteria stays at four; Islamic/value alignment is not available as a substitute criterion** (I.3). Any substitution is stated in the block file.

## Section J — Binding Assessment Evidence

**J.1 What counts as binding.** A binding assessment source is a Half-Yearly or Annual exam paper issued by the school for a given class. Once issued, a paper is binding on that class's Drive Plan, Block-Build Specification, and grammar blocks — its question formats, structures tested, and mark splits constrain what is built.

**J.2 Combined-binding, not replacing.** Binding assessment sources accumulate. Where a class has more than one binding paper — a prior Half-Yearly, a current Half-Yearly, an Annual — all remain simultaneously binding. A new paper adds to the binding set; it does not retire or supersede an earlier one. A structure or format tested only in an older paper stays binding unless a Principal ruling explicitly retires it.

> **J.2 — sourcing note:** This permanence rule is not independently confirmed against surviving evidence. It is reconstructed as the operationally cautious default, consistent with this Charter's general pattern of requiring a Principal ruling to remove or narrow any standing obligation (cf. E.5, E.6). If evidence surfaces that older binding papers lapse, expire, or are superseded automatically — rather than requiring an explicit ruling to retire — J.2 should be revisited before being treated as settled precedent.

**J.3 Union, not replacement, for the exam-format map.** A Drive Plan's exam-format map (§7) is built as the union of every binding paper's requirements for that class. Where two binding papers test the same structure in different formats, both formats are covered — the map does not pick one paper's version over the other's.

**J.4 Binding does not mean sole source.** A binding paper constrains what must be covered; it does not by itself license teaching content the book doesn't supply. Where a binding paper tests a structure the book doesn't teach, that gap is routed through the map-then-decide exception (§E.6 for grammar, §F.4 for vocabulary) — it is not silently absorbed just because a paper happens to test it.

**J.5 Scope.** This Charter governs binding assessment sources project-wide only as evidentiary status — that a paper, once issued, is permanently binding and accumulates rather than replaces. It does not itself list which papers are currently binding for which class; that record is a Drive Plan matter, kept current as new papers are issued.

## Section K — Delivered-Content Lock

**K.1 Definition of delivered.** A block is delivered once its class has actually been taught it — not once it has been built, drafted, or version-bumped. Delivery status is tracked by class, block, and date; it is never assumed from a file's existence or version number.

**K.2 The lock.** Once delivered, a block's teaching script, worksheets, Performance Test, Assignment, and answer keys are frozen. No content or curriculum change is made to a delivered block — not for a new binding paper (§J), not for a Principal ruling on a related item, not for a correction discovered elsewhere. This holds regardless of how clearly the new evidence points to what the block "should" have said.

**K.3 What the lock does not reach.** The lock covers the delivered block's own content. It does not freeze File 2 (vocabulary may still be scheduled or added for future weeks), later blocks not yet delivered, or a Drive Plan's own documentation — a Drive Plan may still be version-bumped to state a corrected cap, format, or rubric going forward. Updating what a Drive Plan says a class *should* do next is not the same as re-delivering what was already taught.

**K.4 Absorption, not revision.** Where new evidence or a ruling would otherwise change a delivered block, the change is never applied to it directly. It is absorbed downstream instead — in a later block, a new strand inside a later block, or a File 2 schedule adjustment where the fix is vocabulary-only. The receiving block's re-walk step names the new content explicitly; prior exposure is never assumed of students just because the content conceptually belongs to an earlier block.

**K.5 Undelivered content is not locked.** A block that has been built or drafted but not yet taught may still be corrected directly, up to the point of delivery. This lock protects what students have already been taught — it is not a freeze on work in progress.

*Procedure for identifying delivery status, routing a downstream fix, and recording the redirect is set out in the Run Book (§10.7, §11); this Charter establishes only that the lock exists and what it protects.*

## Section M — Calendar & Handover

**M.1 Calendar lock as binding gate.** Each class's Drive start date and end/handover date are confirmed against the school's own calendar before that class's Week 1 begins. This confirmation is the binding gate — a class does not begin teaching with its calendar unconfirmed, and it is the point at which any conflict between a proposed schedule and the duration cap (§C.3) must be resolved before proceeding.

**M.2 Dates deliberately left open.** This Charter does not itself fix a start date, end date, or handover date for any class. These are left open by design, to be confirmed against the actual school calendar for that term rather than fixed in advance.

**M.3 Standing weekly rhythm.** The Drive runs on a fixed weekly cadence: four teaching sessions per week (Sunday–Wednesday), nightly homework with a cap of approximately 40 minutes, a **Thursday Performance Test conducted at school under teacher supervision**, and a **weekly Assignment** — given at school after the Performance Test, completed over Thursday–Saturday, and submitted Sunday. This cadence holds project-wide across all five classes unless a Principal ruling states otherwise for a specific class. *(Principal ruling, v1.2: this supersedes the prior take-home, parent-assisted Thursday-test model. See front-matter status table and Open-Items Register, Section C.)*

**M.4 Cross-class build order.** Class 3 is the validated pilot. Other classes follow it in build order — each subsequent class's Drive Plan, vocabulary pool, and grammar blocks are built using Class 3's validated structure as the working reference, adapted throughout to that class's own book, Teacher's Guide, and binding assessment evidence (§E.4, §J).

**M.5 Handover.** Where a build session ends before a class's artefacts are complete, the next session resumes from the current state of the project's files and the implementation record (§K.2) — not by re-deriving policy or procedure this Charter or the Run Book has already settled. Procedure for orienting a new session is set out in the Run Book (§7); this Charter establishes only that continuity, not re-derivation, is the standard.

## Section N — New Word Routine & Dual-Job Staging

**N.1 The routine.** Every new vocabulary word is introduced through a fixed sequence — **Meaning → Class → Use** — anchored to a standing clue card (naming → Noun; doing → Verb; describing a noun → Adjective; how/when/where → Adverb; extended with further rows as a class's spine reaches Pronoun, Preposition, and Conjunction).

**N.2 Rung band, fixed per class.** The depth of this routine follows a fixed, project-wide band. A class applies its rung; it does not set its own.

| Class | Rung | Dual-job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recognise | Not permitted |
| 2 | Recognise-and-Use | Not permitted — a word holds one job only |
| 3 | Apply | Introduced — staged, gentle |
| 4 | Apply / Analyze | Practised, not just introduced |
| 5 | Dual-job fluency + find-the-error | Full fluency, top rung |

**N.3 Dual-job bar below Class 3.** No word in a Class 1 or Class 2 pool carries a dual-job tag. Each pool word holds exactly one part-of-speech tag and is taught in exactly one grammar block. Where a student asks whether a word could serve another role, the routine is acknowledged, not opened — the distinction waits for the class where dual-job begins.

**N.4 Word-selection constraint.** Where dual-job is permitted (Class 3 and above), pairs are drawn primarily from concrete, value-positive noun↔verb words. A pairing outside noun↔verb (e.g. an adjective↔verb pair) is permitted only as a single, explicitly designated anchor case per class — never as general practice.

**N.5 Form constraint.** The verb form in any dual-job pair is kept in base or imperative form. This holds until the class's spine reaches its subject–verb agreement block, avoiding a collision between plural -s (noun) and verb -s (agreement) before that structure is taught.

**N.6 Introduction pacing.** The routine itself is introduced in a class's first grammar block, using single-class words; where a dual-job taste is shown at this stage, it is teacher-modelled only, not a taught item. Real student dual-job practice begins once the class reaches its Noun and Verb blocks, staged a few words at a time and tracked forward block to block — never introduced all at once.

**N.7 Top rung reserved.** Full dual-job fluency across three or more classes-of-word, and the find-the-error discrimination task, are reserved for Class 5's rung. No earlier class drills at this depth, regardless of how strong a cohort's performance is running.
