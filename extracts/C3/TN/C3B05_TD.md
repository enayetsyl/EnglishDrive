**SCHOOL FOR COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT**

**Class 3 — English Grammar Campaign**

**Teacher Delivery Sheet — Block 05: Noun — Gender**

**Runs:** Week 4 · 4 teaching days (Sun–Wed) + Performance Test (Thu) · 35 min/session

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the block, students can:

- Name the four genders — **masculine, feminine, common, neuter** — and say what each means.
- Find the noun(s) in a sentence and write the gender of each (the exam format).
- Give the gender of a single noun from held or block-taught words.
- Build their own sentence for a given gender and say **why** the noun has that gender.

**Teach only to the cap:** pick the noun, name its gender. **No edge-case theory** on the common-vs-neuter boundary — keep to "person who could be male or female = common; a thing = neuter."

## Words used this block

- **Masculine:** father · brother · man · uncle · boy · cock · king
- **Feminine:** mother · girl · hen · sister · aunt · woman · queen
- **Common:** friend · neighbour · teacher · student · child · cousin · baby
- **Neuter:** house · book · pen · bag · garden · roof · table · chair · mirror · egg · cake · wood · class

Say every new gender word aloud before writing it. Graded items use these words only — nothing is graded before its teaching day.

## The four terms — taught, not graded

Run a 3–5 minute **term-practice segment every day** (choral read, syllable-break, board-write, oral recall). Build familiarity by repetition. **No term appears as a graded item** on any worksheet or the PT — where a day shows partial-spelling board recall (e.g. *Mascu____*), that is in-class practice only.

Spelling emphasis: *common* is easy; give more board time to *neuter* (eu), *feminine* (two i's), *masculine* (-line).

## Day 1 (Sunday) — What gender is · Masculine & Feminine

**New words:** uncle · aunt · king · queen · boy · sister (with held father, mother, brother, girl, man, hen)
**Term strand:** introduce **masculine** + **feminine** (first exposure — no recall yet)

1. **Re-walk the noun (0–5).** Books closed. Write: father · house · hen · book · girl. "What do we call these? → Naming words. Nouns." Then: "Today we look at naming words for people and animals — some tell us **male** or **female**."
2. **The new idea (5–9).** Two columns, no headings: father/mother, brother/sister, boy/girl. "This side — male. This side — female. We call this the **gender** of the noun." Leave *gender* on the board.
3. **Masculine & feminine + pairs (9–18).** Head the columns **Masculine (male)** / **Feminine (female)**; fill father–mother, brother–sister, uncle–aunt, boy–girl, king–queen, cock–hen. Teach new words off held anchors ("*uncle* is your father's brother — masculine; *aunt* is your mother's sister — feminine; a *king* rules — masculine; a *queen* — feminine"). Class echoes each pair twice.
   *The pairs are the scaffold. The graded skill is naming a single noun's gender, not converting one word to the other — do not drill conversion.*
4. **Term practice (18–23).** Choral read ×3 masculine/feminine; syllable-break *mas·cu·line* / *fem·i·nine*; board-write with underlines. Post the Clue Card rows. First exposure — no recall yet.
5. **Sorting drill (23–28).** Books closed, class answers masculine/feminine: father · mother · king · girl · uncle · queen · brother · aunt · boy · hen · sister · man. Then reverse, one wrong ("*Aunt is masculine* — fix it").
6. **CW-1 (28–30).** *(CW-1 is on its own sheet.)* Model item 1 of **each part**. Warn: Part B asks for the **gender word**; Part C asks for the **partner word** — students often confuse them.
7. **Exit Check (30–35).** One student per prompt gives the gender: uncle · sister · mother · king · cock · aunt · boy · queen · brother · father · hen · girl · man · boy · sister · cock · queen. *(17 prompts, one per student.)*

## Day 2 (Monday) — Common gender

**New words:** teacher · student · child · cousin · baby (with held friend, neighbour)
**Term strand:** recall masculine/feminine; introduce **common**

1. **Recall Day 1 (0–4).** Books closed, oral masculine/feminine: uncle · aunt · king · queen · boy · girl · brother · sister. "What does gender tell us? → Whether it is male or female."
2. **Term recall (4–7).** Board: `Mascu______   Fem______` — students fill in, no peeking. Two come to the board; fix spelling openly.
3. **Discover common (7–16).** Write "The student is reading." "Is a student a boy or a girl? → Either. The word does not tell us." Introduce **common** — a noun that can be male or female. Build the set: friend · neighbour · teacher · student · child · cousin · baby.
   *Guardrail: keep to "can it be male or female?" Do not open animals/ships edge cases. If asked whether cat is common: "For our work, a person who could be a boy or girl is common; a thing is the next lesson." Hold the line.*
4. **Term practice (16–21).** Choral read ×3 *common*; syllable-break *com·mon* (double m); definition→term match on the two known terms; post Clue Card common row.
5. **Three-way sort (21–25).** masculine/feminine/common: father · friend · queen · teacher · boy · baby · aunt · student · uncle · child · hen · cousin · sister · neighbour. Reverse, one wrong.
6. **CW-2 (25–31).** *(CW-2 is on its own sheet.)* Model item 1.
7. **Exit Check (31–35).** brother · baby · mother · boy · king · student · cousin · hen · girl · father · child · uncle · aunt · neighbour · teacher · queen · sister.

## Day 3 (Tuesday) — Neuter gender

**New words:** table · chair · mirror · egg · cake · wood · class (with held house, book, pen, bag, garden, roof + more)
**Term strand:** recall masculine/feminine/common; introduce **neuter** — all four now in rotation

1. **Recall Days 1–2 (0–4).** masculine/feminine/common: uncle · friend · queen · teacher · boy · baby · aunt · cousin.
2. **Term recall (4–8).** Board: `Fem______   Comm____` — write, no peeking. Preview `Neu____` — "new today; watch the spelling: n-e-u-t-e-r."
3. **Discover neuter (8–17).** Write "The table is brown." "Is a table a boy? A girl? → Neither — a thing. Naming words for things have the gender **neuter**." Build: house · book · pen · bag · garden · roof · table · chair · mirror · egg · cake · wood · class. Contrast to fix the boundary: "*teacher* — a person, could be either → common; *chair* — a thing → neuter." (No theory beyond this.)
4. **Term practice — all four (17–22).** Choral read ×2 all four; syllable-break *neu·ter* (underline the *eu*); teacher gives the meaning, students write the term from memory; post all four Clue Card rows.
5. **Four-way sort (22–26).** father · table · friend · queen · book · teacher · egg · aunt · chair · cousin · king · mirror.
6. **Find-in-sentence + CW-3 (26–31).** Model the exam move on the board — find the noun, name its gender: "The boy has a bag." → boy (masc) / bag (neuter); "My sister reads a book." → sister (fem) / book (neuter); "The teacher helps us." → teacher (common); "The table is brown." → table (neuter). Tell the class: **some sentences have one naming word, some have two — find them all, then write each gender.** *(CW-3 is on its own sheet; HW-3 goes home.)*
7. **Exit Check (31–35).** brother · king · mirror · aunt · boy · mother · neighbour · sister · cock · child · wood · queen · student · egg · cousin · hen · book.

## Day 4 (Wednesday) — Mixed practice · Self-try demonstration

**Term strand:** all four from memory — full recall

1. **Recall all four (0–4).** uncle · table · friend · queen · egg · teacher · boy · baby · chair · aunt · cousin · mirror.
2. **Term recall from memory (4–9).** Teacher gives meaning, students write the term: male → masculine; female → feminine; male or female → common; a thing → neuter. Two students spell each at the board.
3. **Find-the-noun, write-the-gender (9–17).** On the board: "The hen sits on the egg." → hen (fem) / egg (neuter); "My friend gave me a mirror." → friend (common) / mirror (neuter); "The king and the queen smiled." → king (masc) / queen (fem); "The teacher wrote on the board." → teacher (common) / board (neuter). "First find the naming word, then ask the Clue Card question: male? female? either? or a thing?"
4. **Mixed sort + fix-the-error (17–22).** On the board: "Aunt is masculine." · "Table is common." · "Boy is feminine." — each is wrong; fix it and say why. → aunt is feminine · table is neuter · boy is masculine.
5. **Self-try demonstration (22–31). Not collected, not marked** — rehearsal for Thursday's graded Self-try. Model on the board:
   - **a)** a **common** noun: *teacher.*
   - **b)** gender is *common* — because a teacher can be a **man or a woman**.
   - **c)** sentence: *The teacher helps us.*
   - **d)** a **neuter** noun: *The table is brown.* — gender *neuter.*
   "Tomorrow you do this for marks. You'll be told the gender — you choose the word and write the sentence. Your reason must say **why**."
   *Reasoning is evaluated: "because a teacher can be a man or a woman" earns the reasoning mark; "because it is common" (restating the answer) does not.*
6. **CW-4 / HW-4 (31–33).** *(Both on their own sheets.)* Same instruction: some sentences have one noun, some two — find all, write each gender.
7. **Exit Check (33–35).** cake · egg · king · sister · neighbour · friend · book · chair · teacher · aunt · mirror · cousin · father · student · house · brother · queen.

## Performance Test — how to run it (Thursday)

*(The student test is on its own sheet, `C3B05-PT`. Full Marks 42 · 35 minutes.)*

- **Part A — Dictation (10 marks).** Say each word **twice**, then read the whole list once more. Words (all from the W4 held pool — no block-local gender words): **village · city · garden · story · breakfast · classroom · afternoon · neighbour · roof · week.**
- **Part B — Find the noun(s), write the gender (10 sentences × 2).** Mirrors the exam format. Some sentences have one noun, some two. These sentences do not appear on any worksheet.
- **Part C — Gender of each noun (8 × 1).**
- **Part D — Self-try (4 marks).** Student is given the **categories** (common, then neuter), chooses the words, and builds the sentences.

**Self-try rubric — 4 marks, 1 each:** content accuracy · reasoning · organisation · word-class placement. Reasoning must say **why** ("because a teacher can be a man or a woman"), not restate the answer. Value alignment is not scored.
