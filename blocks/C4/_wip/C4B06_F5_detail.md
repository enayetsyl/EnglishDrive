### 2.1 Why "just recast onto held words" is not available here

The at/on/in contrast **is** the concept: the preposition is chosen by the *type of the object* — clock time → *at*,
day/date → *on*, month/season/part-of-day → *in*. The noun is the discriminator, not decoration.

**File 2 holds, by W6, exactly two time nouns: `morning` (W1) and `day` (W1).** No held clock-time word, no held
day-of-week, no held month, no held season. Both held words fall in the *same* category (*in the morning*,
*in the day*), so the pool cannot produce even one contrasting pair.

> **Recasting Group 1 onto held vocabulary would not shrink the block. It would delete the time-preposition
> concept**, which the Drive Plan §2 cap requires ("**Place/time** prepositions the book uses") and the TG
> outcome names ("9.3.4 — use prepositions to indicate positions **and indicate time**").

### 2.2 The instrument gap — this is the real question

Four instruments exist for non-held words. **None of them fits this case**, and the reason is consistent:

| Instrument | Covers | Why it does not fit |
|---|---|---|
| **PD-009 / §5.7** exemplar | words whose only job is to demonstrate a rule; never vocabulary | *Sunday, June, summer* are ordinary lexical items a child should own. Classing them as exemplars would misfile real vocabulary. |
| **PD-011 / §5.9** override | *"common, age-appropriate, **exam-anchored** vocabulary"* | **Not exam-anchored.** See §2.3. |
| **PD-012 / §5.8** block-local | *"a class may teach **exam-required** items in-block"*; rationale is *"some **binding papers** test a concept that cannot be established from the held pool at all"* | Fits the *shape* exactly — concept-necessity, gradeable in-block, not pooled, never dictation. But its stated trigger is **exam requirement**, and there is none. |
| **PD-026** | function-word grammar targets (closed class) | Covers the **prepositions**; the time nouns are open-class content words. |

**The gap:** every instrument is anchored to the exam. C4B06's time nouns are required by a **book/TG-anchored
concept with no exam anchor**. The instrument set has no entry for that.

### 2.3 What the papers actually test (verified at source)

| Paper | Preposition evidence |
|---|---|
| HY 2025 | **Q6(f)** — *"I saw a bird **on** the tree"* → **place** |
| Annual 2025 | **Q4(f)** — *"The keys are **on** the desk"* → **place** |
| HY 2026 | no P.O.S. question; no preposition item at all |

**Both binding preposition items are place. No C4 paper tests a time preposition.** So the time strand is carried
entirely by the Drive Plan cap and the TG outcome — genuine curriculum content, zero exam weight. That asymmetry
is what makes this a real decision rather than a formality.

### 2.4 Options

**Option A — declare a block-local time-noun set under PD-012, extending its trigger from "exam-required" to
"required by the Drive Plan §2 cap."** *(recommended)*

> Declared set: `Sunday · Monday · Thursday · Saturday · June · July · summer · winter · spring · evening ·
> night · dawn` (12). *`morning` and `day` are already held and stay held.*

- Preserves the taught concept at full cap depth.
- Inherits every PD-012 protection: gradeable **only** inside C4B06; **never** dictation or spelling items;
  not held downstream; not in spaced revision.
- **Already compliant today** — the PT dictation list is the W6 held batch (`pollution · waste · smoke · noise ·
  air · ear · kindness · people · street · clothes`) and contains no time noun.
- Cost: a Decision-Log entry extending PD-012's scope (or a new PD), and Day 2's script must **teach** the twelve,
  which it substantially already does through the calendar/clock cue.

**Option B — reduce the time strand to recognition-only, grade place + relation only.**

- Buildable today: place runs on the block-local place set, relation on held kinship nouns.
- Removes ~26 graded items; CW-2 and HW-2 would need rebuilding around place/relation, and the PT loses 4 items.
- **Conflicts with the §2 cap**, which is not the agent's to narrow — this would itself be a Drive Plan change.
- Defensible only on the exam-weight argument in §2.3.

**Option C — add the twelve to File 2 as a proper pool addition.**

- Cleanest downstream (see §2.5), but inflates the W6 fresh batch from 18 to 30 and pulls the twelve into
  dictation, spelling and the retention gate — a real load increase in a week already carrying 18 new words.
- Also reopens File 2, which currently sits at a "PENDING re-map" label defect of its own.
- **Not recommended.**

### 2.5 Downstream consequence you should know before ruling

PD-012 words are **not available to later blocks as held vocabulary**. Time expressions are wanted again at
**Block 8 (Simple Past)** and **Block 9 (Continuous Tenses)** — *last Friday, in the morning, on Sunday* are the
natural carriers for tense work, and the Annual paper's own tense items use them.

Under Option A those blocks must **re-declare** the same words block-locally. PD-012 permits that, but it is
recurring friction and it means the same twelve words are taught as "new" three times. Option C avoids it at the
cost of the batch inflation above.

> If you would rather settle this once for the whole C4 track, the cleaner shape is a **standing C4 time-expression
> set** declared at Block 6 and carried forward to Blocks 8–9 by name. That is a **new PD**, not an extension.

## 4. Addendum surfaced while preparing this — W7 leakage is wider than `quickly`

A **carrier-level sweep** (every word in every graded item against File 2 release weeks — broader than the
held-word gate, which only checks declared triggers) found **four W7 words across 11 items**:

| Word | Released | Items |
|---|---|---|
| `quickly` | W7 | CW-3 A2 · HW-4 A16 — **graded answers** (this is **F3**) |
| **`together`** | **W7** | **CW-3 B21, B26 · HW-3 B21, B26 · PT C6** |
| `mosque` | W7 | HW-2 A8 · PT B7 |
| `dog` | W7 | PT C5 |

**`together` is the serious one.** It is not incidental carrier text — it is the **bracket clue** that tells the
pupil which preposition to write:

> *"I go to school ___ my friend. **(together)**"* → answer `with`

A W7 word is doing the disambiguating work in five W6 graded items, one of them on the Performance Test. A child
who cannot read *together* cannot answer the item, and the gate never saw it because clues are not triggers.

**Proposed:** replace the clue **(together)** with **(with another person)** — or, better, the Bangla gloss
**(সঙ্গে)** already used on the Preposition Clue Card, which is what a Sylhet Class 4 child will actually decode.
`mosque` and `dog` are ordinary carriers and are retired by rows 1–14 above plus a swap of HW-2 A8 / PT B7.

**This is a gate gap worth noting alongside T1/T2:** bracket clues and instruction text are student-facing and
answer-bearing, but no gate reads them.
