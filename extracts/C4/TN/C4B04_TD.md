# Class 1 (Sunday) — What an Article is, and *a / an* by sound

**Objective.** Students know what an article is and name the two types, then use *a* and *an* correctly by listening to the first **sound** of the word that follows, including the four core sound exceptions.

**Exemplars today:** `hour · honest · university · one-taka`

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–9 | What is an Article? |
| 2 | 9–14 | Ears before eyes |
| 3 | 14–19 | Name the rule |
| 4 | 19–29 | The ear beats the eye |
| 5 | 29–31 | Listening drill |
| 6 | 31–32 | CW-1 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — What is an Article? (0–9 min)

**(a) Recall Block 2 (0–2).** Write: `friend · water · Dhaka · tree · money`

> "From Block 2 — which of these can we count? Which is a name?"

Expected: *friend, tree* countable · *water, money* uncountable · *Dhaka* a name (proper noun).

> "Hold on to those three ideas — countable, uncountable, and names. You will need all three this week."

**(b) The idea (2–5).** Write:

```
        friend          a friend          the friend
```

> "Read all three. *Friend* alone is just the word. **A friend** — one, some friend, we do not know which. **The friend** — a special one, the one we already know about."

> "Those little words — **a, an, the** — are called **Articles**. An Article comes **before a noun** and tells us whether we mean **any one** or **the special one**."

> "Where does the Article sit — before the noun, or after it?" → **Before.**

**(c) The two types (5–7).** Build with the class:

| Type | Articles | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Indefinite** | **a, an** | any one — not a special one | I met **a** teacher. |
| **Definite** | **the** | the special one we mean | I met **the** teacher of our class. |

> "This week: today, **a** and **an**. Tomorrow, **the**. On Tuesday, something surprising — sometimes the correct answer is **no article at all**."

**(d) What you already know, and what is new (7–9).**

> "You learned articles in earlier classes. Tell me the rule you learned — when do we use **an**?"

Let them answer. Someone will produce *a, e, i, o, u*. **Write it on the board and leave it there through Step 4.**

> "Yes. **a, e, i, o, u** — the five vowel letters. That rule has served you well and it is right most of the time. Keep it."

> "This year we sharpen it. Articles do not really follow the **letter** we write. They follow the **sound** we say. Usually they agree — that is why your rule works so often. But sometimes they disagree, and then the sound wins."

Books closed.

> "Say these after me — the sound in **aunt**, in **envelope**, in **uncle**. Feel your mouth: open, air coming out freely. **Vowel sounds.**"
>
> "Now the start of **park**, **teacher**, **mountain**. Your lips shut, your tongue moves, your teeth meet. Something blocks the air. **Consonant sounds.**"

> "Today your job is to **hear** which one a word starts with. Later this morning I will show you four words where your eye and your ear disagree."

**Teacher note.** The vowel-letter rule is deliberately affirmed, not corrected — students have used it correctly for years and it holds in most cases. Position sound as the finer tool that explains *why* it works and what to do when it fails. Leave `a e i o u` visible through Step 4: the exceptions land harder when students can see the letter rule and hear it break at once.

### Step 2 — Ears before eyes (9–14 min)

**Books closed. Nothing on the board.**

> "I will say some words. Do not write. Just tell me the first **sound** you hear."

| Teacher says | Class answers |
|---|---|
| *friend* | "f" |
| *uncle* | "u" |
| *teacher* | "t" |
| *envelope* | "e" |
| *aunt* | "a" |
| *park* | "p" |

> "Now say the whole word with **a** or **an** in front."

Chorally: *a friend · an uncle · a teacher · an envelope · an aunt · a park*

> "You just used the rule before I taught it. Your ears already know it."

### Step 3 — Name the rule (14–19 min)

| **a** | **an** |
|---|---|
| a friend | an uncle |
| a teacher | an aunt |
| a mountain | an envelope |

> "Say each aloud. What is different about how the words after *an* begin?"

Let them reach it, then fix the wording:

> **We use *an* before a word that begins with a vowel sound. We use *a* before a consonant sound.**

Quick oral round, held nouns only:
`bird · envelope · letter · uncle · park · aunt · library · team · picture · ticket`

### Step 4 — The ear beats the eye (19–29 min)

The heart of the block. Slow down here.

> "Now I will try to trick you. Books closed — listen only."

**Say** *hour*. Most will answer *a hour*.

> "Say it with me. **Hour.** Do you hear an *h*? No. Your mouth says **our** — open, nothing blocking. A vowel sound. So — **an hour**."

Write it up; let them see the mismatch.

> "Look — we **write** an *h*. We do not **say** it. Your ear was right. Your eye was wrong."

**Say** *university*. Point at the letter list still on the board.

> "Look at our list — *u* is right there. So the letter says **an**. Now say the word aloud. **U-ni-ver-si-ty.** What does your ear say?" → **Yu.** "Consonant sound. So — **a university**."

Build the four core exceptions — class says each aloud twice:

| We write | We say | Article | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| hour | *our* | **an** | the *h* is silent → vowel sound |
| honest | *onest* | **an** | the *h* is silent → vowel sound |
| university | *yu-ni-ver-si-ty* | **a** | begins with a *yu* sound → consonant |
| one-taka note | *wan-taka* | **a** | begins with a *w* sound → consonant |

> "The rule is not about the **letter** you write. It is about the **sound** you hear. Your ears decide, not your eyes."

**Teacher note.** If a student objects that *university* and *one* "start with vowels," accept it and redirect: "You are right about the letter. Now say it aloud — what sound comes first?" The letter/sound split is the lesson, not an error to correct away.

**At the end of this step, erase the letter list.** It has done its job as the bridge; what stays visible is the sound rule.

### Step 5 — Listening drill (29–31 min)

Books closed. Teacher says the noun, students respond chorally, then individuals.

`hour · friend · university · envelope · honest man · teacher · one-taka note · park · aunt · library`

Then reverse, one deliberately wrong:

> "*a hour* — right or wrong? Fix it."

### Step 6 — CW-1 (31–32 min)

Distribute CW-1. Model item 1 on the board (§9.3). Students continue as far as time allows; unfinished CW goes home with HW-1.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

Teacher says the noun; one student per prompt gives article + reason.

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | park | a | consonant sound |
| 2 | uncle | an | vowel sound |
| 3 | hour | an | silent *h* |
| 4 | teacher | a | consonant sound |
| 5 | envelope | an | vowel sound |
| 6 | university | a | *yu* sound |
| 7 | library | a | consonant sound |
| 8 | aunt | an | vowel sound |
| 9 | one-taka note | a | *wan* sound |
| 10 | ticket | a | consonant sound |
| 11 | honest man | an | silent *h* |
| 12 | picture | a | consonant sound |

# Class 2 (Monday) — **the**

**Objective.** Students use **the** for a noun that is specific, already mentioned, or the only one of its kind, and choose correctly between *a/an* and *the*.

**Exemplars today:** `the earth · the moon · the sun` (cumulative 7)

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Day 1; Indefinite vs Definite |
| 2 | 4–11 | Discover **the** |
| 3 | 11–22 | a/an vs the — guided practice |
| 4 | 22–28 | Two-mention passage |
| 5 | 28–30 | Clue Card *the* rows |
| 6 | 30–32 | CW-2 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall Day 1 (0–4 min)

Books closed. Rapid oral round — students give *a* or *an*:

`hour · park · university · aunt · honest man · library · one-taka note · envelope`

> "What decides — the letter you write, or the sound you say?" → **The sound.**

Write and leave up:

```
a / an  =  Indefinite  =  any one
the     =  Definite    =  the special one
```

> "Yesterday we learned the Indefinite Articles. Today, the Definite one."

### Step 2 — Discover *the* (4–11 min)

Write and read aloud:

```
My uncle gave me a pen. The pen is green.
```

> "First time — **a** pen. Any pen. You do not know it yet."
> "Second time — **the** pen. Now you know exactly which one, because I just told you."
> "**a** the first time. **the** after that."

Then the three uses:

**(i) Already mentioned.** The example above is the model — no second example needed.

**(ii) Specific — we say which one.** Write:

```
a school          the school near our house
a letter          the letter my uncle sent
```

> "The extra words tell us **which** one. Once we say which, we use **the**."

**(iii) Only one of its kind.** Write:

```
the earth        the moon        the sun
```

> "How many earths do we live on? One. How many moons are in our sky? One."
> "When there is only one, we always say **the**."

**Teacher note.** *the earth*, *the moon* and *the sun* are exemplars — teach them explicitly here; graded from CW-2 onward. Not vocabulary, never in dictation.

### Step 3 — a/an vs the (11–22 min)

> "So before any noun, ask: **do we know which one?** No → *a* or *an*. Yes → *the*."

Guided practice. Read each aloud; class answers; ask "why?" every time.

| Item | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Our teacher gave us ___ picture. | a | any one, first mention |
| ___ picture was very beautiful. | The | already mentioned |
| ___ moon shines at night. | The | only one |
| My father is ___ doctor. | a | any one of many |
| ___ water in the glass is clean. | The | which water — the one in the glass |
| I want to visit ___ library in our school. | the | we say which one |
| We saw ___ bird near the tree. | a | first mention |
| ___ bird was small and green. | The | already mentioned |

> "Notice item five. *Water* — can you count it? No. But we still used **the**, because we said **which** water. Remember that for tomorrow."

**Teacher note.** That item is deliberate groundwork for Day 3's zero article. Do not resolve it further today — just plant it.

### Step 4 — Two-mention passage (22–28 min)

Write on the board and read aloud, pausing at each blank:

```
Yesterday my uncle sent ___ letter. ___ letter came in ___ envelope.
___ envelope was green. My aunt put it on ___ shop counter near the window.
```

Answers: **a · The · an · The · the**

> "Say why for each one." — first mention · already mentioned · first mention + vowel sound · already mentioned · which counter.

> "Do you see the pattern? **a** the first time, **the** after that."

### Step 5 — Clue Card (28–30 min)

Add the *the* rows with the class:

> **the** — the special one
> · already mentioned · we say which one · only one in the world (**the earth, the moon, the sun**)

### Step 6 — CW-2 (30–32 min)

Distribute CW-2. Model item 1 on the board (§9.3). Unfinished CW goes home with HW-2.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ___ moon | the | only one |
| 2 | ___ friend of my brother | a | any one of many |
| 3 | ___ earth | the | only one |
| 4 | ___ letter my uncle sent | the | we say which one |
| 5 | ___ holiday | a | any one |
| 6 | ___ library in our school | the | we say which one |
| 7 | ___ envelope | an | vowel sound |
| 8 | ___ shop near our house | the | we say which one |
| 9 | ___ hour | an | silent *h* |
| 10 | ___ mountain | a | any one |
| 11 | ___ water in the glass | the | we say which one |
| 12 | ___ picture on the wall | the | we say which one |

Day 1's rule stays live (items 7, 9) so the check is cumulative (Run Book §9.4 r.2).

# Class 3 (Tuesday) — **Zero article (x)**

**Objective.** Students use **x** (no article) before proper nouns and uncountable nouns, and sort correctly between *a/an*, *the*, and *x*.

**Exemplars today:** `Dhaka · Sylhet · Bangladesh · Cox's Bazar` (cumulative 11)

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Days 1–2 |
| 2 | 4–12 | **x** before proper nouns |
| 3 | 12–20 | **x** before uncountables + the *the* qualification |
| 4 | 20–28 | Three-way sort |
| 5 | 28–30 | Clue Card x rows |
| 6 | 30–32 | CW-3 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall Days 1–2 (0–4 min)

Books closed. Students give *a*, *an*, or *the*:

`___ hour · ___ moon · ___ university · ___ letter my uncle sent · ___ envelope · ___ earth · ___ one-taka note · ___ shop near our house`

> "Two questions decide it. Do we know **which one**? Then *the*. Any one of many? Then *a* or *an* — and what decides between those?" → **The sound.**

> "Today, a third answer. Sometimes the correct answer is **no article at all**."

### Step 2 — x before proper nouns (4–12 min)

Write, and read the wrong sentence aloud with full seriousness:

```
I live in the Sylhet.
```

> "Does that sound right?"

They will say no immediately.

> "Why not? What is wrong with it?"

Let them struggle briefly — they can hear it but not name it. Then:

> "*Sylhet* is a **name**. From Block 2 — what do we call a naming word?" → **Proper noun.**

> "A name already tells us exactly which one. There is only one Sylhet. So the article has no work left to do. We write **x** — nothing."

```
I live in x Sylhet.        x Dhaka        x Bangladesh        x Cox's Bazar
```

> "Say each with me. No article before a name."

Now the contrast that makes it stick:

```
I live in x Sylhet.
I live in a city.
```

> "*City* is not a name — any city, so it takes **a**. *Sylhet* is the name of one particular place, so it takes **x**. Same sentence. Different noun. Different answer."

**Teacher note.** The four place names are exemplars — teach explicitly; graded from CW-3 onward. Not vocabulary, never in dictation. If a student asks about *the Bangladesh*, confirm it is wrong and re-anchor: a name needs no article.

### Step 3 — x before uncountables (12–20 min)

> "Names are the first place we use x. Here is the second. From Block 2 — which of these can we count?"

```
letter    water    ticket    money    picture    food
```

Sort with the class: countable — *letter, ticket, picture* · uncountable — *water, money, food*.

> "Now try to say *a water*. Say it aloud."

Let them hear it.

> "*A* means **one**. One water? One money? You cannot count them, so you cannot say *one*. So we use **x**."

```
x water        x money        x food        x health        x peace        x news
```

Now the harder half:

> "But listen. Yesterday we said *the water in the glass*. Was that wrong?"

Let them think.

> "No — because we said **which** water. Once we say which one, we can use **the**, even for something we cannot count."

```
x water is good for health.
The water in the glass is clean.

x food is a blessing from Allah.
The food in the shop is fresh.
```

> "Look at both pairs. Same noun twice. First time — in general, all of it: **x**. Second time — we say **which** one: **the**."

> "So the question is never 'can I count it?' by itself. It is: **have we said which one?**"

**Teacher note.** The most demanding point of the block. Students over-apply x to every uncountable noun and then mark *the water in the glass* wrong. Correct by asking: "Which water? Did we say?" If yes → *the*. If it means water in general → *x*. Two pairs are given rather than one, because a single example reads as a quirk of the word *water*.

### Step 4 — The three-way sort (20–28 min)

> "Three questions, in order.
> **1. Is it a name?** → **x**
> **2. Can we count it? No, and we have not said which** → **x**
> **3. Do we know which one?** Yes → **the** · No → **a** or **an**, by sound."

Guided practice. Read each aloud; ask "why?" every time.

| Item | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| My uncle lives in ___ Dhaka. | x | name |
| We drank ___ water after the match. | x | uncountable, general |
| ___ water in the glass is clean. | The | we said which one |
| I bought ___ ticket for the trip. | a | any one, consonant sound |
| ___ ticket was very cheap. | The | already mentioned |
| My father gave me ___ one-taka note. | a | *wan* sound |
| We visited ___ Cox's Bazar last year. | x | name |
| It took ___ hour to reach the park. | an | silent *h* |
| ___ moon was bright last night. | The | only one |
| She needs ___ money for the trip. | x | uncountable, general |

**Teacher note.** Items 2–3 and 4–5 are deliberate pairs — the same noun taking different articles depending on context. Read each pair together and ask what changed. This is the Analyze step of the day.

### Step 5 — Clue Card (28–30 min)

Add the x rows with the class:

> **x** — no article
> · names (**Dhaka, Sylhet, Bangladesh, Cox's Bazar**) · things we cannot count, in general (**water, food, money, health, peace, news**)
> · *but:* if we say **which one**, use **the** — *the water in the glass*

### Step 6 — CW-3 (30–32 min)

Distribute CW-3. Model item 1 (§9.3). Unfinished CW goes home with HW-3.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ___ Dhaka | x | name |
| 2 | ___ hour | an | silent *h* |
| 3 | ___ money | x | uncountable, general |
| 4 | ___ moon | the | only one |
| 5 | ___ Bangladesh | x | name |
| 6 | ___ library in our school | the | we said which one |
| 7 | ___ food in the shop | the | we said which one |
| 8 | ___ university | a | *yu* sound |
| 9 | ___ Cox's Bazar | x | name |
| 10 | ___ picture | a | consonant sound |
| 11 | ___ health | x | uncountable, general |
| 12 | ___ envelope | an | vowel sound |

Answer spread: x ×4 · the ×3 · a ×3 · an ×2. No run beyond 2.

# Class 4 (Wednesday) — Mixed practice, error-find, Self-try demonstration

**Objective.** Students choose correctly among *a / an / the / x* in mixed exam format, find and fix article errors, and rehearse the Self-try format they will be graded on.

**Exemplars today:** `M.P. · eagle · excellent · historic` (cumulative 13 — full set)

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 | Recall the three-way path |
| 2 | 3–13 | Final four exemplars |
| 3 | 13–14 | CW-4 distribution, one item modelled |
| 4 | 14–21 | CW-4 Part A — error-find |
| 5 | 21–27 | CW-4 Part B — mixed exam format |
| 6 | 27–33 | Self-try demonstration |
| 7 | 33–35 | HW-4 distribution + Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–3 min)

Clue Card visible.

`___ Sylhet · ___ hour · ___ moon · ___ water in the glass · ___ university · ___ money`

> "Three questions. Name? → **x**. Cannot count it, and we have not said which? → **x**. Do we know which one? → **the**. Any one of many? → **a** or **an**, by sound."

### Step 2 — The last four exceptions (3–13 min)

> "You know the rule now. Today, four more words where the eye and the ear disagree."

Each with the Day 1 method — **say it first, write it after.**

**Say** *M.P.*

> "Listen. **Em-pee.** What sound comes out first? **Em** — a vowel sound. So — **an M.P.**"

Write it up.

> "Look at the letter. *M* is not a vowel letter. But we do not say *muh* — we say the letter's name, **em**. Your ear decides."

> "M.P. means Member of Parliament — someone chosen to speak for the people."

**Say** *eagle*, then *excellent*.

> "**Ea-gle.** **Ex-cellent.** Both open sounds — vowel. **an eagle**, **an excellent student**. Here the eye and the ear agree, so these are easy."

**Say** *historic* — the counterweight.

> "**His-toric.** Do you hear the *h*?" → **Yes.**

> "In this class we pronounce the *h* in *historic*. You hear it — so it is a consonant sound — so it takes **a**. **A historic day.**"

Contrast set, read aloud twice by the class:

```
an hour        an honest man        a historic day
```

> "*Hour* and *honest* hide their *h*. *Historic* does not. Same letter, different sound, different article."

**Teacher note.** Expect over-application: students who learned *an hour* will write *an historic*. Correct by ear every time — "Do you hear the *h*? Then use *a*." This is the single most common error in the block.

### Step 3 — CW-4 out (13–14 min)

Distribute CW-4. Model Part A item 1 on the board.

### Step 4 — CW-4 Part A, error-find (14–21 min)

Students work on the sheet. Teacher circulates and corrects at the desk. Take answers aloud at the end and ask "why?" for each.

### Step 5 — CW-4 Part B, mixed exam format (21–27 min)

> "This is exactly how the question comes in your exam. *Fill in the blanks with the correct article — use x if no article is needed. Write only answers.*"

Students work on the sheet; teacher circulates. Take answers aloud with reasons.

### Step 6 — Self-try demonstration (27–33 min)

**Not collected. Not marked.** This is the rehearsal for Thursday's graded Self-try.

**Step 1 — teacher models one.** Use **university**, so every student sees the exemplar they most often get wrong:

> Teacher's word: **university** · Article: **a** · Why: *begins with a yu sound*
> Sentence: *My brother studies at a university in Dhaka.*

Write all three lines on the board as you say them.

**Step 2 — students try one.** Each picks a word from the CW-4 box and fills all three lines. Three or four read aloud; give quick feedback on the reason line, not just the sentence.

> "On Thursday you will do this for marks, with different words. Today is practice."

### Step 7 — HW-4 + Exit Check (33–35 min)

Distribute HW-4 (20 seconds — the task line is on the sheet), then run the Exit Check briskly. Reasons should be two or three words, not sentences.

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ___ M.P. | an | *em* sound |
| 2 | ___ Sylhet | x | name |
| 3 | ___ historic day | a | we hear the *h* |
| 4 | ___ earth | the | only one |
| 5 | ___ eagle | an | vowel sound |
| 6 | ___ money | x | uncountable, general |
| 7 | ___ university | a | *yu* sound |
| 8 | ___ water in the glass | the | we said which one |
| 9 | ___ excellent student | an | vowel sound |
| 10 | ___ Cox's Bazar | x | name |
| 11 | ___ hour | an | silent *h* |
| 12 | ___ picture on the wall | the | we said which one |

Spread: an ×4 · x ×3 · the ×3 · a ×2. No run beyond 2.

**If Step 7 runs short**, trim Step 2 by a minute — the *eagle*/*excellent* pair is the easy half of the four exemplars (eye and ear agree) and can move faster than *M.P.* and *historic*.

# Self-construction rubric (PT Part E)

**4 marks** — Charter §I.3 (v1.3), four criteria at 1 mark each, uniform across all five classes.

| Criterion | Marks | What earns it |
|---|---|---|
| Content accuracy | 1 | article correct for the chosen word |
| Reasoning | 1 | line (b) names the rule — sound, name, uncountable, unique, mentioned |
| Organisation | 1 | complete sentence, correct order, readable |
| Correct word-class placement | 1 | article sits before the noun it belongs to |
| **Total** | **4** | |

Each criterion scores **1 or 0** — no partial credit at this total. Line **(b)** carries 1 of 4: a blank reasoning line caps the student at 3.

**Islamic/value alignment is not scored** (Charter §I.3, Run Book §13.1). Charter §H binds every item, prompt and passage in this block in full — but a student's own sentence is marked on the four criteria above. A religiously neutral, on-topic sentence earns full marks. A sentence that conflicts with §H is flagged and corrected as a values matter, never marked down as a language error.
