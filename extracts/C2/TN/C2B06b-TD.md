# W6 · Sunday — **have / has**: one owner vs many owners

**Day 1 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout · Clue Card issued today**
**Verbs graded-eligible from today (for later match work):** come, get, put, give, help
**New skill today:** using **have / has** to tell what someone owns or holds — **has** for one owner, **have** for many.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Fast reactivation: the one-vs-many rule they already know from doing words |
| 2 | 4–8 | The new words — *have* and *has* |
| 3 | 8–16 | Discovery: **one owner → has**; **many owners → have** (rich examples) |
| 4 | 16–22 | Rule box + guided "one or many?" drill |
| 5 | 22–30 | Guided practice — CW1 out |
| 6 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Fast reactivation (0–4 min)
*They met the one-vs-many rule last week with doing words. Reactivate it, don't re-teach.*
"Assalamu alaikum. Last week you learned a rule for doing words: **one** doer adds **s** — *he read**s*** — and **many** doers keep it plain — *they read*. Quick — is it *she look**s*** or *she look*?" *(Take 3–4 fast.)* "You know the one-vs-many rule well. Today we meet **two new little words** that follow the **very same rule**."

### Step 2 — The new words (4–8 min)
Write two sentences on the board, side by side, and read them:
> Maryam **has** a book.    Maryam and Fatima **have** two books.

"Same idea — *owning a book* — but two different words. One owner, Maryam, uses **has**. Two owners use **have**. Today we learn **when** to use **has** and when to use **have**." *(Flag this as the day's new learning.)*

### Step 3 — Discovery: one owner vs many owners (8–16 min)
Act it out with real students so the rule is felt. Build two columns headed **One owner → has** and **Many owners → have**.

*Tie it straight back to last week so it feels like the same rule, not a new one:*
> "Remember the one-vs-many rule? One doer takes **s** — *he read**s***. It is the **same** here: one owner takes **has** — *he **has***. Many keep the plain word: *they read → they **have***."

Call one student holding a book:
"Aisha is **one** owner. Aisha **has** a book." *(write under One owner → has)*
Now two students each holding a book:
"These are **two** owners. They **have** books." *(write under Many owners → have)*

Repeat with each pair, always one owner then a group:
| One owner → **has** | Many owners → **have** |
|---|---|
| Maryam **has** a book. | Maryam and Fatima **have** books. |
| Yusuf **has** a jar. | Yusuf and Abdullah **have** jars. |
| The hen **has** an egg. | The hens **have** eggs. |
| Aisha **has** a seed. | Aisha and Raima **have** seeds. |
| The tree **has** a leaf. | The trees **have** leaves. |

Then plant the pronoun cases explicitly (these trip children up):
"With **I**, **we**, **you**, **they** — always **have**. *I have. We have. You have. They have.*
With **he**, **she**, **it**, or **one naming word** — use **has**. *He has. She has. The boy has.*"

*(Optional Bangla support: একজন মালিক হলে → **has**; অনেক মালিক হলে, বা I / we / you / they → **have**। ঠিক কাজ-শব্দের এক-vs-অনেক নিয়মের মতোই। Keep main delivery in English.)*

### Step 4 — Rule box + guided drill (16–22 min)
Write the rule box, leave it up all week:
> **One owner → has.  Many owners (and I/we/you/they) → have.**

Rapid oral "one or many?" drill — teacher says a subject, class calls *has* or *have*:
> Maryam → *has* · the boys → *have* · I → *have* · my father → *has* · we → *have* · the hen → *has* · Aisha and Raima → *have* · the tree → *has*

### Step 5 — Guided practice (22–30 min)
Hand out **CW1**. Do the first two items together on the board (one owner, then many), then let them work. Circulate; watch for the *has/have* swap and the plural-s slip.

### Step 6 — Exit Check · `C2B06b-EC-Sun` (30–35 min)
One oral prompt per student (roll 14): teacher names the subject, student says the full sentence with *has* or *have*. **14 prompts ready** (expected answer in brackets):

1. Maryam … *(has)* · 2. The boys … *(have)* · 3. My father … *(has)* · 4. Aisha and Raima … *(have)* · 5. The hen … *(has)* · 6. We … *(have)* · 7. Yusuf … *(has)* · 8. The children … *(have)* · 9. My sister … *(has)* · 10. They … *(have)* · 11. Nusair … *(has)* · 12. The trees … *(have)* · 13. Fatima … *(has)* · 14. The girls … *(have)*

*(Spare, if a student needs a re-try: The bird … (has) · The cows … (have).)*
Target today: **has/have choice only.** Record pass/juggle for tomorrow's grouping.

# W6 · Monday — **have / has** extended: harder subjects + first match rows

**Day 2 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout**
**Verbs graded-eligible from today:** + show, point, use, know, brush *(with Sunday's five)*
**New skill today:** using **have/has** with **harder subjects** — "X and Y", *the children*, *the boys* — and a first, gentle taste of **matching** to make a sentence.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Exit-Check recall; re-show the rule box |
| 2 | 4–12 | Harder subjects: compound "and", group naming words |
| 3 | 12–18 | First match taste — join a subject to *has/have + object* |
| 4 | 18–26 | Guided practice — CW2 out |
| 5 | 26–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)
"Assalamu alaikum. One owner takes …? *has.* Many owners take …? *have.* Good." Re-show the rule box. Fix any pattern the Exit Check flagged.

### Step 2 — Harder subjects (4–12 min)
*Yesterday's subjects were single. Today stretch to compound and group subjects — still just one-vs-many.*
"Some subjects **look** like one but mean **many**. Listen: *Yusuf and Abdullah* — that is **two** people, so → **have**. *The children* — that is **many**, so → **have**. *The family* — that is **one** group, so → **has**."

Board contrast:
| Sounds like… | Really… | Word |
|---|---|---|
| Yusuf **and** Abdullah | two owners | **have** |
| The children | many | **have** |
| The family | one group | **has** |
| My mother **and** father | two owners | **have** |
| The boys | many | **have** |
| The cow | one | **has** |

Drill: teacher says the subject, class calls *has/have*. Watch the "and" cases — the commonest slip.

### Step 3 — First match taste (12–18 min)
*Introduce matching gently — just join a subject to the right have/has phrase.* Put three subjects on the left, three have/has phrases on the right (scrambled):
> Maryam —— · The children —— · The hen ——
> ( has an egg ) ( has a book ) ( have their books )
"Read each subject with each phrase. Which one **makes sense** and has the **right word**? *The children* is many → needs **have** → *The children have their books.* *Maryam* is one → **has a book.** *The hen* is one → **has an egg.**" Do all three aloud together. This is the seed for tomorrow's full match task.

*New word today — **field*** *(মাঠ): an open piece of grassy ground. Say it, write it on the board, and use it in a sentence — "The boys play in the **field**." "The cows eat grass in the **field**." (Children meet* field *this week as an outdoor place, alongside* pond, tree, forest, grass*.)*

### Step 4 — Guided practice (18–26 min)
Hand out **CW2**. First item together, then independent. Circulate.

### Step 5 — Exit Check · `C2B06b-EC-Mon` (26–35 min)
One oral prompt per student: teacher gives a **harder** subject, student supplies *has/have* + a sensible held object. **14 prompts ready** (expected word in brackets):

1. Yusuf and Abdullah … *(have)* · 2. The family … *(has)* · 3. My mother and father … *(have)* · 4. The children … *(have)* · 5. Porshi and Jesmin … *(have)* · 6. The cow … *(has)* · 7. Aisha and Maryam … *(have)* · 8. The boys … *(have)* · 9. My brother and sister … *(have)* · 10. The hens … *(have)* · 11. The girl … *(has)* · 12. Raima and Fatima … *(have)* · 13. The bird … *(has)* · 14. Nusair and Yusuf … *(have)*

Target: **has/have with harder subjects.** Record for Tuesday.

# W6 · Tuesday — **Match to make a sentence**: subject + doing/joining part

**Day 3 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout**
**Verbs graded-eligible from today:** + go, grow, wait, love, ride *(with Sun–Mon's ten)*
**New skill today:** **matching a naming part to a doing/joining part** so the whole line is a sensible sentence — the AN25 Q11 format.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | have/has recall (kept warm) |
| 2 | 4–12 | What "match to make a sentence" means — read the whole line, does it make sense? |
| 3 | 12–18 | The two checks: **sense** and (if have/has) the **right word** |
| 4 | 18–26 | Guided practice — CW3 out |
| 5 | 26–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — have/has recall (0–4 min)
Quick oral round: "My mother …? *has.* The boys …? *have.*" Keep it live — it returns in the match task and the PT.

### Step 2 — What matching means (4–12 min)
"When we **match to make a sentence**, we join a **naming part** (who/what) to a **doing part** (what they do) so the whole line **makes sense**." Board, two columns, scrambled on the right:

> **Naming part**  |  **Doing part**
> Maryam           |  live in the tree
> The birds        |  reads a book
> The cow          |  eats grass

"Read Maryam with each doing part. *Maryam live in the tree?* No sense. *Maryam reads a book?* Yes! *Maryam eats grass?* No. So Maryam → **reads a book**." Work all three together aloud, drawing the join line. Stress: **read the whole sentence and ask — does it make sense?**

### Step 3 — The two checks (12–18 min)
"Two things make a match right:
1. **Sense** — the whole line means something real. *(The frog sits on a stone — yes. The frog reads a book — no.)*
2. **Right word** — if it uses **has/have**, one owner → *has*, many → *have*."
Show one of each on the board and check them aloud together:
> The children —— have their books. *(many → have ✓, and it makes sense ✓)*
> My father —— goes to the village. *(makes sense ✓)*

*(Values note for the teacher: keep animals in outdoor frames — pond, tree, forest, field, grass — never indoors or in school scenes; §H.8. Read every built sentence for sense before accepting it.)*

### Step 4 — Guided practice (18–26 min)
Hand out **CW3**. Do the first match row together — read across, test for sense, draw the join — then independent. Circulate; the common slip is grabbing the first plausible word without reading the whole line.

### Step 5 — Exit Check · `C2B06b-EC-Tue` (26–35 min)
One oral prompt per student: teacher says the subject, student completes it into a **sensible** sentence using a held doing word (many right answers — accept any that makes sense). **14 prompts ready:**

1. The birds … · 2. Maryam … · 3. The cow … · 4. My father … · 5. Yusuf … · 6. The frog … · 7. Fatima … · 8. The children … · 9. The sun … · 10. Nusair … · 11. My mother … · 12. The boys … · 13. Aisha … · 14. The insect …

*(Sample good answers: The birds live in the tree · The cow eats grass · Yusuf brushes his teeth · The sun comes in the morning.)*
Target: **build one sensible sentence.** Record for Wednesday.

# W6 · Wednesday — **Integration**: have/has + match together, then **Self-try**

**Day 4 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout**
**Verbs graded-eligible from today:** + eat, drink, fly, throw, sit *(all 20 W6 verbs now in play)*
**New skill today:** using both skills side by side, then **building your own sentence** (small Create — the self-construction task).

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Mixed recall: has/have + match |
| 2 | 5–12 | Both skills side by side |
| 3 | 12–18 | Model the **self-try**: build your own sentence |
| 4 | 18–26 | Guided practice — CW4 out (ends with self-try) |
| 5 | 26–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Mixed recall (0–5 min)
Alternate quickly: "The hen …? *has.* … Now match: *The cow* —— ? *eats grass.*" Keep both skills warm for the combined PT tomorrow.

### Step 2 — Both skills side by side (5–12 min)
Two short board tasks back to back:
> **A. has or have?** The trees ___ leaves. → *have.*  My sister ___ a jar. → *has.*
> **B. Match:** Nusair —— (counts the stones / live in the pond) → *counts the stones.*
"Same two jobs you learned this week. Tomorrow's test uses **both**, plus your doing words and am/is/are from last week."

### Step 3 — Model the self-try (12–18 min)
*This is the block's self-construction free-thinking task (Charter §I, PD-010).* Model it aloud, thinking on your feet:
"I will build my **own** sentence. I choose a naming part — *my mother*. One owner → **has**. I choose a held object — *a plant*. So: **My mother has a plant.** Does it make sense? Yes. Is the word right? One owner → has → yes."
Then a match one: "I choose *the birds* — many. They … *live in the tree.* **The birds live in the tree.** Sense? Yes."
Give the four things the teacher will look for (say them simply): *right idea, makes sense, neat and in order, right word in the right place.*

### Step 4 — Guided practice (18–26 min)
Hand out **CW4**. It ends with the **self-try**: *Build one sentence of your own — either a have/has sentence or a match sentence — using words you know.* Give quiet thinking time; do not over-scaffold.

### Step 5 — Exit Check · `C2B06b-EC-Wed` (26–35 min)
One oral prompt per student: student says **one sentence of their own** (has/have or a matched sentence), correct and sensible. **14 starter prompts ready** (each opens a free build — accept any correct, sensible answer):

1. Tell me what your father has. · 2. Make a sentence about the birds. · 3. What does your sister have? · 4. Make a sentence with "the children". · 5. Tell me what the cow does. · 6. What does Maryam have? · 7. Make a sentence about the trees. · 8. Tell me what Yusuf does in the morning. · 9. What do the boys have? · 10. Make a sentence about the frog. · 11. What does your mother have? · 12. Tell me what the sun does. · 13. Make a sentence with "we". · 14. What does the hen have?

Target: **independent build.** This previews the PT self-try. Record readiness.
