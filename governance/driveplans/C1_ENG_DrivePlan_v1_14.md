## Summary (read this first — 60 seconds)

Class 1 is the drive's most unusual class. The NCTB Class 1 book is **pre-grammar**: it teaches alphabet decoding, numbers 1–10, greetings, classroom commands, simple Q&A, and rhymes — no grammar is named anywhere. The Mohammadpur exam, however, tests a near-complete Class 2–3 grammar spine. This plan navigates that gap with a **two-track structure**:

**Track A — Spoken/Book Core** (runs every class, every week, all year): alphabet, numbers, greetings, commands, Q&A patterns, rhymes. This is what the NCTB book actually teaches. It is maintained as a 5–10 minute oral routine at the start of each session — never replaced by the grammar track.

**Track B — Grammar Track** (this plan's main content): a cumulative, exam-targeted grammar spine taught carefully from the bottom, using the principal's four-bucket decisions (CONSOLIDATE / KEEP SELECTIVELY / POSTPONE / REMOVE). Because most core topics are **already taught** at ~70–80% grasp this year, the drive's job is to **close the remaining 20–30% gap**, not to re-teach from scratch.

**Ten grammar blocks, 10 weeks** (W8 shared: B8 Punctuation + B9 Sentence rearrangement + Conjunction Bridge; W9–W10 = Composition). Already-taught topics (noun identification, article, preposition/place sentence) have no standalone block — diagnostic check in W1, exam-format practice in the Performance Test from W1 onward. The heaviest exam marks (doing words/verbs [10], vocabulary/meaning [10+10]) arrive early. Every block ends with a free-thinking / self-try task on the criteria rubric. Bloom's stays **Remember/Understand-heavy** throughout, with simple Apply at the upper end. **No dual-job words** at Class 1. All content is present-tense only; past-tense audit runs before every block is finalised.

**File 2 (`C1_ENG_VocabPool_v1.xlsx`) is complete.** The vocabulary pool has 70 content words (Noun 46 · Adjective 11 · Verb 13) + 12 months special batch, sourced from the NCTB Class 1 book (2025 edition, OCR-extracted). Fresh batches run W1–W7; pool clears end of W7. The drive launches in **Phase 2 (concurrent)** from W1.

The load-bearing sections for building File 3 are **§2 (depth caps and bucket tags)** and **§4 (the block-by-week schedule)**.

## §1 — What this plan is

### 1a. The conflict and the resolution

The NCTB Class 1 book contains **zero named grammar**. The school's own exam papers test a near-full grammar spine (nouns, articles, plurals, gender, verbs, pronouns, adjectives, demonstratives, prepositions, punctuation, composition, months). This is a **systematic book/exam conflict**, resolved topic-by-topic using the charter's framework:

- **KEEP** (CONSOLIDATE): topics already taught this year at ~70–80% grasp, grounded in the book's sentence patterns (naming sentence, description sentence, place sentence), and heavily tested in the exam.
- **KEEP SELECTIVELY**: topics not in the book explicitly but meeting the charter's "genuine gap / prerequisite / manageable" test; taught at recognition depth only.
- **POSTPONE**: topics deferred to Class 2 (only: Conjunction *and/but*).
- **REMOVE**: topics on the exam but excluded because they exceed manageable scope, have no book/prerequisite anchor, or lack exam-weight justification.

All KEEP-SELECTIVELY and REMOVE decisions are **principal-locked**. No scope adjustment mid-build without a new ruling.

### 1b. Two-track structure

| Track | What | When | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Spoken/Book Core** | Alphabet (aA–zZ), numbers 1–10, greetings, classroom commands (stand up / sit down / open / look), Q&A (What's your name? / Where do you live? / What's this?), rhymes | Every session, every week | 5–10 min oral routine at session start; teacher-led, oral-only; no worksheet |
| **B — Grammar Track** | **10** cumulative grammar blocks (see §2) | **10 weeks** — one block per week, except W8 (B8 + B9 + Conjunction Bridge shared) and W9–W10 (B10 Composition) | Worksheets + Performance Test; Bangla-medium delivery; grammar terms in English |

Track A is the book's real content. It is non-negotiable and runs even when a grammar block is being taught. Track B is the exam-coverage layer.

### 1c. What is already taught (consolidation context)

The following topics were taught in Class 1 this year with approximately 70–80% class grasp. The drive closes the remaining gap; it does not re-teach from zero:

- Noun identification · Naming sentence ("This is a ___") · Description sentence (adjective-containing) · Article *a/an/the* · Place sentence (prepositions *in/on/under*)

Grammar terms (Noun, Article, Preposition, Adjective) are introduced **openly by name** from Block 2 onward, in line with the charter Rule 1 (explicit, book-bounded).

## §5 — Vocabulary cycle design (Class 1)

### 5a. Pool sourcing

Words were drawn from three sources, in priority order:

**Source 1 — NCTB Class 1 *English for Today* (2025 ed., OCR-extracted):** All readable content words from Units 1–5 (greetings dialogues, rhymes, classroom commands, Q&A lessons, family tree, animal sounds). ## §6 — Bloom's ladder (Class 1)

*Authority: REF-18 V1C §4 (class band) + §5 (self-try framing) + §6 (watch-outs). Where this section and REF-18 differ, REF-18 wins.*

### 6a. Class 1 band

**Dominant levels:** Remember · Understand
**Suitable exposure:** simple Apply · simple Create (self-try task only)
**Hard ceiling:** Analyze, Evaluate, Create (beyond self-try) are **not** targeted at Class 1.

**Indicative % mix across a block (REF-18 §4.2, Class 1–2):**

| Remember | Understand | Apply | Analyze | Evaluate | Create |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35–45% | 30–40% | 15–25% | 0–10% | 0–5% | 0–5% (self-try only) |

These percentages describe the **whole block** across all sessions, not a single session. Each block climbs through the band across S1–S3.

### 6b. Session-level climb (every block)

| Session | Bloom's level | What it looks like at Class 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 — Introduce** | Remember | Teacher names the rule; students repeat. *"A plural noun means more than one. Say after me: one cat, two cats."* Point, say, repeat. No explanation yet. |
| **S2 — Guided CW** | Understand → Apply | Teacher explains rule briefly; students apply it to teacher-given items with support. *"Choose: one cat / two cat_. Which is correct? Why?"* Students attempt with teacher guiding. |
| **S3 — Worksheet + self-try** | Apply → simple Create | Students work independently on exam-format items (fill-in, choose, circle). Self-try task at close: student produces one original sentence/answer using the block rule and a word they know. |
| **S4 — Performance Test** | Remember + Apply | Spelling recall (Remember); Bengali meanings recall (Remember); exam-format drill (Apply). No new grammar content. |

### 6c. Block-by-block Bloom's shape

| Block | S1 Remember anchor | S2 Understand/Apply task | S3 Apply task | Self-try (simple Create) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B1 Word** | Spell and say 5 words from the batch | Write the Bengali meaning from memory; say the word from the Bengali clue | Dictation + write 2 words beginning with a target letter | Write 1 word from the batch in a short sentence: *"A ___ is …"* |
| **B2 Plural** | *"Cats. That is a plural. One cat, two cats."* — repeat 5 pairs | Choose singular or plural for 3 items in a sentence | Fill singular/plural table (5 pairs); circle the correct form | Write: *"I have two ___s."* using a word from the batch |
| **B3 Gender** | *"Father — masculine. Mother — feminine."* — repeat 6 pairs | Fill the partner in a table (given one, write the other) | Complete a gender table (6 family pairs); teacher reads a pair, student writes opposite | Write: *"My ___ is a ___."* (e.g. My sister is a girl.) |
| **B4 Adjective** | *"Big. Big is a describing word. A big cat."* — repeat 5 adjectives | Choose the correct adjective from two options (tall/long · soft/hard) | Underline the adjective in 5 description sentences; choose correct adjective for 3 more | Write: *"My ___ is a ___ person."* using one adjective from the batch |
| **B5 Demonstrative** | *"This — near. That — far."* Point to pictures; students call out this/that/these/those | Match demonstrative to picture (near/far; 1 object/many objects) | Fill-in-blank: *"___ is a book."* using picture cues; word box *(as built: no word box printed — PD-023; picture supplies the noun)* | Write one naming-frame sentence about a classroom object: *"This is a ___."* |
| **B6 Pronoun** | *"He, she, it, they — these words replace a name."* Teacher models: *"Nusair → He"* | Draw a line from noun to pronoun (word box); 5 items | Circle the correct pronoun for 5 sentences; fill-in-blank with word box | Write: *"___ is my ___."* replacing a name with the correct pronoun |
| **B7 Verb** | *"Sit — that is a doing word. Sit!"* Teacher acts out 5 verbs; students say the word | Fill-in-blank: choose the correct doing word for 3 sentence contexts (word box) | Fill am/is/are: 5 items. Fill correct doing word: 5 items. Both from word box | Write: *"I ___ every day."* using one action word from the batch |
| **B8 Punctuation** | *"Capital letter starts every sentence. Full stop ends a statement."* Read 2 examples | Identify the error in 3 given sentences (wrong capital / missing full stop) | Rewrite 4 sentences with correct capital, full stop, comma, or question mark | Write 1 sentence about themselves correctly punctuated (*"My name is ___."*) |
| **B9 Rearrangement** | *"Words make a sentence in the right order."* Teacher arranges word cards on board | Arrange 3 sets of 3 words into sentences with teacher guidance | Rearrange 4–5 jumbled-word items (word box); write the correct sentence | Write 1 original sentence using any three words from their vocabulary batch |
| **B10 Composition** | Re-walk three frames: naming / description / place. Read a completed model paragraph | Fill in a word bank paragraph (5 blanks; topic: My Family) with teacher support | Complete a second word-bank paragraph independently (topic: My Country); self-check against model | **Self-try: write the three frames from memory (word bank removed); mark on rubric** |

### 6d. The self-try task (simple Create)

Every block ends in a self-try task. At Class 1, "simple Create" means **producing one original sentence or short response using the block's rule and a word the student has learned** — it is guided, not open-ended.

**Rules for framing a Class 1 self-try:**
- Always anchor to a sentence frame: *"Write your own sentence using the word ___ and the pattern we learned today."*
- Never fully open: *"Write anything you want"* is not appropriate at this level.
- One sentence is enough for B1–B9; B10 self-try is the full paragraph without the word bank.
- Marked on the 10-mark criteria rubric (REF-18 §5), read proportionally for a one-sentence answer — criteria are the point, not the raw total.

**Self-try rubric (Charter §I.3):**

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Content accuracy (rule used correctly) | 1 |
| Reasoning (can the student explain their choice in simple words?) | 1 |
| Organisation (sentence starts with capital, ends with stop) | 1 |
| Correct word-class placement (the word is used in a position its class allows) | 1 |
| **Total** | **4** |

*For one-sentence self-try tasks (B1–B9), teachers read the rubric proportionally. A perfectly correct, well-organised sentence scores 4 regardless of length. Islamic/value alignment is not scored (Charter §I.3, Run Book §13.1).*

### 6e. Watch-outs for Class 1 (REF-18 §6 applied)

- **Copying is not Remember.** Asking students to copy a word from the board is a handwriting task, not a Bloom's task. Recall means from memory, board covered.
- **Choosing is Apply, not just Remember**, if the student must *use* the rule to decide. "Circle the plural" = Apply. "What is the plural of cat?" said aloud = Remember.
- **Doing-word fill-in is Apply**, not Create — there is one correct answer. The self-try is Create because the student decides the content.
- **Do not over-demand.** Class 1 students do not explain grammar rules in English. *"Explain why you used 'an' before 'apple'"* → ask in Bangla, accept a Bangla answer. The rule may be understood even if the student cannot verbalise it in English.
- **Do not under-demand either.** A Class 1 student *can* say which of two adjectives is correct for a picture — that is Apply, not too hard. The self-try is simple Create — it is appropriate and expected.

## §8 — New Word Routine staging (Class 1)

*Authority: charter §N (class band rule). Class 1 sits at the Recognise rung — below Class 3's Apply (Meaning → Class → Use). No dual-job at Class 1.*

### 8a. The Class 1 rung — Recognise

**Routine:** `See → Hear → Say → Mean → Frame`

| Step | What happens | Teacher action | Student action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **See** | Word written on board in clear print | Writes word; points to it | Look |
| **Hear** | Teacher says the word clearly twice | *"Cat. Cat."* | Listen |
| **Say** | Choral repetition × 2, then individual | *"All together — cat. Again — cat. Nusair, say it."* | Repeat chorally, then individually |
| **Mean** | Bengali meaning given by teacher; student repeats | *"Cat means বিড়াল. Say — বিড়াল."* | Say the Bengali meaning |
| **Frame** | Teacher uses word in a simple sentence frame; students complete a second frame | *"This is a cat."* → *"A cat is an animal."* → students: *"I see a ___."* | Fill in the word to complete the frame |

This five-step routine runs for every new word when it first enters the Performance Test. It takes **2–3 minutes per word**; for a batch of 8–12 words, the teacher introduces 3–4 new ones per session across S1–S3 of the batch week, not all at once.

### 8b. What Class 1 does NOT do

| Class 3 routine step | Class 1 status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Classify the word by its job (Noun/Verb/Adjective) | **Not at Class 1** | Grammar blocks teach one word class at a time; students do not need to cross-classify independently |
| Dual-job (*water* = Noun here, Verb there) | **Not at Class 1** (charter §N) | Dual-job starts at Class 3; Class 1 has one class per word |
| Clue card (naming/doing/describing/how-when-where) | **Not at Class 1** | No clue card; grammar term introduced inside the relevant block only |
| *"Use it in a sentence of your own"* (free production) | **Not in the routine** | Free production is the self-try task at block close (§9), not the word routine |

### 8c. How the routine connects to each block

The routine introduces words in the vocabulary cycle. Grammar blocks then *reuse* held words — students do not see the routine inside the grammar block, but the word is already familiar. The connection:

| Block | Words already held via routine | Routine step most relevant to the block |
|---|---|---|
| B2 Plural | cat, dog, bird, cow, duck, frog, book, tree… | **Frame** — students already said *"This is a cat"*; now they say *"two cats"* |
| B3 Gender | father, mother, brother, sister, teacher, student | **Mean** — Bengali meanings anchor the family relationships |
| B4 Adjective | good, big, small, clean, little, brown, white, black, red, quiet | **Frame** — students already used adjectives inside *"a ___ cat"* frames |
| B5 Demonstrative | full noun pool | **See + Frame** — naming-frame sentence (*"This is a ___"*) mirrors the routine's Frame step |
| B6 Pronoun | all nouns | **Say + Mean** — students know the noun; now they learn which pronoun replaces it |
| B7 Verb | sit, stand, open, close, look, listen, read, write, say, go, live, jump, fly | **Hear + Frame** — action words were heard as classroom commands; now formalised |
| B10 Composition | full pool | All five steps — composition paragraph is the longest Frame task |

### 8d. Introducing new words in the Performance Test

When a new batch enters the Performance Test for the first time, the teacher runs a quick **2-minute preview** at the start of S4 (before the test begins):
- Write the 3 new words from this week's batch on the board.
- Run See → Hear → Say → Mean for each (no Frame in the preview — Frame happens in S1–S3).
- Then begin the test.

This ensures no student encounters a word for the very first time on the test paper. The preview is not teaching — it is a memory-activation step.

### 8e. No dual-job — Class 1 cap

**No word in the Class 1 pool carries a dual-job tag.** The word *open* (Verb in B7) is not also taught as an adjective (*"the open door"*) at Class 1 — that distinction waits until Class 3. The word *good* (Adjective) is not a verb at Class 1. Every pool word has exactly one POS tag and is taught in exactly one grammar block.

If a student asks *"Is 'open' a noun too?"* — teacher script: *"Good question. In Class 1, 'open' is a doing word. We will learn more about that word later."* Do not introduce dual-job; acknowledge the question and move on.

## §9 — Self-try (free-thinking) task

*Charter §I: every grammar block ends with a self-construction task. This is the simple Create moment in the Bloom's climb — the student produces something of their own using the block's rule. It is never optional.*

### 9a. Purpose and framing rules

The self-try task exists so that students move beyond drill and do something the teacher did not write for them. At Class 1, this is kept tightly anchored — students fill in a sentence frame, not a blank page.

**Rules for every Class 1 self-try:**

- **Anchored, not open.** Always provide a sentence frame or a clear constraint. *"Write your own sentence using a word you learned and the pattern we studied today."* Never: *"Write anything."*
- **One sentence is enough** for B1–B9. B10 (Composition) is the only block where the self-try is a short paragraph.
- **Bangla is accepted for the reasoning step.** If the rubric asks *why*, a student may explain in Bangla. The English sentence itself must be in English.
- **The word must be from the held pool.** Students choose which word — that choice is itself a small creative act.
- **Islamic anchor optional but encouraged.** The frame can be about family, school, Allah's creation, or daily life. Avoid culturally neutral fillers (*"The cat sat on a mat"*) when an Islamic equivalent is equally simple (*"The cat is in the masjid garden."*).
- **No word-class classification required.** Class 1 does not name the word's class before using it (that starts at Class 3). The task is: use the rule correctly with a real word.

### 9b. Per-block self-try tasks

| Block | Self-try task | Frame given to student | Islamic anchor example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1 Word** | Write 1 sentence using any word from this week's batch | *"A ___ is ___."* | *A bird is an animal. / A tree is in the garden.* |
| **B2 Plural** | Write 1 sentence with a plural noun from the pool | *"I see two ___s."* | *I see two birds. / I see two trees in the masjid.* |
| **B3 Gender** | Write 1 sentence naming a family member and their gender role | *"My ___ is a ___."* | *My father is a man. / My sister is a girl.* |
| **B4 Adjective** | Write 1 description sentence using an adjective from the pool | *"___ is a ___ [adjective] ___."* | *Nusair is a good student. / The masjid is a big building.* |
| **B5 Demonstrative** | Write 1 naming-frame sentence about a classroom object or picture, using this/that/these/those | *"___ is a ___."* / *"___ are ___s."* | *This is a book. / These are flowers.* |
| **B6 Pronoun** | Write 1 sentence replacing a name with the correct pronoun | *"___ [pronoun] is my ___."* | *He is my brother. / She is my teacher.* |
| **B7 Verb** | Write 1 sentence using a doing word from the pool, OR 1 am/is/are sentence | *"I ___ every day."* / *"___ is ___."* | *I read the Quran every day. / Allah is great.* |
| **B8 Punctuation** | Write 1 correctly punctuated sentence about themselves or their family | Free — student chooses sentence; must use correct capital + full stop or question mark | *My name is Aisha.* / *Is your mother at home?* |
| **B9 Rearrangement** | Write 1 original sentence using any 3 words from the pool (student chooses) | *"Write a sentence using three of your words."* | *My father has a big house. / The bird is on the tree.* |
| **B10 Composition** | Write the three-frame paragraph **without** the word bank (word bank removed for self-try only) | Naming frame + description frame + place frame | *"This is my father. He is a good man. He lives in Sylhet."* |

### 9c. Marking rubric (4 marks — Charter §I.3)

Applies to every block's self-try task. For one-sentence tasks (B1–B9), read proportionally — a perfectly correct, well-organised sentence earns full marks regardless of length.

**Rubric** = Charter §I.3 — **4 marks, four criteria at 1 mark each, uniform across all five classes**. Islamic/value alignment is **not** a scored criterion (Charter §I.3, Run Book §13.1): §H binds all authored material in full, but a religiously neutral on-topic student answer earns full marks, and a §H conflict is flagged as a values matter rather than marked down. Each criterion scores 1 or 0; no partial credit.

| Criterion | Marks | What it looks like at Class 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **Content accuracy** — rule used correctly | 1 | Correct plural form / correct adjective / correct pronoun / correctly punctuated etc. |
| **Reasoning** — student can explain their choice (in English or Bangla) | 1 | *"I use 'an' because 'apple' starts with a vowel sound."* / *"বাবা মানে father, তাই I write 'my father'."* |
| **Organisation** — capital letter start; full stop or question mark end; neat | 1 | Sentence starts with capital; ends with correct stop mark; legible handwriting |
| **Correct word-class placement** — the word is used in a position its class allows | 1 | *father* used as a naming word, *runs* as a doing word — Class 1 is not asked to *name* the class, only to place the word correctly |
| **Total** | **4** | |

*Note: the rubric is now identical at every class (Charter v1.3 §I.4). Class 1 gains the word-class-placement criterion it previously lacked, judged by correct use rather than by naming the class.*

### 9d. Teacher script for running the self-try

The self-try closes S3. Approximate timing: **5–7 minutes**.

1. *"Now you will try on your own. I will not help. This is your turn to think."* (Bangla: *"এখন তুমি নিজে চেষ্টা করবে। আমি সাহায্য করব না।"*)
2. Write the sentence frame on the board. Read it aloud once.
3. Students choose their word from the batch list (posted or dictated).
4. Students write their sentence in silence. No copying from classmates.
5. After 4–5 minutes: 2–3 volunteers share aloud. Teacher gives brief feedback on accuracy only — no marking aloud in front of the class.
6. Collect books. Mark using the rubric before the next session.

**Do not skip the self-try** even if time is short. If S3 overruns, cut the final worksheet item — the self-try is the highest-Bloom's moment in the block and cannot be dropped.

## §10 — Values and house style (Class 1)

*Authority: charter §H (Salafi/athari alignment) + charter §N (house style). All content for Class 1 is authored in-house — no textbook passages, no internet-sourced examples. Where this section and the charter differ, the charter wins.*

### 10a. Core values alignment

All student-facing content in every Class 1 block is:

| Rule | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Tawhid-centred** | Allah is the creator, sustainer, and giver. Sentences about creation, food, animals, and nature reference Allah as their source. *"Allah made the bird."* / *"We eat the food Allah gives us."* |
| **No shirk or bid'ah** | No superstitions, no saint-veneration, no fortune-telling, no ritual not grounded in Quran/Sunnah. If a theme could carry this risk, change the theme. |
| **No other-faith festival content** | No Christmas, Diwali, Eid-e-Milad (birthdays of the Prophet), or any celebration not authentically grounded in Islam. Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha are permitted contexts. |
| **No music or instruments** | The book contains rhymes/songs — these are handled in Track A oral only, not grammar block content. No song lyrics in worksheets. Dhikr, du'ā, and Quran recitation are positive alternatives. |
| **Modest and akhlaq-positive** | Sentences show good manners, family respect, care for creation, honesty, kindness. No aggression, mockery, or culturally inappropriate content. |
| **Same-gender and mahram pairings** | Non-mahram pairings (unrelated boy + girl in a descriptive sentence) are not used. Family pairs (brother/sister; father/mother) are fine. House names are used in same-gender contexts unless a mahram relationship is explicit. |

### 10b. House-style names

Use these names only. Do not import names from the NCTB book (Raju, Sheela, Rita, Sakib, Sara etc.) into grammar block content — those are Track A oral/dialogue names only.

| Male names | Female names |
|---|---|
| Nusair · Yusuf · Abdullah · Abdur Rahim | Aisha · Raima · Porshi · Rabab · Jesmin Akter |

**Pairing rule:** in example sentences that mention two people, pair same-gender names OR an explicit family pair (Nusair and his father; Aisha and her sister). Never pair an unrelated male and female name in the same sentence at Class 1.

**Name frequency:** rotate names across blocks so no single name appears more than 3 times in one worksheet. This prevents the class from associating a grammar concept with one child's name.

### 10c. Themes — permitted and excluded

**Permitted themes (use freely):**
- Family: father/mother/brother/sister/grandfather/grandmother — daily family life
- School and learning: classroom, teacher, books, studying, reading Quran
- Allah's creation: animals, trees, flowers, water, sky, birds — with gratitude frame
- Salah and Islamic daily life: Fajr, Eid, fasting (Ramadan), du'ā — age-appropriate references only
- Cleanliness and hygiene: washing hands, keeping home/school clean — book-grounded theme
- Food and sustenance: halal foods from the vocabulary pool (fish, bird — not pork); gratitude to Allah
- Village and city life: home, pond, field, forest — Bangladesh-grounded settings

**Excluded themes:**
- Birthdays and birthday parties (not Islamic celebration)
- Fictional characters (Disney, cartoons, superheroes)
- Sports content requiring non-mahram mixing
- Violence, conflict, or negative behaviour (even as a "don't do this" example)
- Animals used in un-Islamic contexts (dogs as pets indoors — dogs are impure in Islamic fiqh; use cats, birds, cows instead)
- Music, singing, dancing

**Class 1 note:** at this age, the most natural themes are family, classroom, and animals. These overlap almost entirely with the vocabulary pool. Default to these three unless a block specifically needs a different context (e.g. B8 Punctuation benefits from variety in sentence types).

### 10d. Example sentence bank

These sentences are pre-cleared for use in any Class 1 block. Teachers and block builders may use them as-is or adapt by swapping the bold word with another pool word.

**Naming sentences (B2 Plural / B5 Demonstrative / B10 Composition):**

- This is my **father**. · This is my **sister**. · This is my **teacher**.

### 10e. Delivery language

- **Classroom language:** Bangla for instruction and explanation. English for grammar terms, examples, and student production.
- **Grammar terms** are introduced and kept in English: noun, plural, adjective, pronoun, verb, etc. Students learn and use the English term from the first lesson of each block.
- **Track A oral routine** is conducted in English only (the book's actual language of delivery).
- **Bengali meanings** in the vocabulary Performance Test are written in Bangla script by the student — this is a tested skill, not a workaround.
- **Reasoning in self-try** may be given in Bangla (see §9a) — this is an accommodation, not a permanent expectation. As the drive progresses, teachers may gently encourage simple English reasoning (*"I use 'an' because…"*) from students who are ready.
