## Adjective — Clue Card · `C1B04-CC`

Built with the class Sun–Tue; on the wall by Thursday. The English rule and examples are the teaching content; the one Bangla line is a recognition cue, and Bangla meanings are given **orally** by the teacher, not printed beside the words.

> ### An adjective is a **describing word.**
> It describes a naming word (noun) — like *boy, bird, mango.*
>
> **Noun কে বর্ণনা করে। জিজ্ঞেস করে — এটি কেমন?**
>
> **Our describing words:**
> tall · long · soft · hard
> clean · dirty · sweet · sour
> weak · strong

**The five describing pairs we learn this week:**

| Adjective | Example |
|---|---|
| **tall** ↔ **long** | a **tall** boy · a **long** road |
| **soft** ↔ **hard** | a **soft** bed · a **hard** stone |
| **clean** ↔ **dirty** | a **clean** hand · a **dirty** bag |
| **sweet** ↔ **sour** | a **sweet** mango · a **sour** lemon |
| **weak** ↔ **strong** | a **weak** cat · a **strong** lion |

> **How to read this chart:**
> Read the **English word first**, then the picture in your mind, then the meaning.
> *A describing word comes before a naming word:* **a soft bird**, **a strong cow**.

*Teacher note (optional — §3.14 r.4):* বাচ্চা যদি ইংরেজি শব্দটি বুঝতে না পারে, তখনই মুখে বাংলা অর্থটি বলুন। চার্টে বাংলা আছে শুধু সাহায্যের জন্য — বাচ্চাকে আগে ইংরেজি শব্দটি পড়তে দিন, নাহলে সে ইংরেজি পড়া বাদ দিয়ে বাংলার উপর নির্ভর করবে। *(Give the Bangla meaning aloud only when a child cannot follow the English word. The Bangla on the chart is a support — let the child read the English first, or they will lean on the Bangla and stop reading the English.)*

## Teacher checklist

- ☐ Teach only to the §2 cap — **recognition only**: choose the correct adjective; underline the adjective. **No sub-type labelling** (quality/size/colour), **no comparatives**, **no production** beyond the one guided self-try.
- ☐ Re-walk "naming word / noun" (Blocks 1–2) fast before the block's own work — a bridge, not re-teaching.
- ☐ Teach the term **adjective** — say it (**ad · jec · tive**, read it part by part from the board), read the whole word, spell it. Taught and recalled, **not** a graded blank.
- ☐ Teach → read → spell → **use**: every adjective is taught for meaning, reading, and spelling **before** it appears in any choose-item. A word a child cannot read is not a distractor — it is noise.
- ☐ Graded items use the declared **override set** only, and **only adjectives taught up to that day** (stage table). Nothing graded before its teaching day.
- ☐ Each teaching day (Sun–Wed) closes with its **Exit Check** — one prompt per student (**Sylhet Class 1 roll = 7**), 2–5 min, oral, day's target only. At Class 1 the answer alone suffices where the target is recall/recognition. Adjust prompt count on the day if the roll changes, without a file revision (§9.4).
- ☐ **Wednesday: no new vocabulary** — recognition, matching, underline; **ungraded** self-try rehearsal. Tell the class the Thursday PT covers spelling/meanings + a describing-word sentence of their own.
- ☐ **Thursday graded self-try** uses **different prompts** from Wednesday's rehearsal (§6.5), same item-type coverage.
- ☐ Values: **no living-being imagery** (word-cards, not pictures), non-mahram screen, sacred-word guard (§H.3), **attribution screen** (no natural phenomenon as an autonomous giver — screen every nature sentence).
- ☐ Worksheets **English only**; Bangla given orally where a child is stuck, never printed beside items.
- ☐ **CW is completed in class every day (Class 1 standing rule)** — worksheets are sized (4–5 items) to finish within the lesson; HW is separate independent practice, never carried-over CW.
- ☐ Answer keys **programmatically verified** — stage audit, de-patterning (no run >2), CW↔HW order ≤35%, HW ≤2 identical to CW, single defensible answer per blank, totals recomputed from the key.

# Sunday — First Teaching: the term *adjective* · pairs 1–2 (tall/long, soft/hard)

**Day 1 of 4 teaching days** · 35 minutes · English throughout · Clue Card issued today
**Vocabulary today:** the term *adjective*; **tall, long, soft, hard** (first exposure — read and spell).

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine (never cut) |
| 2 | 5–8 | Re-walk: what is a naming word? |
| 3 | 8–14 | **The term: adjective** (say · break · read · spell) |
| 4 | 14–18 | Teach pair 1 — **tall / long** (meaning · read · spell · use) |
| 5 | 18–23 | Teach pair 2 — **soft / hard** (meaning · read · spell · use) |
| 6 | 23–33 | CW-1 — in the notebook (~10 min) |
| 7 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)

Run the standing routine (§3e): alphabet spot-check · numbers 1–10 · greeting · command drill · one Q&A pattern. **Never shortened to make room for grammar.**

### Step 2 — Re-walk: what is a naming word? (5–8 min)

**Books closed.** Write: `boy · bird · mango · book · tree`

> "From our earlier blocks — what do we call these words?" → **Naming words. Nouns.**
> "A naming word names a person, an animal, a place, or a thing. Say it — *a naming word names something.*"
> "Today we learn a **new kind of word** — a word that **describes** a naming word."

### Step 3 — The term: adjective (8–14 min)

> "Look at these: a **tall** boy. A **soft** bed. A **sweet** mango. The words *tall, soft, sweet* — they **describe** the naming word. They are called **adjectives**."

Board-write **adjective**. Show the parts: **ad · jec · tive** — teacher reads it part by part, class reads it back part by part, then the whole word, ×3.

> "Read it with me — *adjective.*" (whole word, ×3)
> "Now we spell it: **a – d – j – e – c – t – i – v – e**." Class says the letters aloud; teacher writes each letter on the board as they say it; class copies the word once.
> "An adjective is a **describing word.** It describes a **naming word.**"

Post the Clue Card; read the top line together.

*First exposure of the term — say it, read it, spell it. Recall is checked from Monday.*

### Step 4 — Teach pair 1: tall / long (14–18 min)

Word-cards, one at a time. **Meaning → read → spell → use.**

| Teacher does | Class |
|---|---|
| Show **tall**. "This says **tall**. A tall boy is high — he stands up high." | reads *tall* ×3 |
| "Spell it — t – a – l – l." | says letters; copies once |
| Show **long**. "This says **long**. A long road goes far — like this." (gesture) | reads *long* ×3 |
| "Spell it — l – o – n – g." | says letters; copies once |
| "A **tall** tree. A **long** road. Which word for the boy who stands high?" | *tall* |
| "Which for the road that goes far?" | *long* |

*(Bangla meaning given orally only if a child is stuck — not printed. In Bangla both are **লম্বা**: **tall** is লম্বা in height (উপরে), **long** is লম্বা in length. English has two words where Bangla has one — that is the thing to teach. Let the child read the English first.)*

Clue Card row 1.

### Step 5 — Teach pair 2: soft / hard (18–23 min)

| Teacher does | Class |
|---|---|
| Show **soft**. Touch something soft. "This says **soft**. A pillow is soft." | reads *soft* ×3 |
| "Spell it — s – o – f – t." | says letters; copies once |
| Show **hard**. Tap the desk. "This says **hard**. A stone is hard." | reads *hard* ×3 |
| "Spell it — h – a – r – d." | says letters; copies once |
| "A **soft** bed. A **hard** stone. Which for the bed?" | *soft* |
| "Which for the stone?" | *hard* |

Clue Card row 2.

### Step 6 — CW-1 in the notebook (23–33 min)

**No worksheet.** Teacher writes the four items on the board (or reads each aloud); children copy and answer in the **khata**. Model item 1: read the sentence, read **both** choices, think which fits, circle/write it. *"My father is a tall man — tall or long? A man stands up high — **tall**."* Four items — completed in class in the ~10 min. **HW-1** goes home as the separate worksheet.

**CW-1 notebook items (answers for the teacher in brackets):**
1. My father is a (tall / long) man. → **tall**
2. The tree is very (tall / soft). → **tall**
3. A pillow is (soft / hard). → **soft**
4. The stone is (hard / long). → **hard**

### Step 7 — Exit Check (33–35 min)

One prompt per student (roll = 7). Teacher shows/says a describing word from today; student **reads it and uses it in "a ___ ___"** (adjective + noun).

| # | Prompt | Sample answer |

| 1 | tall | a tall tree |

| 3 | long | a long road |
| 4 | hard | a hard stone |
| 5 | tall | a tall boy |
| 6 | soft | a soft pillow |
| 7 | hard | a hard desk |

*Answer alone suffices (§9.4 — recognition target). All four Sunday words covered; the four adjectives recur so every one of the 7 students is heard.*

# Wednesday — Consolidation: recognition + underline + ungraded self-try rehearsal

**Day 4 of 4** · 35 minutes · English throughout

### Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)

Standing routine (§3e).

### Step 2 — Revise all ten + new task shape (5–12 min)

Fast oral round, all ten words. Then teach the recognition move:

> "In a sentence, the adjective is the **describing word**. In *'The tall boy reads a book'*, which word describes the boy?" → **tall.** "We **underline** it."
> Model 2–3 on the board. The child finds the adjective and underlines it.

### Step 3 — CW-4 out (12–24 min)

Distribute **CW-4** (underline-the-adjective; all five pairs, all taught words). Five items — students complete **CW-4 in class** in the ~12 min. **HW-4** goes home as separate independent practice.

### Step 4 — Self-try rehearsal (24–31 min) — **ungraded**

> "Now you try on your own. I will not help. Write **one** sentence. Use **one** describing word you learned and a **naming word** you know."

Frame on the board: **___ is a ___ [adjective] ___.** *(e.g. My brother is a tall boy.)*

Students write in silence, ~4 min. 2–3 volunteers read aloud; teacher gives feedback on **accuracy only** — **no marks**. This is the rehearsal; Thursday's graded self-try uses **different prompts** (§6.5).
