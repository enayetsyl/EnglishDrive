## Preposition Clue Card

> ### THE PREPOSITION — **পদান্বয়ী অব্যয়**
>
> A **preposition** is a small word that sits **before a noun** and shows **where** a thing is, **when** something happens, or **how** one thing relates to another.
> *(পদান্বয়ী অব্যয় একটি ছোট শব্দ — noun-এর **আগে** বসে; জায়গা (কোথায়), সময় (কখন), বা সম্পর্ক বোঝায়। এটি কোনো কিছু বর্ণনা করে না।)*
>
> **Ask yourself:** *Does this word show **where** or **when** — the position or time of a thing?* → if yes, it is a **preposition**.
> *(নিজেকে জিজ্ঞাসা করো: শব্দটি কি **কোথায়** বা **কখন** বোঝাচ্ছে? — হ্যাঁ হলে এটি preposition।)*
>
> ---
>
> **① Place — কোথায় (where a thing is)**
>
> | Preposition | বাংলা | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **in** | ভিতরে | The ball is **in** the box. |
> | **on** | উপরে | The book is **on** the table. |
> | **under** | নিচে | The bag is **under** the chair. |
> | **near** | কাছে | The shop is **near** the river. |
> | **into** | ভিতরের দিকে (moving) | The ball rolled **into** the box. |
> | **at** | নির্দিষ্ট জায়গায় | My uncle waits **at** the gate. |
>
> **② Time — কখন (when something happens)**
>
> | Preposition | Use | বাংলা | Example |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | **at** | a clock time | ঘড়ির সময় | The class starts **at** 6 o'clock. |
> | **on** | a day or date | দিন / তারিখ | We go to school **on** Sunday. |
> | **in** | a month, season, part of day | মাস / ঋতু / দিনের অংশ | My exam is **in** June. · We meet **in** the morning. |
>
> **③ Relation — সম্পর্ক (how things connect)**
>
> | Preposition | বাংলা | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | **with** | সঙ্গে (together) | I go to school **with** my friend. |
> | **from** | থেকে (coming from) | I got a letter **from** my sister. |
> | **of** | এর (belonging to) | This is the bag **of** my brother. |
> | **to** | দিকে (towards) | I give the book **to** my teacher. |
>
> ---
>
> **⚑ Remember — মনে রেখো:**
> - A preposition always sits **before a noun**: *in the **box**, on **Sunday**, with my **friend***.
> *(preposition সবসময় noun-এর আগে বসে।)*
> - **at** does two jobs — a **place** (*at the shop*) **and** a **time** (*at 6 o'clock*). Look at the word after it.
> *(at-এর দুটি কাজ: জায়গা ও সময়।)*
> - A preposition shows **where / when**; it does **not describe** a thing (that is an adjective's job).
> *(preposition জায়গা/সময় দেখায় — বর্ণনা করে না।)*

*(The ten cap prepositions — in · on · under · at · with · from · into · near · of · to. The place row is illustrated by the non-living picture chart (ball + box/table/chair/gate style); the time uses are shown on a clock + calendar cue.)*

# Class 1 (Sunday) — Prepositions of place: *where* a thing is (box + ball)

**Objective.** Students recognise a preposition as a word that sits before a noun and shows **where** a thing is (in / on / under / near / into / at-a-place), using a real box and ball; and find the preposition in a sentence.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Re-walk: the classes we know (four core + pronoun) |
| 2 | 5–14 | Box + ball — *where* words (live demo) |
| 3 | 14–21 | The preposition sits before a noun |
| 4 | 21–27 | Find the preposition |
| 5 | 27–29 | Preposition Clue Card (Place row) |
| 6 | 29–32 | CW-1 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Re-walk the classes we know (0–5 min)

Books closed. Write on the board: `river · flow · cold · it`

> **Say:** "Before something new, let us remember our Clue Card. Four classes we already know. *River* — does it name a thing, or act?" → **"Names — noun."** "*Flow* — name or action?" → **"Action — verb."** "*Cold* — what does it do to a noun?" → **"Describes it — adjective."** "And *it* — does it name something new, or stand in place of a noun?" → **"Stands in place — pronoun."**

> **Say:** "So far we know these classes: **Noun, Verb, Adjective, Adverb** — the four we built first — and **Pronoun**, which we added last block. Today we meet a new one — a word that does not name, act, describe, replace, or tell *how*. It tells us **where** a thing is, **when** something happens, or **how** things relate. Today we start with the first job — **where**."

**Teacher note — say both terms in class.** As every week: *"The **role** of a word is what the exam calls its **part of speech**. When the exam says 'identify the part of speech,' it means the word's role."* Repeat through the week so no child meets "part of speech" first in the exam.

### Step 2 — Box + ball: the *where* words (5–14 min)

**Hold up the box and the ball.** This is the concept-discovery step — move the ball, let the class name the position. Do it slowly; let them see each one. *(As you name each object — box, ball — write it on the board; these ten concrete words are the block-local set taught today, PD-012.)*

> **Say:** "Watch my hands. I have a box and a ball. I am going to put the ball in different places, and you tell me **where** it is."

Perform each, pausing for the class to answer, then write the *where*-word on the board as they say it:

| Teacher does | Ask | Class says | Write |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drops ball inside the box | "Where is the ball?" | "In the box." | **in** |
| Rests ball on top of the box | "And now?" | "On the box." | **on** |
| Holds ball under the box | "Now where?" | "Under the box." | **under** |
| Holds ball close beside the box | "And now — close to it?" | "Near the box." | **near** |
| Rolls ball into the box | "Watch it move — where does it go?" | "Into the box." | **into** |

> **Say (after all five):** "Every answer had a little word doing the same job — *in, on, under, near, into*. Each one told us **where** the ball is. A word that shows **where** a thing is, is called a **Preposition**."

> **Say (the *into* note):** "One small difference. *In* shows where the ball **rests** — *in the box*. *Into* shows the ball **moving** to get there — *the ball rolls into the box*. Movement → *into*."

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student answers with a describing word** (e.g. "the ball is red"): "Red tells us **what kind** of ball — that is an adjective. I am not asking what kind; I am asking **where**. Listen again — *where* is the ball? In, on, under? That *where*-word is our preposition."

**`at` — place use, introduced here.**
> **Say:** "One more *where*-word, but this one points at a **spot** rather than in-or-on. We say the ball is **at** the corner, or a boy is **at** school, **at** the mosque, **at** the shop — *at* points to the place where someone or something is. Remember *at* — we will meet it again tomorrow doing a second job, with **time**."

Write `at` on the board with the others. *(Flag lightly — the dual job lands properly Day 2.)*

### Step 3 — The preposition sits before a noun (14–21 min)

Write, one under the other:

```
The ball is in the box.
The bird sits on the tree.
The cat hides under the chair.
The shop is near the river.
```

> **Say:** "Look where each preposition sits. *In* — what word comes right after it?" → **"box."** → "A noun. *On* — after it?" → **"tree — a noun."** "Every time, the preposition sits **before a noun** and shows where. *in the box · on the tree · under the chair · near the river*."

> **Say:** "This is important, because an adjective **also** sits near a noun. But watch the difference: *a **tall** tree* — *tall* **describes** the tree, tells us what kind. *the bird **on** the tree* — *on* does not describe the tree; it tells us **where** the bird is. Describe → adjective. Where → preposition."

**Anticipated confusion & correction.**
> **If a student marks the noun after the preposition as the preposition** (e.g. says "box" is the where-word): "*Box* is the naming word — the noun. The where-word is the small word **before** it: *in*. The preposition points to the noun; it is not the noun itself."

### Step 4 — Find the preposition (21–27 min)

> **Say:** "Now you find the *where*-word. I read a sentence; you tell me which word shows where, and which noun it sits before."

Work through aloud (all nouns held or block-local):

| Sentence | Preposition | Sits before |
|---|---|---|
| The book is **on** the desk. | on | desk |
| The children play **in** the park. | in | park |
| A boat floats **near** the river. | near | river |
| The bag is **under** the table. | under | table |
| The ball rolled **into** the box. | into | box |
| My uncle waits **at** the shop. | at | shop |

> **Say (item 1):** "*The book is on the desk.* Which word shows where the book is?" → **"on."** → "And it sits before which noun?" → **"desk."** "Good — *on the desk*."

> **Say (close the step):** "Every preposition points to a noun and shows **where**. Ask yourself: *does this little word tell me where a thing is?* If yes — preposition."

### Step 5 — Preposition Clue Card (27–29 min)

> **Say:** "Let us look at our Preposition Clue Card." *(Pin it beside the box-and-ball demo; the place row's picture chart goes up here.)*

> **Preposition** *(পদান্বয়ী অব্যয়)* — sits before a noun, shows **where** (or **when**) · in · on · under · at · near · into
> *(noun-এর আগে বসে — জায়গা বা সময় দেখায়।)*

*(Today the class meets the **Place** row; the Time and Relation rows are filled in on Days 2 and 3.)*

### Step 6 — CW-1 (29–32 min)

Distribute CW-1. Model item 1:

> **Say:** "Item one — *The child plays in the park.* Underline the preposition — the *where*-word. Which is it?" → **"in."** → "Write it on the line, then finish on your own. What you do not finish goes home as HW-1."

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

Teacher reads; one student per prompt names the preposition (the *where*-word).

| # | Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The ball is **in** the box. | in |
| 2 | The bird sits **on** the branch. | on |
| 3 | The cat hides **under** the chair. | under |
| 4 | The shop is **near** the river. | near |
| 5 | The ball rolled **into** the box. | into |
| 6 | My uncle waits **at** the corner. | at |
| 7 | The book is **on** the desk. | on |
| 8 | The children play **in** the garden. | in |
| 9 | A boat floats **near** the bridge. | near |

# Class 2 (Monday) — Prepositions of time: *when* something happens (at / on / in)

**Objective.** Students use *at / on / in* to show **when** something happens (at = exact time; on = days/dates; in = months/seasons/parts of the day), told apart from the place use met on Day 1 — on a clock/calendar cue, not object pictures.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Day 1 — where-words |
| 2 | 4–8 | prepositions' second job: time (starting with *at*) |
| 3 | 8–16 | on (days/dates) · in (months/parts of day) |
| 4 | 16–23 | The three-way sort — at / on / in |
| 5 | 23–27 | Place or time? — the same word, two jobs |
| 6 | 27–29 | Preposition Clue Card (Time row) |
| 7 | 29–32 | CW-2 distribution |
| 8 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall the where-words (0–4 min)

Books closed. On the board: `in · on · under · near · at`

> **Say:** "Yesterday, with the box and ball, we learned five *where*-words. What do we call a word that shows where a thing is?" → **"Preposition."** "Good. Today the preposition does a **second** job — it shows not only *where*, but **when**."

### Step 2 — prepositions do a second job: time (starting with *at*) (4–8 min)

Draw a simple **clock** on the board showing 6 o'clock.

> **Say:** "Yesterday I said *at* points to a spot — *at the shop, at school*. Watch it do a new job. *(Point to the clock.)* The class starts **at** six o'clock. I wake up **at** six in the morning. Here *at* does not show where — it shows **when**, an **exact time**."

> **Say:** "So *at* has two jobs — *at the shop* (a place) and *at six o'clock* (a time). And *at* is not alone: **in** and **on** do the same — we saw *in the box* and *on the table* for place, and in a moment we will use *in June* and *on Sunday* for time. Same word; look at what comes after it. A place-noun → where; a time → when."

### Step 3 — *on* (days/dates) and *in* (months, seasons, parts of day) (8–16 min)

Draw a **calendar page** (SUNDAY / 12 May) beside the clock.

> **Say:** "*On* for **days and dates**. *(Point.)* We go to school **on** Sunday. The holiday begins **on** the 12th of May. Whenever we name a **day** or a **date**, we use **on**."

Write:
```
on Sunday        on the 12th of May
```

> **Say:** "*In* for the **bigger times** — months, seasons, and parts of the day. My exam is **in** June. It is cold **in** winter. We will meet **in** the morning. Months, seasons, morning/evening — all take **in**."

Write:
```
in June        in winter        in the morning        in the evening
```

> **Say:** "A quick way to hold it: **at** a clock time, **on** a day or date, **in** a long time — a month, a season, a part of the day. Small time → *at*. Day → *on*. Big time → *in*."

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student says "in Sunday" or "on June":** "Listen to the size of the time. Sunday is a **day** — a day takes **on**: *on Sunday*. June is a **month** — a big time takes **in**: *in June*. Day → on; month → in."

### Step 4 — The three-way sort (16–23 min)

The Analyze step. **Slow down here.**

> **Say:** "I say a time; you tell me *at, on,* or *in* — and **why**."

| Time phrase | Preposition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ___ 8 o'clock | at | exact clock time |
| ___ Sunday | on | a day |
| ___ June | in | a month |
| ___ the morning | in | part of the day |
| ___ the 12th of May | on | a date |
| ___ 6 a.m. | at | exact clock time |
| ___ summer | in | a season |

> **Say (item 1):** "*Eight o'clock* — is that a clock time, a day, or a big time?" → **"Clock time."** → "So we use…?" → **"at."** "*at eight o'clock*."

> **Say (item 4):** "*The morning* — clock time, day, or part of the day?" → **"Part of the day."** → "So…?" → **"in."** "*in the morning*. Good."

### Step 5 — Place or time? The same word, two jobs (23–27 min)

The heart of the day — *at, on, in* each do **both** jobs. Do not rush.

> **Say:** "Now the real skill. *At, on, in* can each show **where** or **when**. The words after them decide. Listen to two sentences with the same word."

| Sentence | Preposition | Job |
|---|---|---|
| The keys are **on** the desk. | on | place (where) |
| We go to school **on** Sunday. | on | time (when) |
| My uncle is **at** the shop. | at | place (where) |
| The class starts **at** 8 o'clock. | at | time (when) |
| The fish swims **in** the pond. | in | place (where) |
| My exam is **in** June. | in | time (when) |

> **Say:** "*On the desk* — desk is a **place**, so *on* shows **where**. *On Sunday* — Sunday is a **day**, so *on* shows **when**. Same word. Different job. Look at what comes after it: a place-noun → where; a day, date, or time → when."

> **Say (close):** "But notice — whether it shows where **or** when, the word's **class** is the same. *On the desk*, *on Sunday* — *on* is a **preposition** both times. The job changes; the part of speech does not. In the exam, both are **prepositions**."

### Step 6 — Preposition Clue Card — Time row (27–29 min)

Point to the **Time** section of the Preposition Clue Card:

> **Time uses —** *at* a clock time (*at 6 o'clock*) · *on* a day or date (*on Sunday*) · *in* a month, season, or part of the day (*in June · in the morning*).
> *(সময়: at — ঘড়ির সময়; on — দিন/তারিখ; in — মাস/ঋতু/দিনের অংশ।)*

### Step 7 — CW-2 (29–32 min)

Distribute CW-2. Model item 1:

> **Say:** "Item one — *A cold night came.* Wait — that has no blank; read on to the fill part. *The class starts ___ 8 o'clock.* Clock time, day, or big time?" → **"Clock time."** → "So we write…?" → **"at."** "Finish on your own; the rest is HW-2."

### Step 8 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

Teacher reads; one student per prompt gives the missing preposition (mix of time and place).

| # | Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The class starts ___ 8 o'clock. | at |
| 2 | We go to school ___ Sunday. | on |
| 3 | My exam is ___ June. | in |

# Class 3 (Tuesday) — Relation prepositions + the identify-P.O.S. boundary (with / from / of / to)

**Objective.** Students use *with / from / of / to* to show the relation between people or things, then identify a preposition sitting **among** noun / verb / adjective / pronoun in an "identify the part of speech" line — the exam's own format.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall: where-words and when-words |
| 2 | 4–12 | Relation words — with / from / of / to |
| 3 | 12–20 | Preposition among the five classes |
| 4 | 20–27 | The identify-P.O.S. boundary sort |
| 5 | 27–29 | Preposition Clue Card (Relation row) |
| 6 | 29–32 | CW-3 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall where and when (0–4 min)

Books closed. On the board: `in · on · under · near · at · into`

> **Say:** "Two days of prepositions. Day one — *where* a thing is: *in the box, near the river*. Day two — *when* something happens: *at 6, on Sunday, in June*. Today, a third job: prepositions that show the **relation** between people or things — how one connects to another."

### Step 2 — Relation words: with / from / of / to (4–12 min)

Write each with a held-vocabulary example, one under the other:

```
with  —  together with        I go to school with my friend.
to    —  towards someone      I give the book to my teacher.
from  —  coming from someone   I got a letter from my sister.
of    —  belonging to          This is the bag of my brother.
```

> **Say (*with*):** "*With* means **together**. *I go to school **with** my friend.* The friend goes along — together with me."

> **Say (*to*):** "*To* points **towards** someone or something. *I give the book **to** my teacher.* The book goes towards the teacher."

> **Say (*from*):** "*From* is the opposite — it shows where something **comes from**. *I got a letter **from** my sister.* The letter came from her."

> **Say (*of*):** "*Of* shows **belonging**. *the bag **of** my brother* — the bag belongs to my brother."

> **Say:** "Four relation words — *with, to, from, of*. Each still sits **before a noun**, like every preposition: *with my friend, to my teacher, from my sister, of my brother*."

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student reads *to* as part of a verb** (e.g. *want to go*): "Careful — today *to* points **towards a noun**: *to my teacher, to the shop*. We are not touching *to* in front of a verb — that is different work we do not do this week." *(Holds the "no prepositional-verb" guardrail.)*

### Step 3 — Preposition among the five classes (12–20 min)

The exam bridge. Recall the **Word-Role Clue Card** (five word-classes, from Blocks 1/5) — the wall chart the identify-P.O.S. question uses.

> **Say:** "The exam does not ask 'find the preposition.' It underlines one word in a sentence and asks: **what is the part of speech?** The underlined word could be any of our five classes. Watch."

Build this on the board, underlining the bold word each time:

| Sentence | Underlined | Class | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| The bird sits **on** the tree. | on | Preposition | shows where |
| The **bird** sits on the tree. | bird | Noun | names a thing |
| The bird **sits** on the tree. | sits | Verb | shows action |
| A **small** bird sits there. | small | Adjective | describes the bird |
| **It** sits on the tree. | It | Pronoun | stands for the bird |

> **Say:** "Same sentence, five different underlined words, five different classes. When the underlined word shows **where** or **when** — *on, in, at, near* — the answer is **Preposition**. That is what the exam wants."

### Step 4 — The identify-P.O.S. boundary sort (20–27 min)

The Analyze step. **Slow down.** These mirror HY25 Q6 / Annual Q4 exactly.

> **Say:** "I read a sentence; you name the part of speech of the underlined word, and **why**. Some are prepositions; some are not — you must decide."

| Sentence | Underlined | Class |
|---|---|---|
| I saw a bird **on** the tree. | on | Preposition |
| The keys are **on** the desk. | on | Preposition |
| My **sister** reads a book. | sister | Noun |
| We play **in** the park. | in | Preposition |
| The boy runs **quickly**. | quickly | Adverb |
| I got a gift **from** my uncle. | from | Preposition |
| **They** live near the river. | They | Pronoun |
| The **tall** tree gives shade. | tall | Adjective |

> **Say (item 1):** "*I saw a bird on the tree.* — *on*. Does it name, act, describe, replace, or show where?" → **"Show where."** → "So the class is…?" → **"Preposition."**

> **Say (item 5):** "*The boy runs quickly.* — *quickly*. Where or how?" → **"How."** → "That is not a preposition — it tells **how** the action is done. We met that word class before: **adverb**. Careful — *quickly* is not a where-word."

> **Say (close):** "So never guess from the word alone. Ask the Clue Card questions in order: names? acts? describes? replaces? shows where or when? The first *yes* is your answer."

### Step 5 — Preposition Clue Card — Relation row (27–29 min)

Point to the **Relation** section of the Preposition Clue Card:

> **Relation uses —** *with* (together) · *to* (towards) · *from* (coming from) · *of* (belonging to). Each sits before a noun: *with my friend · to my teacher · from my sister · of my brother.*

# Class 4 (Wednesday) — Mixed identify, error-find, Self-try demonstration

**Objective.** Students identify prepositions and the four other classes in mixed sentences, find and fix a wrong label, and rehearse the graded Self-try format.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall the week — three jobs, one class |
| 2 | 4–12 | Mixed identify (all five classes) |
| 3 | 12–19 | Error-find: locate + relabel |
| 4 | 19–26 | Use a preposition — build a sentence |
| 5 | 26–33 | Self-try demonstration |
| 6 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall the week (0–4 min)

Books closed. On the board: `where? · when? · relation?`

> **Say:** "This week the preposition did three jobs. *Where* — *in the box, near the river*. *When* — *at 6, on Sunday, in June*. *Relation* — *with my friend, from my sister, of my brother*. But all three jobs, one class. When the exam asks the part of speech of any of these words, the answer is one word: **Preposition**."

### Step 2 — Mixed identify (4–12 min)

> **Say:** "Exam format. I underline a word; you name its part of speech. All the classes you know are here — noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition."

| Sentence | Underlined | Class |
|---|---|---|
| The doctor works **in** the village. | in | Preposition |
| The **doctor** works in the village. | doctor | Noun |
| We **share** our food with others. | share | Verb |
| A **kind** boy helped my sister. | kind | Adjective |
| **She** waits near the gate. | She | Pronoun |
| The old man walks **slowly**. | slowly | Adverb |

> **Say (item 1):** "*in the village* — where, when, or relation?" → **"Where."** → "Part of speech?" → **"Preposition."**
> **Say (item 3):** "*We share our food* — *share*. Names, acts, or shows where?" → **"Acts."** → "Part of speech?" → **"Verb."** "Not every small word is a preposition — read what the word **does**."
> **Say (item 6):** "*The old man walks slowly.* — *slowly*. Where, when, or **how**?" → **"How."** → "Part of speech?" → **"Adverb."** "*Slowly* tells how he walks — not a preposition."

### Step 3 — Error-find: locate + relabel (12–19 min)

> **Say:** "Now harder. Each line has a word with the **wrong** label. Find it and give the right class."

| Line (as a student wrote it) | Wrong | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| *on* the desk → labelled **Noun** | Noun | **Preposition** |
| *river* → labelled **Preposition** | Preposition | **Noun** |
| *near* the shop → labelled **Adjective** | Adjective | **Preposition** |
| *helps* → labelled **Preposition** | Preposition | **Verb** |

> **Say (line 1):** "*on the desk* — someone wrote *on* is a **noun**. Is *on* a naming word?" → **"No — it shows where."** → "So the right class is…?" → **"Preposition."**

> **Say (line 2):** "*river* — labelled **preposition**. Does *river* show where, or **name** a thing?" → **"Names a thing."** → "So *river* is a…?" → **"Noun."** "The preposition is the small word before it — *near the river* — not the river itself."

### Step 4 — Use a preposition — build a sentence (19–26 min)

> **Say:** "The cap says *identify **and use**.* So now you build. I give you a preposition and a held noun; you say a sentence that shows **where** or **when**."

Model two, then take oral offers:

> **Say (model):** "*under* + *table* → *The bag is under the table.* *at* + *6 o'clock* → *The class starts at 6 o'clock.*"

Take offers for: `on + desk` · `in + park` · `near + river` · `with + friend`. Accept any correct, sensible sentence where the preposition sits before the noun and shows where/when/relation. *(Attribution guard if a natural-phenomenon sentence arises — e.g. "The rain falls in June" → gently model "by Allah's will" only if the sentence frames autonomous causation; a plain where/when sentence needs no attribution.)*

### Step 5 — Self-try demonstration (26–33 min)

**Not collected. Not marked.** This is the rehearsal for the graded Self-try (the combined PT's Part F).

> **Say:** "On the test you will do this for marks, with **different** words. Today is practice. I give you a preposition. You: (1) name its part of speech, (2) write one sentence using it correctly, showing where, when, or relation."

Demonstrate with the class, thinking aloud, on **two** demo prepositions:

> **Demo 1 — *under*:** "Part of speech? *Preposition* — it shows where. Sentence? *The ball is under the chair.* Does *under* sit before a noun and show where? Yes. Good."

> **Demo 2 — *at* (time):** "Part of speech? *Preposition*. Sentence? *We go to the mosque at four o'clock.* Here *at* shows **when** — a clock time. Still a preposition."

Then let a few students try aloud with: `with`.

> **Say (close):** "On the test: name the part of speech, then one correct sentence. **Different** prepositions from today — so today's practice does not become the answer." *(Rehearsal/graded disjointness, §6.5 — the graded Self-try box avoids under · at · with.)*
