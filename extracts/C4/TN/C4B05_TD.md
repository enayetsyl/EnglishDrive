**SCHOOL FOR COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT**

**Class 4 — English Grammar Campaign**

**Teacher Delivery Sheet — Block 05: Adjective + Pronoun**

**Runs:** Week 5 · 4 teaching days (Sun–Wed) + Performance Test (Thu) · 35 min/session · **paired block, one shared Performance Test**

---

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the block, students can:

- Recognise an **adjective** as a word that describes a noun — *what kind · which · whose*.
- Recognise that **my · his · her · our · their · this · that** *before a noun* are adjectives.
- Recognise a **personal pronoun** as a word that **stands instead of** a noun — subject (I/he/she/it/we/they) and object (me/him/her/us/them).
- Identify Noun · Verb · Adjective · Pronoun in mixed sentences — the exam format.
- Find a wrong label and correct it, and build their own sentence with a reason.

**Teach only to the cap.** No comparative or superlative. No *mine/hers/ours/theirs* standalone forms. No relative or reflexive pronouns. **No adverb label** — the class holds no adverbs yet.

## Say both terms all week

This week we call a word's job its **role**, because it is the plainer word for the children. **But the exam question says "part of speech."** Tell the class directly and repeat it daily:

> *"The job — the **role** — of a word is what the exam calls its **part of speech**. They mean the same thing."*

Both terms must be familiar by Thursday, so no child meets "part of speech" for the first time in the exam.

## The one design decision — the *my / his / this* boundary

This is the analytic spine of the week, and the binding papers grade exactly this.

- **my · his · her · our · their · this · that — BEFORE a noun → Adjective.** They tell us *whose* or *which*.
- **I · he · she · it · we · they · me · him · her · us · them — standing INSTEAD of a noun → Pronoun.** They replace a naming word.

Day 2 sets up the adjective side; Day 3 lands the pronoun side against it. Teach it as **one idea**, not two rules. Every graded item is pinned by position — a possessive counts as an adjective only when a noun follows it; a pronoun counts only when it stands alone.

**Placement rule (state once, plainly).** A **what-kind** word (*tall, kind, cold*) can sit **before** the noun (*a tall tree*) **or after** *is/are* (*the tree is tall*). A **which** word (*this, that*) and a **whose** word (*my, his, her, our, their*) sit **only before** the noun — never *"the book is my."*

*Position is not the test; the describing job is. Do not drill position.*

## Day 1 (Sunday) — Adjectives: describing words (*what kind / which*)

**Objective.** Students recognise an adjective as a word that describes a noun, find the adjective in a sentence, and use one to describe.

1. **Re-walk the four word-classes (0–6).** Write: `teacher · play · tall · mosque`.

   > "Which word **names** a person or a place?" → *teacher, mosque* → "A naming word is a…?" → **Noun.**
   > "Which word shows an **action**?" → *play* → "Yes — a **Verb**, a doing word."
   > "That leaves one: *tall*. It does not name and it does not act. Today we look closely at the **describing** word."

   *If a student already says "adjective": "You are ahead of us — yes. Let us find out exactly what an adjective does."*

2. **What an adjective does (6–13).** Write and read, one pair under the other:

   ```
   a tree          a tall tree
   a bird          a small green bird
   ```

   > "*A tree.* … *A tall tree.* What has *tall* added? What does it tell us?" → *(size / what kind)* → "Yes — *tall* tells us **what kind** *(কেমন)* of tree. *Tree* names the thing; *tall* describes it. A word that describes a noun — that tells us *what kind* or *which one* — is an **Adjective**."
   > "In *a small green bird*, how many describing words?" → **two.**
   > "Where does the adjective sit — before the noun or after?" → **Before.**
   > "Most often, yes. But an adjective can also come **after** the noun with a joining word like *is* or *are*: *The tree **is tall**.* *Our uniforms **are white**.* Same job — telling what kind — but sitting after."
   > "So do not ask only 'is it before the noun?' Ask: **does this word describe the noun?** If it describes, it is an adjective, wherever it sits."

   *Anticipated error — student points to the verb as the describing word:* "Careful — *play* is a doing word, an action. Describing is not doing. Does the word tell me **what kind**? If yes, adjective. If it tells an **action**, verb."

3. **Find the adjective (13–20).** Write each phrase; read aloud; class names the describing word:

   `The kind teacher · a healthy meal · the dirty spoon · The water is cold · a colourful flower · The boy is strong`

   > "*The kind teacher.* Which word describes the teacher?" → **kind** → "And here it sits **before** *teacher* — the most common place."
   > "*The water is cold* — which word describes the water?" → **cold** → "Here it sits **after** *is*. Still an adjective, because it still describes."

   *Anticipated error — student names the noun instead:* "That is the naming word — the noun. I want the word that **describes** it. What kind of teacher? → *kind*."

4. **Describe with an adjective (20–27).** Write: `___ tree · ___ meal · ___ letter · ___ friend`.

   > "I say a noun; you put an adjective **before** it and say the whole phrase."

   Take several answers each. Accept any held adjective that fits. Praise correct placement specifically.

   *Anticipated error — a bare "tree tall":* "Nearly — but *tree tall* is not complete. Two fixes: put it **before** — *a **tall** tree* — or add *is* and put it **after** — *the tree **is tall***. Say both with me."

   *Keep both noun and adjective from words the class already holds — the child is practising placement, not learning new vocabulary here.*

5. **Clue Card — describing row (27–29).** Read the **Adjective** row and its Bangla gloss aloud with the class:

   > **Adjective** *(বিশেষণ)* — describes a noun: *what kind / which / whose* — tall · kind · **my** book · **this** tree

6. **CW-1 (29–32).** *(CW-1 is on its own sheet.)* Model item 1:

   > "Item one — *The small bird sat on the leaf.* The bold word is *small*. Does it name, act, or describe?" → **Describe** → "What class?" → **Adjective.** "Write it. What you do not finish goes home with HW-1."

7. **Exit Check (32–35).** One student per prompt names the bold word's role. **12 prompts, one per student.**

   | # | Prompt | Answer | # | Prompt | Answer |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | The **tall** tree | Adjective | 7 | a **short** letter | Adjective |
   | 2 | a **healthy** meal | Adjective | 8 | the **cold** water | Adjective |
   | 3 | the **dirty** spoon | Adjective | 9 | a **strong** boy | Adjective |
   | 4 | a **colourful** flower | Adjective | 10 | the **new** library | Adjective |
   | 5 | the **kind** teacher | Adjective | 11 | a **beautiful** picture | Adjective |
   | 6 | the **big** mountain | Adjective | 12 | the **small** bird | Adjective |

## Day 2 (Monday) — *my / his / this* before a noun (still adjectives)

**Objective.** Students recognise that *my/his/her/our/their* and *this/that* **before a noun** describe *whose* or *which* — so they are adjectives — and identify them in a passage.

1. **Recall (0–4).** Books closed. Write: `the tall tree · a healthy meal · the kind teacher · a colourful flower`. Class calls out the adjective each time.

   > "Where does the adjective usually sit?" → **Before the noun** → "Usually before — or after *is/are*. Hold that. Today we meet words you might **not** expect to be adjectives — but they are, for exactly that reason."

2. ***whose* (কার) and *which* (কোনটা) are still describing (4–12).** Write, one column beside the other:

   ```
   a book          my book          this book
   a tree          his tree         that tree
   ```

   > "*A book. My book. This book.* In *my book* — whose book is it? *(কার বই?)*" → *(mine / yours)* → "It tells us **whose**. And *this book* — **which** book?" → **This one.**
   > "Do both come **before** the noun *book*?" → **Yes.** → "They sit before the noun and tell us something about it — **whose**, **which**. That is exactly what a describing word does. So *my, this, his, that* — before a noun — are still **Adjectives**."
   > "Our describing question: *what kind, which, or whose?* Words like *tall* answer **what kind**. *My* and *this* answer **whose** and **which**. All of them describe. All are adjectives."

   **The key moment of the day — when a student says "*my* is a pronoun":**

   > "I understand completely — it feels like one, and later this week you will meet words that really are pronouns. But look at **where *my* sits**. Is there a noun right after it?" → **Yes — *book*.** → "Then it is doing an adjective's job: sitting before the noun, telling us **whose**. The **place** decides the class."

   > *(Reinforce, class repeats once:)* "*my, his, this, that — before a noun — adjective.*"

   *Unlike *tall*, the possessives only sit **before** the noun. We do not say "the book is my." English uses *mine* there — but *mine/yours/hers* are pronouns taught later, not this block.*

3. **Find them in sentences (12–20).** Read aloud; class names the describing word:

   `my uncle · his sister · our school · their village · this bird · that mountain · your teacher`

   > "*My uncle.* The describing word?" → **my** → "It tells us…?" → **whose uncle.**
   > "*This bird.*" → **this** → "It tells us…?" → **which bird.**

   *Anticipated error — student skips *my/his/this* looking for a "normal" adjective:* "There is no *tall* or *kind* here — but there is still a describing word. What sits before *uncle* and tells us whose? → *my*."

4. **Passage identify (20–25).** Write on the board and read aloud slowly:

   ```
   My kind teacher gave us a healthy meal. His small bird sat in the tall tree.
   This green leaf fell on our clean table.
   ```

   > "Find **every** describing word — the plain ones like *kind*, and the whose/which ones like *My* and *This*."

   Underline as they find them: **My, kind, healthy, His, small, tall, This, green, our, clean** (10).

   > "Ten describing words. *kind, healthy, small, tall, green, clean* tell us **what kind**; *My, His, This, our* tell us **whose** or **which**. Every one is an adjective, because every one describes its noun."

   *Anticipated error — student calls *gave* or *fell* an adjective:* "Test it — does *gave* describe a noun, or show an **action**? An action → **verb**."

5. **Clue Card — the boundary line (25–28).** Add with the class, and read the Bangla gloss:

   > *my · his · her · our · their · this · that* **before a noun** → **Adjective** (whose / which)
   > *(noun-এর আগে বসলে এগুলো বিশেষণ — কোনটা / কার তা বলে দেয়।)*

6. **CW-2 (28–32).** *(CW-2 is on its own sheet.)* Model item 1: "Look at the bold word. Is it before a noun? Then what class?" Then let them work; the rest is HW-2.

7. **Exit Check (32–35). 12 prompts.**

   | # | Prompt | Answer | # | Prompt | Answer |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | **my** uncle | Adjective | 7 | the **tall** tree | Adjective |
   | 2 | **this** bird | Adjective | 8 | **his** sister | Adjective |
   | 3 | the **kind** teacher | Adjective | 9 | a **colourful** flower | Adjective |
   | 4 | **our** school | Adjective | 10 | **that** mountain | Adjective |
   | 5 | a **healthy** meal | Adjective | 11 | the **cold** water | Adjective |
   | 6 | **their** village | Adjective | 12 | **your** teacher | Adjective |

## Day 3 (Tuesday) — Pronouns: words that stand *instead of* a noun

**Objective.** Students recognise a personal pronoun as a word that **replaces** a noun, and tell it apart from an adjective sitting before a noun.

1. **Recall the boundary (0–4).** Books closed. Write: `my book · this tree · his sister`.

   > "In *my book* — what role does *my* play?" → **Adjective.** *(Some may hesitate or say "pronoun" — that is the whole point of this week; do not be discouraged.)*
   > *If a student says "pronoun":* "I understand why. But look at **where it sits**. Is there a noun right after it?" → **Yes — *book*.** → "Then it is describing that noun. Before a noun, it does an adjective's job."
   > "So *my, this, his* — before a noun — are all…?" → **Adjectives.** "Hold that firmly, because today we meet words that do the **opposite**. They stand **instead of** the noun."

2. **Replacing a noun (4–12).** Write both lines, one under the other:

   ```
   Yusuf helps his mother.        He helps his mother.
   The teacher likes the boy.     The teacher likes him.
   ```

   > "*Yusuf helps his mother.* Who helps?" → **Yusuf.** → "Now *He helps his mother.* Do we still know who?" → **Yusuf.** → "*He* stands **in the place of** Yusuf."

   Underline *He* and draw a small arrow back to *Yusuf*.

   > "*The teacher likes him.* What did *him* replace?" → **The boy.**
   > "See the difference from yesterday. *My* book — *my* sits **before** the noun and describes it: adjective. *He* sits **in the place of** the noun: pronoun. One describes the noun. The other **takes its place**."

   *Anticipated error — student says the pronoun "describes" the noun:* "Does *He* tell us **what kind** of Yusuf, or **which** Yusuf? No — there is no Yusuf left in the sentence at all. Describing needs a noun to describe; replacing removes the noun."

3. **Subject and object pronouns (12–20).** Build the table with the class:

   | Subject (does the action) — **কর্তা** | Object (receives the action) — **কর্ম** |
   |---|---|
   | I · he · she · it · we · they | me · him · her · us · them |

   > "The **subject** pronoun *(কর্তা)* does the action — usually first. *She helps her mother.* Who helps? *She*. Subject."
   > "The **object** pronoun *(কর্ম)* receives the action — usually after the verb. *The teacher likes him.* Who is liked? *him*. Object."

   Read aloud, class repeats, then individuals:
   `She helps her mother. · The teacher likes him. · We play in the park. · My father met them. · They live in the village. · Aisha gave the picture to me.`

   **The *this/that* light note — say once, do not drill.**

   > "One small thing. Yesterday *this* sat before a noun — *this book* — and was an adjective. But *This is my book* — here *this* stands alone, so it is acting as a pronoun. We will not test this today; just notice it can do both."

4. **The boundary sort (20–26). The heart of the day — slow down here.**

   > "The same little word can be an adjective **or** a pronoun. The **place** decides. I read a sentence; you tell me the role of the bold word, and **why**."

   | Sentence | Word | Role | Why |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | **His** sister is a doctor. | His | Adjective | before the noun *sister* |
   | The teacher likes **him**. | him | Pronoun | stands instead of the boy |
   | **This** bird is small. | This | Adjective | before the noun *bird* |
   | **They** live in the village. | They | Pronoun | stands instead of the people |
   | **Her** brother helped us. | Her | Adjective | before the noun *brother* |
   | My father met **her** at the shop. | her | Pronoun | stands instead of the person |

   **The critical pair — items 5 and 6. Do not rush.** Same word, *her*, in its two jobs.

   > "Item five — *Her brother helped us.* Item six — *My father met her at the shop.* Same word both times. Are they the same class?"

   *Anticipated split answer: some say "yes, same word, same class." This is the exact error to correct.*

   > "No — and this is the most important idea of the week. In *Her brother*, is there a noun right after *her*?" → **Yes — brother.** → "So it describes **whose** brother: **adjective**. In *met her*, is there a noun after *her*?" → **No.** → "It stands in the place of a person: **pronoun**. Same word. Different **place**. Different class."
   > "Never ask only 'what is the word?' Ask 'what is it **doing here** — sitting before a noun, or standing in its place?'"

5. **Clue Card — Pronoun row (26–28).** Add with the class; read the Bangla gloss:

   > **Pronoun** *(সর্বনাম)* — stands instead of a noun · Subject: I/he/she/it/we/they · Object: me/him/her/us/them
   > *(একা বসলে — noun-এর জায়গায় — এগুলো সর্বনাম।)*

6. **CW-3 (28–32).** *(CW-3 is on its own sheet.)* Model item 1: "*She helps her mother.* The bold word is *She*. Before a noun, or standing alone?" → **Standing alone** → **Pronoun.** The rest goes home as HW-3.

7. **Exit Check (32–35). 12 prompts**, alternating the two classes so the check tests the boundary, not a run of one answer. Reasons in two or three words.

   | # | Prompt | Answer | # | Prompt | Answer |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | **She** helps her mother. | Pronoun | 7 | My father met **them**. | Pronoun |
   | 2 | **my** uncle | Adjective | 8 | **his** sister | Adjective |
   | 3 | The teacher likes **him**. | Pronoun | 9 | **They** live in the village. | Pronoun |
   | 4 | **this** bird | Adjective | 10 | Aisha gave it to **me**. | Pronoun |
   | 5 | **We** play in the park. | Pronoun | 11 | **our** school | Adjective |
   | 6 | **their** village | Adjective | 12 | The doctor helped **us**. | Pronoun |

## Day 4 (Wednesday) — Mixed identify, error-find, Self-try demonstration

**Objective.** Students identify Noun / Verb / Adjective / Pronoun in mixed sentences, find and fix a wrong label, and rehearse the graded Self-try format.

1. **Recall (0–4).** Clue Card visible.

   > "Four classes on our card. **Noun** — names. **Verb** — acts. **Adjective** — describes: *what kind, which, whose*. **Pronoun** — stands instead of a noun. And the boundary: *my, this, his* **before a noun** → adjective; *he, him, them* **alone** → pronoun."
   > "One placement reminder: *what-kind* adjectives can sit before the noun or after *is/are*; *whose* and *which* adjectives sit only before the noun."
   > "Today we mix all four together — exactly like your exam."

2. **Mixed identify (4–12).** Read each aloud; class names the bold word's role, then says **why**:

   `The **tall** tree · **She** helps her mother · My mother **washes** the spoon · **our** school · a **doctor** helped the child · The teacher likes **him**`

   > *tall* → "Adjective — describes the tree." · *She* → "Pronoun — stands instead of a name." · *washes* → "Verb — an action." · *our* → "Adjective — before the noun, tells whose." · *him* → "Pronoun — stands instead of the boy."

   *Anticipated error — student calls *our* a pronoun:* "Where does *our* sit?" → **Before *school*.** → "Before a noun, telling us whose → adjective."

3. **CW-4 out (12–13).** *(CW-4 is on its own sheet.)* "Part A item one — decide the role of the bold word. Name, act, describe, or replace?"

   *Remind the class once more: "On the exam this same question says 'identify the **part of speech**' — it means exactly this, the word's **role**." Say both terms so no child is thrown by the exam wording on Thursday.*

4. **CW-4 Part A, mixed identify (13–20).** Students work; circulate and correct at the desk. Take answers aloud at the end, asking "why?" for each — **especially the my/his boundary items**.

   > *(to anyone stuck)* "Ask the two questions. Does it sit **before a noun**? Then adjective. Does it **stand alone** in a noun's place? Then pronoun."

5. **CW-4 Part B, error-find (20–26). The Analyze step.**

   > "Part B is different. Each sentence already has a label written after it. Some labels are **right** and some are **wrong**. If it is right, write a tick. If it is wrong, cross it out and write the correct role."

   > *(taking answers)* "Number twenty-one — *The tall tree gave shade*, labelled *Noun*. Is *tall* a noun?" → **No — adjective.** → "So we correct it. Good — you are checking the label, not just reading it."

   *Anticipated error — student ticks a wrong label without checking:* "Read the word, then decide the role **yourself** first. Now compare with the label. Do they match? Only then tick."

6. **Self-try demonstration (26–33). Not collected. Not marked.** Rehearsal for Thursday's graded Self-try.

   **Teacher models one first.** Write all three lines on the board as you say them:

   > "Watch me do one. My word is **kind**. Its role — *kind* describes a noun, tells what kind — so **Adjective**. Why: *it describes a noun*. Now my sentence: *A kind boy shared his food.* See — *kind* sits before the noun *boy*, doing an adjective's job."

   **Then students try one.** Each picks a word from the CW-4 box and fills all lines: word · class · why · sentence. Take three or four aloud. Give quick feedback **on the reason line**, not just the sentence.

   > *(feedback model)* "Good sentence. Now your reason — you wrote *'it is a pronoun.'* Tell me **why** it is a pronoun." → *(student:)* "It stands instead of a name." → "That is the reason. Always write the *why*, not just the class."

   > *(close)* "On Thursday you will do this for marks, with **different** words. Today is practice."

7. **HW-4 + Exit Check (33–35).** Distribute HW-4 *(on its own sheet; the task line is printed there)*, then run the Exit Check briskly. **12 prompts.**

   | # | Prompt | Answer | # | Prompt | Answer |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | a **healthy** meal | Adjective | 7 | the **kind** doctor | Adjective |
   | 2 | The children **play**. | Verb | 8 | My uncle **visits** us. | Verb |
   | 3 | The teacher likes **him**. | Pronoun | 9 | a **letter** from home | Noun |
   | 4 | the green **leaf** | Noun | 10 | **this** bird | Adjective |
   | 5 | **my** uncle | Adjective | 11 | Aisha gave it to **me**. | Pronoun |
   | 6 | **They** live here. | Pronoun | 12 | the **strong** boy | Adjective |

## Word-Role Clue Card (`C4B05-CC`) — the Pronoun row is added this week

The Adjective row is already on the card from Block 1. Build the **Pronoun** row and the boundary lines with the class across Days 2–3; the full card should be on the wall by Thursday. *(Also issued as its own sheet for the wall.)*

> ### WHAT ROLE DOES THE WORD PLAY? — **কোন পদ?**
> *(In the exam this is called the **part of speech**. Role and part of speech mean the same thing.)*
>
> | Ask | Role | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | Does it **name** a person, place or thing? *(নাম বোঝায়?)* | **Noun** *(বিশেষ্য)* | teacher · mosque · water |
> | Does it show an **action**? *(কাজ বোঝায়?)* | **Verb** *(ক্রিয়া)* | play · help · write |
> | Does it **describe** a noun — *what kind / which / whose*? *(noun-কে বর্ণনা করে — কেমন / কোনটা / কার?)* | **Adjective** *(বিশেষণ)* | tall · kind · **my** book · **this** tree |
> | Does it **stand instead of** a noun? *(noun-এর বদলে বসে?)* | **Pronoun** *(সর্বনাম)* | he · she · it · **them** · **us** |
>
> **The boundary to remember — মনে রাখার সীমারেখা:**
> *my · his · her · our · their · this · that* **before a noun** are **Adjectives** (they describe *which/whose*).
> *(noun-এর **আগে** বসলে এগুলো **বিশেষণ** — কোনটা / কার তা বলে দেয়।)*
> *he · she · it · me · him · us · them* **standing alone** are **Pronouns** (they replace the noun).
> *(একা বসলে — noun-এর জায়গায় — এগুলো **সর্বনাম**, noun-এর বদলে বসে।)*
>
> **Placement —** what-kind words (*tall, kind*) go **before** the noun or after *is/are*. Which/whose words (*this, my*) go **only before** the noun.
> *(what-kind: noun-এর আগে বা *is/are*-এর পরে; which/whose: শুধু noun-এর আগে।)*

## Performance Test — how to run it (Thursday)

*(The student test is on its own sheet, `C4B05-PT`. Full Marks 36 · 35 minutes. In-school, teacher-administered — the take-home model is retired. A separate weekly Assignment follows the test.)*

**Timing guide —** A 5 min · B 7 min · C 5 min · D 6 min · E 6 min · 6 min checking.

- **Part A — Spelling and Dictation [10].** Say each word **twice**, then read the whole list once more at the end. **The word list is in the Teacher Answer Key (`C4B05-AK`), not on this sheet.**
- **Part B — Identify the part of speech of the bold word [10×1].** The exam format. Write only answers.
- **Part C — Tick or correct the label [6×1].** Some labels are right; do not hint which.
- **Part D — Replace the bold noun with the correct pronoun [6×1].** Full new sentence required.
- **Part E — Self-try [4].** Word box: `tall · kind · my · this · she · them`. **Deliberately different from Wednesday's demonstration box** — a rehearsed sentence does not transfer. The box mixes an adjective (*tall, kind*), a possessive-adjective (*my*), a demonstrative (*this*) and pronouns (*she, them*), so the child must first name the role correctly.

**Self-construction rubric — 4 marks, 1 each.** Each criterion scores **1 or 0** — no partial credit.

| Criterion | What earns it |
|---|---|
| Content accuracy | word used correctly for its role — adjective describes / pronoun replaces |
| Reasoning | line (b) names **why** — *describes a noun* / *stands instead of a noun* |
| Organisation | complete sentence, correct order, readable |
| Correct word-class placement | adjective before its noun (*a tall tree*) or after *is/are* (*the tree is tall*); pronoun sits where the noun would |

**Islamic/value alignment is not scored** — a religiously neutral, on-topic sentence earns full marks.
