## Summary (read this first — 60 seconds)

This is the **Class 5 cut** of the drive, and the heaviest. Class 5 is the **top of the spine**, so it re-walks every lower rung (Word → … → Composition) as fast revision and then secures its own deeper rungs — chiefly the **tense system**, **word formation**, **WH-question production**, and **applied writing** (letter, email, application, paragraph, story). Grammar stays **book-bounded**: every structure is grounded in the NCTB *English for Today* Class 5 book and its Teacher's Guide. Where the school's papers test beyond the book, the item is taught only under a **Principal-locked exam-bridge ruling** (the ledger in §2) — never silently.

The Class 5 book is itself grammar-rich, and the school's papers draw their reading passages straight from it (Garden→U2, Home District→U4, Sundarbans→U7, Wind & Sun→U19, inclusion/Braille→U14), so content alignment is strong. The conflicts were narrow and are now ruled: the **tense ladder** is tiered (**Teach** present perfect; **Bridge** past perfect; **Recognition** present-perfect-continuous; **exam-revision-pack-only** future perfect), **countable/uncountable** and **word formation** are full Teach, **sentence types** and **transformation (two directions only)** are revision/bridge, and **collective nouns** are recognition only.

Three things hold every block together, exactly as in the pilot: practice **reuses words students already hold**; every block **ends in a self-construction (self-try) task** marked on the 4-point rubric; and every block carries the **New Word Routine** — at Class 5 this is **full dual-job fluency + find-the-error** (the top rung of the §N ladder). All examples are authored in-house to Salafi/athari values in the school's house style.

**One convention changes at Class 5 (and Class 4):** the pilot's *present-tense-only* rule for student-facing examples cannot hold once past, future and perfect tenses are taught. From Classes 4–5, **examples may reflect the tense being taught**; simplicity still matters, but **tense authenticity takes precedence** (§10).

The load-bearing sections for building File 3 are **§2 (depth caps + the exam-bridge ruling ledger)** and **§2A (the block-number concordance)**. **§4's week map remains superseded** — see the governance note at the end of §2.

## §1 — What this plan is

The Class 5 grammar track is **remedial and cumulative at the top of the spine**: it works bottom-up, securing the lower rungs before the deeper Class 5 rungs, and revisiting each earlier rung as later ones build on it. "Remedial" describes the bottom-up, gap-closing intent — not that any rung is revised before it has been taught. **First-appearance teaching:** every grammar concept receives its first explicit instruction, to its intended depth (§2 cap), at the point it first appears in the spine; no concept's initial teaching is deferred to a later revision or consolidation block, and the re-walk step (Rule 2) is a bridge for students who already hold the lower rung, not the place a rung is first taught. It is **book-bounded** (Rule 1) — every structure is evidenced in the NCTB *English for Today* Class 5 text and/or its Teacher's Guide. Where the school's four papers test **beyond** the book, the structure is included **only** under the Principal's locked exam-bridge ruling (§2 ledger); it is never taught merely because Mohammadpur tests it (Rule 1 / §J source precedence).

The rungs below are the **Class 5 inventory**, finalised at this build stage from the C5 TG, skeleton and papers exactly as charter §E directs (§E fixes only the Class 3 pilot inventory; Classes 1/2/4/5 are cut at their own build). File 3 builds the blocks **one at a time, in spine order**, each as a self-contained teacher-facing file.

## §5 — Vocabulary reuse base (which words a block may use)

*Provisional — week→block attach pending the calendar resolution (see §2 governance note).* **Graded items** draw **only from words held by that week** — fresh batch to date + already-taught lower-class words + the universal core (the skeleton's 371-word shared core). POS tags in the pool route each word to its matching block (nouns → noun/article blocks; verbs → tense blocks; adjectives → adjective/word-formation; adverbs → adverb block). The front-load logic (nouns early; verbs/adverbs held before the tense and adverb blocks) carries over from the pilot.

## §6 — Bloom application (Class 5)

**Band (REF-18 §4.1).** Class 5 sits at the **top of the Class 3–5 band**: dominant **Apply** and real **Analyze**, with **guided Evaluate/Create** at the free-thinking close. Class 5 can run "why / how / compare / find-the-error" on familiar content as standard, not just exposure.

**Indicative chapter-scope mix (REF-18 §4.2, Classes 3–5):** Remember 20–30% · Understand 25–35% · Apply 25–35% · Analyze 10–20% · Evaluate 0–10% · Create 0–10% — C5 weighting toward the **Apply/Analyze** upper end. These describe the **whole block**, not a single item.

**Climb within each block (Rule 5 / REF-18 ready-check):** open at Remember/Understand (recall/explain the rule), move to Apply (use it in the exam format), reach **Analyze** (sort, compare tenses, transform a sentence, **find the error**), and close at **Create** (the self-try task). Run REF-18 §6 watch-outs before finalising any set — a high-level verb does not make a high-level item; the *mental work* sets the level.

**New Word Routine on the same logic:** Class 5 sits at **Apply–Analyze** — **dual-job fluency across 3–4 classes** and **find-the-error** (§8 / charter §N top rung).

## §8 — New Word Routine staging (Class 5)

**Rung (REF-18 / charter §N):** Class 5 = **dual-job fluency / find-the-error** (top of the ladder) — `Meaning → Class → Use`, now with the same word taking **3–4 different classes** across sentences and a **find-the-error** discrimination task. The clue card is extended beyond noun/verb/adj/adverb to include **preposition and conjunction** clues, to serve the **Block 9** parts-of-speech identification format.

**Dual-job at Class 5 (full).** The genuinely harder adjective/adverb dual-jobs held back at Class 3 — **hard, still, open** (and the noun↔verb core: water, plant, brush, drink, help, turn, start, cut, call, play, place, step) — are now **drilled to fluency**, with minimal-pair sets ("same word, two jobs — classify each") and **error-spotting** items ("this word is tagged Verb here — is that right?"). This is the transferable engine that lets students handle words beyond the memorised pool on the exam's identify-and-name items.

**Where it sits in the blocks:** Block 1 establishes fluency; **Block 9 (Parts of speech)** is where it is most heavily exercised against the exam format; every later block carries a few transfer items in its self-try close.

## §9 — Free-thinking close + marking rubric

Every block ends with a **self-construction (self-try) task** (charter §I): the student writes their own sentence/example (or, in the writing blocks, a short paragraph/letter) using the block's rule **and** a word from the current set, after first **naming the word's class**. Frame it anchored, never open-ended — e.g. *"Write your own sentence in the **present perfect** about a halal daily habit, then name the tense and the verb's class"* or *"Write one **optative** sentence as a du'ā using a word you learned this week."*

**Rubric** = Charter §I.3 — **4 marks, four criteria at 1 mark each, uniform across all five classes**. Islamic/value alignment is **not** a scored criterion (Charter §I.3, Run Book §13.1): §H binds all authored material in full, but a religiously neutral on-topic student answer earns full marks, and a §H conflict is flagged as a values matter rather than marked down. Each criterion scores 1 or 0; no partial credit.

| Criterion | Marks |
|---|---|
| Content accuracy (rule used correctly) | 1 |
| Reasoning (why it fits the rule / type / tense) | 1 |
| Organisation | 1 |
| **Correct word-class placement** *(charter §N)* | 1 |
| **Total** | **4** |

*Scaling note:* read the rubric **proportionally** to the task — a one-sentence self-try is not marked like a paragraph. At Class 5 the writing-block self-try (paragraph, letter, story) carries the full criteria; sentence-level closes are read for the criteria, not the raw total. Sentence-variety and reasoning carry more weight here than at the lower classes, in line with the C5 Bloom band.

## §10 — Islamic alignment & house style

All content is **authored in-house** to Salafi/athari values (charter §H): tawhid-centred; no shirk/bid'ah-adjacent content; no other-faith festival/holiday content; no music/instruments (choral recitation, spoken repetition, du'ā/dhikr phrasing substitute for songs/rhymes); modest and akhlaq-positive throughout.

**House style — confirmed from the school's own papers** (mirror it in every block):
- **Islamic register is natural and strong at C5:** Salah, Zakat, Hajj, Ramadan, Suhoor, the Seerah, the Quran, "obeying Allah," *Bismillah / SubhanAllah / Alhamdulillah / in-sha-Allah*, and du'ā phrasing for the optative ("May Allah bless you with patience"). The papers model this heavily — keep it.
- **Names (house style):** Nusair, Yusuf, Abdullah, Abdur Rahim (male); Aisha, Raima, Porshi, Rabab, Jesmin Akter (female). The C5 papers also use Abdullah, Maryam, Bilal, Amina, Fahim, Abrar — all house-consistent. **Authored practice items use house-style names**; where a reading passage is drawn from the NCTB text it may carry the book's names, but our practice/self-try items are re-authored to house style.
- **Themes:** worship and akhlaq, family and neighbours, cleanliness and care for creation, study and school, national heritage (Sundarbans, Nakshi Kantha) — matching the book's units and the papers.

**Non-mahram pairing screen (required before every delivery).** All dialogue pairs and letter sender/recipient pairs must be **same-gender or mahram**. The papers already model this (Abdullah → Bilal; Maryam → Amina); keep letter and dialogue tasks same-gender.

**Curation already applied at source (do not reintroduce):** poems (Units 10 & 15) are treated as **recitation, not song** — no melody/performance; the Experimental-edition full-colour figures get the **faceless/illustration rule** at Project 03; Unit 6 "An Email from Indonesia" is reviewed for framing only (the language objective, 7.3 correspondence, is neutral). The Quran and sacred items are **not** used as graded test targets in exercises.

**Tense authenticity (C4–5 convention, §2 note):** examples may carry the tense being taught; the present-only rule is a Classes 1–3 convention. Keep sentences simple, but do not distort tense to stay in the present.
