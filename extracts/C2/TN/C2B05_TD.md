## Teacher checklist

- ☐ Teach only to the §2 cap — write the plural; regular **-s / -es / -y→-ies** plus the taught changers. **No -f→-ves** (Class 3). **No countable/uncountable rules.**
- ☐ Re-walk "naming word" (Blocks 1–2) fast before the block's own work (Rule 2) — a bridge, not re-teaching.
- ☐ Teach the term **plural** (with *singular* as its partner) — the proper name for "more than one." Taught and recalled, **not** a graded blank.
- ☐ Produce → name → rule at every step: child says the plural from "one ___, two ___?" *before* the rule is named. Go slow.
- ☐ Graded items use **held words** or the declared **block-local set** (class, watch, box, dress, glass) only — nothing graded before its teaching day.
- ☐ **-es** graded on the block-local set only. ***day*** taught as the oral vowel-y trap; graded only in choose-format, never produced from a blank.
- ☐ **Cue words** *some / many* taught (Tuesday Step 1) before they appear on any worksheet.
- ☐ Each day closes with its **Exit Check** — one prompt per student (confirm the Sylhet Class 2 roll, 14), 2–5 min, oral, day's target only.
- ☐ Wednesday: **no new grammar** — revision, choose-and-rewrite practice, plural self-try, mixed Exit Check. Tell the class the Thursday PT covers **both Gender and Plural**.
- ☐ Values: **no living-being imagery** (word-cards, not pictures), non-mahram screen, sacred-word guard (§H.3 — *Allah/Quran* never a plural target), **attribution screen** (no natural phenomenon as an autonomous giver).
- ☐ All student-facing worksheets/tests **present tense, verb-s-free** (first-person / plural subject); house-style names rotated.

# Monday — First Teaching: singular & plural · the term *plural* · the base rule

**Day 1 of 2 teaching days** · 35 minutes · English throughout · Clue Card issued today.
**Term strand today:** introduce **singular** and **plural** — first exposure.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Re-walk: what is a naming word? |
| 2 | 5–10 | The idea: one, and more than one |
| 3 | 10–14 | **Term: singular / plural** |
| 4 | 14–20 | The rule: most words add **s** |
| 5 | 20–26 | Words ending s, sh, ch, x → **es** |
| 6 | 26–30 | One changer: tooth → teeth · CW-1 out |
| 7 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Re-walk: what is a naming word? (0–5 min)

**Books closed.** Write: `book · bird · tree · bag · hen`

> "From Blocks 1 and 2 — what do we call these words?" → **Naming words. Nouns.**
> "A naming word names a person, an animal, a place, or a thing. Say it with me — *a naming word names something.*"
> "Today we look at naming words when there is **one**, and when there are **many**."

### Step 2 — The idea: one, and more than one (5–10 min)

Hold up one book, then several.

> "How many books is this?" *(one)* → **One.** &emsp; "And now?" *(several)* → **Many.**

Write two columns, no headings yet:

```
one              more than one
book             books
bird             birds
tree             trees
```

> "This side is **one** thing. This side is **more than one.**"
> "Listen to the naming word when there are many — *book… books.* What did you hear at the end?" → **An 's' sound.**

*The concept before the label — the child hears the change before naming it.*

### Step 3 — The term: singular / plural (10–14 min)

Head the columns.

| **Singular** *(one)* | **Plural** *(more than one)* |
|---|---|
| book | books |
| bird | birds |
| tree | trees |

> "One thing has a name: **singular.** Say it — *sin·gu·lar.*"
> "More than one has a name: **plural.** Say it — *plu·ral.*"
> "When we mean more than one, we say the **plural**. I give you one, you give me the plural — the *more-than-one* word."

**Term practice (~2 min):** choral read ×3 (*singular … plural*); teacher board-writes **plural**, class reads it back; post the Clue Card, read row 1 together.

> "Look at this word well today — **plural.**"

*First exposure — no recall demanded yet; Tuesday recalls it.*

**Teacher note.** *Plural* is the proper name for "more than one," so the child should own the word itself, not just the ending. *Singular* is taught as its partner so *plural* has a clear opposite. Keep the work on "give me the plural" — no need to drill "write the singular."

### Step 4 — The rule: most words add **s** (14–20 min)

Books closed. Go slowly — say the plural first, then name it, then the rule.

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "One book, two —?" | books |
| "One cat, five —?" | cats |
| "One tree, three —?" | trees |
| "One bird, many —?" | birds |
| "So the plural of *book* is —?" | books |
| "The plural of *cat* is —?" | cats |
| "Listen to the end — book, book**s.** What sound did we add?" | s |
| "So for most words, to make the plural, we add —?" | s |

Clue Card, row 1. **Held words for writing:** book, bird, tree, cat, stone, pebble, animal, hen, parrot, frog.

### Step 5 — Words ending s, sh, ch, x → **es** (20–26 min)

Word-cards, one at a time. Let them try the plural first.

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "One class, two —?" | *(may try 'class-s')* |
| "Listen — *class… classes.* One class, two —?" | classes |
| "One box, three —?" | boxes |
| "One watch, two —?" | watches |
| "So the plural of *class* is —?" | classes |
| "Say *class* with only *s* — *class-s.* Can you say it easily?" | No. |
| "That is why we add **es** — *classes.* Now you can hear it." | — |
| "Look at the last letter. Is it s, sh, ch, or x? Then we add —?" | es |

Clue Card, row 2. *(Block-local set: class, watch, box, dress, glass — gradeable in-block.)*

### Step 6 — One changer + CW-1 out (26–30 min)

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "One tooth, two —?" | *(may try 'tooths')* |
| "Listen — *tooth… teeth.* One tooth, two —?" | teeth |
| "So the plural of *tooth* is —?" | teeth |
| "Did we add *s*? No — the word changed inside. Some words we just remember." | — |

Clue Card, changers row. Distribute **CW-1** (see `C2B05-CW1`); model item 1 of each part, **including one singular** — *"I have one pen"* → *pen*, not *pens* — so the child sees "one" means leave it alone.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (30–35 min)

One prompt per student. Teacher says the singular; student says **and spells** the plural. *(Sized for 14 — confirm roll.)*

| # | Prompt | Answer | | # | Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | book | books | | 8 | frog | frogs |
| 2 | bird | birds | | 9 | animal | animals |
| 3 | tree | trees | | 10 | pebble | pebbles |
| 4 | cat | cats | | 11 | stone | stones |
| 5 | hen | hens | | 12 | tooth | teeth |
| 6 | parrot | parrots | | 13 | bird | birds |
| 7 | cat | cats | | 14 | book | books |

> **HW-1 out at end of class.** See `C2B05-HW1`.

# Tuesday — Plural completed: the **-y** contrast + changers

**Day 2 of 2 teaching days** · 35 minutes · English throughout.
**Term strand today:** recall **plural**; no new term.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Recall Monday + **cue words: some / many** |
| 2 | 5–8 | Term recall — plural (partial spelling) |
| 3 | 8–17 | The y-rule: consonant before y → **ies** |
| 4 | 17–24 | The y-trap: a/e/i/o/u before y → just **s** |
| 5 | 24–27 | Changers: child → children · *sheep* stays same |
| 6 | 27–30 | CW-2 out |
| 7 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall Monday + cue words (0–5 min)

**Books closed.** Rapid oral round — teacher says singular, class gives plural: `book · class · tree · box · hen · watch · frog · glass`

> "When we mean more than one, what do we call it?" → **The plural.**
> "Most words — what do we add?" → **s.** "Words ending s, sh, ch, x?" → **es.**

**Cue words (~1 min):**

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "Sometimes a number tells us to use the plural — *two books, three hens.*" | — |
| "Two more words also tell us **more than one** — **some** and **many.**" | — |
| "*Some birds* — one, or more than one?" | more than one |
| "*Many boys* — one, or more?" | more → boys |
| "So **some** and **many** mean plural too — add the ending, like a number." | — |

### Step 2 — Term recall: plural (5–8 min)

**Books closed.** On the board, partial spelling: `plu____`

> "Finish the word — the name for *more than one.*" → **plural.**

Teacher completes it; class reads it back.

### Step 3 — The y-rule: consonant before y → **ies** (8–17 min)

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "One city, two —?" | *(may try 'citys')* |
| "Listen — *city… cities.* One city, two —?" | cities |
| "One story, three —?" | stories |
| "One family, two —?" | families |
| "So the plural of *city* is —?" | cities |

Write on the board: **cit y → cit ies**

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "Look at the letter just before the *y* in *city* — what is it?" | t |
| "Is *t* one of a, e, i, o, u?" | No. |
| "When the letter before *y* is not a, e, i, o, u — the *y* changes to **ies.**" | — |

Clue Card, y-row. **Held words:** city, story, family.

### Step 4 — The y-trap: a/e/i/o/u before y → just **s** (17–24 min)

Do it slowly — this catches children who over-apply the y-rule.

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "One day, two —?" | *(some say 'daies' — expected)* |
| "Listen — *day… days.* One day, two —?" | days |
| "One boy, three —?" | boys |
| "One monkey, two —?" | monkeys |

Put the two side by side: **cit y → cities** &emsp; **da y → days**

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "In *city*, the letter before y is *t* — not a, e, i, o, u. So y → **ies.**" | — |
| "In *day*, the letter before y is *a* — one of a, e, i, o, u! So we just add **s.**" | — |
| "*Monkey* — letter before y?" | e → just add s → monkeys |
| "*Story* — letter before y?" | r → not a, e, i, o, u → stories |

Clue Card, vowel-y note. **Held words:** boy, monkey. *(day taught as the trap; not a graded production target.)*

### Step 5 — Changers + *sheep* (24–27 min)

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "One child, two —?" | *(may try 'childs')* |
| "Listen — *child… children.* One child, two —?" | children |
| "One sheep, two —?" | *(some say 'sheeps')* |
| "Listen — one sheep, two **sheep.** It does not change at all." | sheep |
| "A few words stay the same. We remember these too." | — |

Clue Card, changers + "no change" rows: *tooth → teeth · child → children · sheep → sheep.*

### Step 6 — CW-2 out (27–30 min)

Distribute **CW-2** (see `C2B05-CW2`); model item 1 of each part. On the board, model one vowel-y (*day → days*) and one consonant-y (*city → cities*) side by side so the child sees the check: **look at the letter before the y before you write.**

### Step 7 — Exit Check (30–35 min)

One prompt per student. Teacher says singular; student says **and spells** the plural. *(Sized for 14.)*

| # | Prompt | Answer | | # | Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | city | cities | | 8 | family | families |
| 2 | day | days | | 9 | boy | boys |
| 3 | story | stories | | 10 | child | children |
| 4 | family | families | | 11 | city | cities |
| 5 | monkey | monkeys | | 12 | sheep | sheep |
| 6 | boy | boys | | 13 | story | stories |
| 7 | boy | boys | | 14 | day | days |

> **HW-2 out at end of class.** See `C2B05-HW2`.

# Wednesday — Consolidation (both strands) · no new grammar

### Step 1 — Gender revision (0–5 min) — oral, no new teaching

> **Teacher note — tell the class:** "Tomorrow's Performance Test covers **both** what we learned this week — **gender** (masculine, feminine, common) **and plurals** (one → many). Today we go over both, so revise both at home tonight."

Rapid oral round — teacher says a noun, class gives the gender: `father · mother · king · queen · boy · girl · friend · teacher`

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "*father* — masculine or feminine?" | masculine |
| "*queen*?" | feminine |
| "*friend* — can it be a boy or a girl?" | both — common |
| "The three genders are —?" | masculine, feminine, common |

Post the gender sub-card beside the plural Clue Card. Both stay up for Thursday. **Revision only — do not re-teach.**

### Step 2 — Plural revision (5–12 min)

Books closed. Walk the Clue Card top to bottom, produce-first.

| Teacher says | Expected answer |
|---|---|
| "Most words — one book, two —?" | books · *add s* |
| "Ends in s, sh, ch, x — one glass, two —?" | glasses · *add es* |
| "Ends in y, letter before not a,e,i,o,u — one city, two —?" | cities · *y → ies* |
| "Ends in y, letter before is a,e,i,o,u — one day, two —?" | days · *just add s* |
| "Changers — tooth? child? sheep?" | teeth · children · sheep (no change) |

Then: "*story* — which rule?" → *r before y → ies → stories* · "*boy* — which rule?" → *o before y → just s → boys*

### Step 3 — Exam-format practice: choose-and-rewrite (12–26 min)

**First time this format is taught** — model, then students practise on CW-3 Part B in class.

**Model (12–17):**
> *"I drink two (glass / glasses) of water."* — "How many? *Two* — more than one. Which word? *glasses.* Now write the **whole** sentence." → *I drink two glasses of water.*
> Then a singular: *"I have one (box / boxes)."* → *one* → *box* → *I have one box.*

Emphasise: **choose the right word, then copy the whole sentence.** Marks are for the correct choice; neat copying is expected.

**Student practice (17–26):** class works CW-3 Part B (see `C2B05-CW3`) while the teacher circulates, picking up y-contrast and number-reading errors one-to-one as they appear.

### Step 4 — Plural self-try (26–31 min) — 4-mark rubric

The block's mandatory self-construction close. Anchored, not open.

> **Write your own sentence about your home using a naming word that means more than one. Then: name the word's job, say why in one sentence, and circle the plural word.**

Teacher models one: *"I have three books."* → job: **naming word** · why: *"it names things"* · circle **books**.

Started in class; completed for submission. **Separate from Sunday's gender self-try.**

**Self-construction rubric — 4 marks (1 each):**

| Criterion | 1 mark for |
|---|---|
| Content accuracy | The sentence is correct and uses a genuine plural noun |
| Reasoning | One sentence — language or mixed — tied to the word-job named |
| Organisation | Sentence written cleanly; word-job label present |
| Word-job placement | The plural word is circled and labelled *naming word* |

*Value alignment not scored.*

# Thursday — Performance Test: Gender + Plural (W4)

**In-school, teacher-administered · English only · no hints · one sitting.**

Student paper: `C2B05-PT`. Answer key and dictation word list live in the teacher-only `C2B05-AK`.

## PT instructions (procedure)

1. **Distribute** the PT paper. Name and Date fields on top. No talking during the test.
2. **Part A — Spelling & Dictation [10].** Read each word twice, clearly, at a natural pace. Do **not** show the word. Do **not** repeat after the second reading. The word list is in the teacher answer key (`C2B05-AK`) — never on the student paper.
3. **Part B — Write the plural [8].** Students work independently. No prompting on the rules.
4. **Part C — Choose the correct word and write the whole sentence [6].** Remind (once, at the start of the Part) that they must **copy the whole sentence**, not just the chosen word.
5. **Part D — Write the feminine or masculine [6].** Independent work.
6. **Part E — Write names of 5 common gender [5].** Students choose from the taught common set. Spelling is graded.
7. **Part F — Self-try [4].** Read the instruction aloud once. Then no further prompting. Marked on the **4-mark rubric** (see Wednesday Step 4). Value alignment not scored.
8. **Collect** at the end of the sitting. Take-home extension is not permitted.
9. **Marking** uses the answer key `C2B05-AK`. Do not return marked papers before Sunday.

**Total: 39 marks** (10 + 8 + 6 + 6 + 5 + 4).
