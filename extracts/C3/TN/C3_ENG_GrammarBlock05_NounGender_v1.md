## The four gender terms — taught, not graded (Principal ruling, 2026-07-25)

*Masculine · Feminine · Common · Neuter* are **taught** across the week so students recognise, pronounce, and become familiar with them — but they are **not a graded element** on any worksheet or the PT. (This supersedes an earlier session ruling that made the terms graded spelling objectives.)

- **Metalanguage, not vocabulary.** The four terms sit outside File 2 (grammar terminology, not naming/doing/describing words) and outside the block-local 20 (those are example *nouns*; these are category *names*).
- **In-class term practice every teaching day**, written into the Lesson Plan as a named segment (like the Exit Check): choral reading, syllable-breaking, board-writing, and oral recall. This is teaching practice, not a graded task.
- **No term appears as a graded item** on worksheets or the PT. Where the day scripts show board recall (e.g. *Mascu____* completion in Day 2), that is in-class practice only.
- **Spelling-difficulty note (for teaching emphasis).** *Common* is phonically easy; *neuter* (eu), *feminine* (double-i pattern), *masculine* (-line ending) are the harder three — give them more board time.

# Class 1 (Sunday) — What gender is · Masculine & Feminine

**Objective.** Students learn that some naming words tell us **male** or **female**, name the two genders *masculine* and *feminine*, and match the common counterpart pairs. They begin learning to spell **masculine** and **feminine**.

**New words today:** `uncle · aunt · king · queen · boy · sister` (with held *father, mother, brother, girl, man, hen*)
**Term strand today:** introduce **masculine** + **feminine** (first exposure — no recall yet)

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Re-walk: what is a noun? |
| 2 | 5–9 | The new idea: male and female |
| 3 | 9–18 | Masculine and feminine + the pairs |
| 4 | 18–23 | **Term practice — masculine / feminine** (3–5 min) |
| 5 | 23–28 | Sorting drill |
| 6 | 28–30 | CW-1 distribution |
| 7 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Re-walk: what is a noun? (0–5 min)

**Books closed.** Write: `father · house · hen · book · girl`

> "From Blocks 3 and 4 — what do we call these words?" → **Naming words. Nouns.**
> "A noun names a person, an animal, a place, or a thing. Say it with me — *a noun is a naming word.*"

> "Today we look closer at naming words for **people and animals**. Some of them tell us something special — whether we mean a **male** or a **female**."

### Step 2 — The new idea: male and female (5–9 min)

Write two columns, no headings yet:

```
father        mother
brother       sister
boy           girl
```

> "Look at this side — *father, brother, boy.* Are these male or female?" → **Male.**
> "And this side — *mother, sister, girl?*" → **Female.**

> "Every naming word for a person or animal can tell us **male** or **female**. We call this the **gender** of the noun. Gender just means: is it male, or female?"

Write **gender** on the board and leave it up.

### Step 3 — Masculine and feminine + the pairs (9–18 min)

Now head the columns:

| **Masculine** *(male)* | **Feminine** *(female)* |
|---|---|
| father | mother |
| brother | sister |
| uncle | aunt |
| boy | girl |
| king | queen |
| cock | hen |

> "The male gender has a name: **masculine.** Say it — *mas·cu·line.*"
> "The female gender has a name: **feminine.** Say it — *fem·i·nine.*"

Teach the new words with the held anchors:

> "You already know *father, brother, boy.* Here are their partners' partners — *uncle* is your father's brother, a man, so **masculine**. *Aunt* is your mother's sister, a woman, so **feminine**. A *king* is a man who rules — masculine. A *queen* is a woman who rules — feminine."

Say each pair aloud twice, class echoing. Emphasise: **the pairs go together** — every masculine word here has a feminine partner.

**Teacher note.** The pairs are the *scaffold* for the concept. The graded skill later is naming a single noun's gender, **not** converting one word to the other. Do not drill conversion as if the exam tests it — it does not.

### Step 4 — Term practice: masculine / feminine (18–23 min)

**The first term-strand segment. 3–5 minutes, explicit.**

1. **Choral read** ×3: *masculine … feminine.*
2. **Syllable-break**: *mas·cu·line* (3) · *fem·i·nine* (3).
3. **Board-write** — teacher writes *masculine*, underlines *-cu-* and *-line*; writes *feminine*, underlines the two *i*'s.
4. Post the **Clue Card** rows.

> "Look at these words well today."

*No recall yet — first exposure.*

### Step 5 — Sorting drill (23–28 min)

Books closed. Teacher says a noun; class answers **masculine** or **feminine**, chorally then individuals:

`father · mother · king · girl · uncle · queen · brother · aunt · boy · hen · sister · man`

Then reverse — one deliberately wrong:

> "*Aunt is masculine* — right or wrong? Fix it." → **Wrong. Aunt is feminine.**

### Step 6 — CW-1 (28–30 min)

Distribute CW-1. Model item 1 of **each part** on the board (§9.3) — students often confuse Part B and Part C, writing the *partner* (mother) beside father in Part B instead of the *gender* (masculine). Show explicitly: **Part B asks for the gender word; Part C asks for the partner word.** Students continue as far as time allows; unfinished CW goes home with HW-1.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (30–35 min)

Teacher says the noun; one student per prompt gives the gender.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | uncle | masculine | | 10 | father | masculine |
| 2 | sister | feminine | | 11 | hen | feminine |

| 4 | king | masculine | | 13 | man | masculine |

# Class 2 (Monday) — **Common** gender

**Objective.** Students learn that some naming words can be **male or female** — the **common** gender — and tell common apart from masculine and feminine. They recall *masculine / feminine* from memory and begin learning **common**.

**New words today:** `teacher · student · child · cousin · baby` (with held *friend, neighbour*)
**Term strand today:** recall **masculine / feminine**; introduce **common**

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Day 1 |
| 2 | 4–7 | **Term recall — masculine / feminine** (partial spelling) |
| 3 | 7–16 | Discover common gender |
| 4 | 16–21 | **Term practice — common** |
| 5 | 21–25 | Three-way sorting drill |
| 6 | 25–31 | CW-2 distribution |
| 7 | 31–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall Day 1 (0–4 min)

Books closed. Rapid oral round — students give **masculine** or **feminine**:

`uncle · aunt · king · queen · boy · girl · brother · sister`

> "What does gender tell us about a naming word?" → **Whether it is male or female.**

### Step 2 — Term recall: masculine / feminine (4–7 min)

**First recall — the terms were drilled a full day.** On the board, partial spellings:

```
Mascu______        Fem______
```

> "Fill in the rest — no peeking. What are the two genders we learned yesterday?"

Students write the terms, then two come to the board. Fix spelling openly: *mas·cu·line*, *fem·i·nine*.

### Step 3 — Discover common gender (7–16 min)

Write and read:

```
The student is reading.
```

> "*Student.* Is a student a boy, or a girl?" → *Could be either.*
> "Right — a student can be a **boy** or a **girl**. The word does not tell us. So *student* is not masculine, and not feminine."

> "Words like this have their own gender: **common.** A **common** gender noun can be **male or female** — the word does not say which."

Build the set with held + new words:

| Common — male **or** female |
|---|
| friend · neighbour |
| teacher · student |
| child · cousin · baby |

> "A *teacher* — man or woman? Either. A *student*? Either. A *baby*? We may not even know yet. A *cousin* — your uncle's son **or** daughter. All **common** gender."

**Teacher note (§2 guardrail).** Keep to "can it be male or female?" Do **not** open the common-vs-neuter edge cases (e.g. animals, ships). If a child asks whether *cat* is common, answer simply: "We can say it either way, but for our work, if it is a **person** who could be a boy or a girl, that is common. A **thing** is our next lesson." Hold the line.

### Step 4 — Term practice: common (16–21 min)

**3–5 min, explicit.**

1. **Choral read** ×3: *common.*
2. **Syllable-break**: *com·mon* (2).
3. **Board-write** — teacher writes *common*, points out the double *m*.
4. **Definition→term match** on the two *known* terms only (masculine, feminine):
   > "Which term means *male*? Which means *female*?" — students write the word, not copy it.
5. Post the **Clue Card** common row.

### Step 5 — Three-way sorting drill (21–25 min)

Books closed. Teacher says a noun; class answers **masculine**, **feminine**, or **common**:

`father · friend · queen · teacher · boy · baby · aunt · student · uncle · child · hen · cousin · sister · neighbour`

Reverse, one wrong:

> "*Teacher is masculine* — right or wrong?" → **Wrong. A teacher can be male or female — common.**

### Step 6 — CW-2 (25–31 min)

Distribute CW-2. Model item 1 (§9.3). Unfinished CW goes home with HW-2.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (31–35 min)

| # | Prompt | Answer | | # | Prompt | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | brother | masculine | | 10 | father | masculine |
| 2 | baby | common | | 11 | child | common |
| 3 | mother | feminine | | 12 | uncle | masculine |
| 4 | boy | masculine | | 13 | aunt | feminine |
| 5 | king | masculine | | 14 | neighbour | common |
| 6 | student | common | | 15 | teacher | common |
| 7 | cousin | common | | 16 | queen | feminine |
| 8 | hen | feminine | | 17 | sister | feminine |
| 9 | girl | feminine | | | | |

# Class 3 (Tuesday) — **Neuter** gender

**Objective.** Students learn that naming words for **things** — not male, not female — have the **neuter** gender, and tell neuter apart from the other three. They recall *masculine / feminine / common* and begin learning **neuter**.

**New words today:** `table · chair · mirror · egg · cake · wood · class` (with held *house, book, pen, bag, garden, roof* + many more)
**Term strand today:** recall **masculine / feminine / common**; introduce **neuter** — all four now in rotation

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Days 1–2 |
| 2 | 4–8 | **Term recall — common / neuter build** (partial spelling) |
| 3 | 8–17 | Discover neuter gender |
| 4 | 17–22 | **Term practice — all four** |
| 5 | 22–26 | Four-way sorting drill |
| 6 | 26–31 | Find-in-sentence + CW-3 |
| 7 | 31–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall Days 1–2 (0–4 min)

Books closed. Rapid round — **masculine / feminine / common**:

`uncle · friend · queen · teacher · boy · baby · aunt · cousin`

### Step 2 — Term recall (4–8 min)

Partial spellings on the board:

```
Fem______        Comm____
```

> "Write the term — no peeking."

Then preview: `Neu____` — "This one is new today. Watch the spelling: **n-e-u-t-e-r**."

### Step 3 — Discover neuter gender (8–17 min)

Write:

```
The table is brown.
```

> "*Table.* Is a table a boy? A girl?" → *Neither — it is a thing.*
> "A table is not male and not female. It is a **thing.** Naming words for things have the gender **neuter.**"

Build with held + new:

| Neuter — a thing, not male or female |
|---|
| house · book · pen · bag · garden · roof |
| table · chair · mirror · egg · cake · wood · class |

> "*Chair, mirror, egg, cake* — all things. None is male or female. All **neuter.**"

Contrast, to fix the boundary (§2 cap — no theory beyond this):

> "*Teacher* — a person, could be male or female → **common.** *Chair* — a thing, not male or female → **neuter.** A person who could be either is common. A thing is neuter."

### Step 4 — Term practice: all four (17–22 min)

**3–5 min, explicit — the fullest term segment.**

1. **Choral read** ×2 of all four: *masculine · feminine · common · neuter.*
2. **Syllable-break**: *neu·ter* (2). Contrast the tricky *eu* with a clear board underline.
3. **Recall write** — teacher gives the *meaning*, students write the *term* from memory:
   > "Write the gender for a **thing**." → *neuter*
   > "Write the gender for a word that is **male or female**." → *common*
4. All four Clue Card rows now posted.

### Step 5 — Four-way sorting drill (22–26 min)

Four-way oral sort — teacher says a noun; class answers **masculine**, **feminine**, **common**, or **neuter**:

`father · table · friend · queen · book · teacher · egg · aunt · chair · cousin · king · mirror`

### Step 6 — Find-in-sentence + CW-3 (26–31 min)

The **exam-format move**, worked as the model for CW-3 — find the noun in a sentence, name its gender (held/taught words only):

| Sentence | Noun | Gender |
|---|---|---|
| The boy has a bag. | boy | masculine |
| My sister reads a book. | sister | feminine |
| The teacher helps us. | teacher | common |
| The table is brown. | table | neuter |

> "This is exactly what your test will ask: find the naming word, then write its gender."

Tell the class: **some sentences have one naming word, some have two. Find them all, then write the gender of each.** This is the model for CW-3 item 1 (§9.3). Distribute CW-3; students continue as far as time allows. Unfinished CW goes home with HW-3.

### Step 7 — Exit Check (31–35 min)

| 5 | boy | masculine | | 14 | egg | neuter |
| 6 | mother | feminine | | 15 | cousin | common |

# Class 4 (Wednesday) — Mixed practice, find-in-sentence, Self-try demonstration

**Objective.** Students name all four genders in mixed exam format, find nouns in sentences and write their gender, and rehearse the Self-try format they will be graded on Thursday.

**Term strand today:** **all four from memory** — full recall

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall all four genders |
| 2 | 4–9 | **Term recall — all four, from memory** |
| 3 | 9–17 | Find-the-noun, write-the-gender (exam format) |
| 4 | 17–22 | Mixed sort + fix-the-error |
| 5 | 22–31 | Self-try demonstration |
| 6 | 31–33 | CW-4 / HW-4 distribution |
| 7 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall all four (0–4 min)

Books closed. Rapid four-way round:

`uncle · table · friend · queen · egg · teacher · boy · baby · chair · aunt · cousin · mirror`

### Step 2 — Term recall: all four from memory (4–9 min)

Teacher gives meaning, students **write the term**:

1. Gender for a **male** person or animal → *masculine*
2. Gender for a **female** person or animal → *feminine*
3. Gender for a word that is **male or female** → *common*
4. Gender for a **thing** → *neuter*

Two students spell each at the board; class self-corrects.

### Step 3 — Find-the-noun, write-the-gender (9–17 min)

The exam format, worked together on the board:

| Sentence | Noun(s) | Gender |
|---|---|---|
| The hen sits on the egg. | hen / egg | feminine / neuter |
| My friend gave me a mirror. | friend / mirror | common / neuter |
| The king and the queen smiled. | king / queen | masculine / feminine |
| The teacher wrote on the board. | teacher / board | common / neuter |

> "First find the naming word. Then ask the Clue Card question: male? female? either? or a thing?"

### Step 4 — Mixed sort + fix-the-error (17–22 min)

Fix-the-error (reaches simple Analyze):

> On the board: *"Aunt is masculine." · "Table is common." · "Boy is feminine."*
> "Each one is wrong. Fix it and say why."
> → *aunt is feminine (female)* · *table is neuter (a thing)* · *boy is masculine (male).*

### Step 5 — Self-try demonstration (22–31 min)

**Not collected. Not marked.** Rehearsal for Thursday's graded Self-try.

Model the format on the board with the class:

> **a)** Your word must be a **common** noun. My choice: *teacher.*
> **b)** Its gender is *common* — because a teacher can be a **man or a woman**.
> **c)** My sentence: *The teacher helps us.*
> **d)** Now a **neuter** noun: *The table is brown.* Gender: *neuter.*

> "Tomorrow you do this on your own, for marks. You will be told which gender to use — you choose the word and write the sentence. Your reason must say **why** — not just the gender, but how you know."

**Teacher note.** C3 reasoning is *evaluated* (PD-010) — "because a teacher can be a man or a woman" earns the reasoning mark; "because it is common" (restating the answer) does not. Model the difference aloud.

### Step 6 — CW-4 / HW-4 (31–33 min)

Distribute CW-4 (in-class) and HW-4 (home). Tell the class: **some sentences have one naming word, some have two. Find them all, then write the gender of each.** Task lines are on the sheets.
