## Learning outcomes (plain form)

- Understand that *this / that / these / those* are pointing words that point at naming words (nouns).
- Choose the correct pointing word from near/far (this·these = near, that·those = far) and one/many (this·that = one, these·those = many).
- Produce the four naming-frame sentences from a pointed picture: *This is a ___. / That is a ___. / These are ___. / Those are ___.*
- Read the picture cue (near = large/foreground, far = small/background; one = single, many = group) and write the matching sentence.
- Write one pointing sentence of their own (guided self-try).

## Clue Card content (build with the class Sun–Tue; on the wall by Thursday)

> **This · That · These · Those are pointing words.**
> They point at a naming word (noun) — like *book, tree, house.*
>
> **কাছে (near) → this / these · দূরে (far) → that / those**
> **একটি (one) → this / that · অনেক (many) → these / those**

| | one (একটি) | many (অনেক) |
|---|---|---|
| **near (কাছে)** | this | these |
| **far (দূরে)** | that | those |

**Our four sentences:** This is a book. · That is a house. · These are pens. · Those are trees.

*How to read the chart:* ask two questions — **near or far?** and **one or many?** — then choose. near + one = this · far + one = that · near + many = these · far + many = those.

*Optional teacher note:* Give the Bangla meaning aloud only when a child cannot follow the English. Show near/far with your hand, not with words on the sheet — the child must learn to read the distance, because the worksheet picture carries it with no words.

# Sunday — First Teaching: near/far · this/that (one object)

**Vocabulary today:** the pointing words *this* and *that*; near/far. All frame nouns are held (W1–W4).

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine (never cut) |
| 2 | 5–9 | Re-walk: what is a naming word? what is a describing word? (fast bridge) |
| 3 | 9–18 | Introduce **near/far** with **this/that**; teacher points at real classroom objects |
| 4 | 18–23 | Model the frames *This is a ___* / *That is a ___* on the board |
| 5 | 23–33 | **CW-1 done in class** (~10 min) |
| 6 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

**Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)**

The fixed oral opener (never cut). Run it as every session.

**Step 2 — Re-walk: naming word + describing word (5–9 min)**

Fast bridge, not re-teaching. Keep it under four minutes — this only wakes up the nouns the demonstrative frames will point at.

**Say (word-for-word):**
> *"Before we start, two quick questions. A **naming word** — what does it do? It names a thing. Give me one."*
> *(Take three or four: book · pen · tree · cat · house.)*
> *"Good. And a **describing word** — from last week — tells us how a thing is. Give me one."*
> *(Take two or three: big · small · clean.)*
> *"Today we learn a new kind of word — a **pointing word**. It points at a naming thing."*

**Worked examples (hold up or point):**
- Hold a book: *"book — that's a naming word."*
- Point at the door: *"door — a naming word too."*
- *"Now watch how we **point** at them."* → leads into Step 3.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- A child gives a describing word when asked for a naming word (*"big"*). Correct gently: *"Big tells us **how** — that's a describing word. A naming word is the **thing**. What thing is big? A big… bag. **Bag** is the naming word."*
- A child gives a whole sentence. Accept the noun inside it: *"'The cat runs' — good, the naming word there is **cat**."*

**Step 3 — Introduce near/far · this/that (9–18 min)**

This is the heart of the first lesson. The whole idea is: **near = this · far = that.** Teach it by moving your body and your hand, never by writing "near/far" as the answer.

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *(Hold a book right against your chest.)* *"Look. This book is **near** me — it is close. I say: **This is a book.** — **This**, because it is near."*
> *(Walk to the door and touch it, then walk back and point at it from far.)* *"Now the door is **far** from me. I say: **That is a door.** — **That**, because it is far."*
> *(Hold a pen close.)* *"Near or far? … Near. So — **This is a pen.**"*
> *(Point at a tree through the window.)* *"Near or far? … Far. So — **That is a tree.**"*

**Do at least four more, mixing near and far, and make the class answer before you do:**
- Hold a pencil close → ask *"This or that?"* → class: *this* → *"This is a pencil."*
- Point at the far wall-side bag → *"This or that?"* → class: *that* → *"That is a bag."*
- Hold your own book near → *this* → *"This is a book."*
- Point at the far door → *that* → *"That is a door."*

**The key move — same object, near *and* far, to prove it's about distance not the thing:**
> *(Hold a pen near.)* *"This is a pen."* *(Then place the pen on a far table and point.)* *"Now — **That is a pen.** Same pen! Near it was **this**, far it is **that**. The word changes with the **distance**, not the thing."*

**Show near/far with your hand and your distance — do NOT write "near"/"far" on the board as the answer cue.** The child must learn to *read* the distance, because on the worksheet the picture (big/near, small/far) carries it, with no words.

*Bangla support (oral only):* কাছে থাকলে **this**, দূরে থাকলে **that**। *(near → this, far → that)*

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child says *that* for a near object: *"Is it near or far? … Near. Near is **this**. Try again — This is a…?"*
- Child drops the frame and says only the noun (*"book"*): *"Yes, a book — now the whole sentence: **This is a book.**"*
- Child uses *this/that* as a pointing gesture but forgets *is a*: model the full frame again slowly, tapping each word: *"This — is — a — book."*
- A child points at a classmate to make a sentence (*"That is a boy"*): redirect to objects only — *"We point at **things** today — book, pen, tree. Point at a thing near you."* *(Keeps within the naming-frame cap and avoids person-description.)*

**Step 4 — Model the frames (18–23 min)**

Now fix the two sentence shapes on the board so the class can *see* them, not just hear them.

Write on the board:
> **This is a ___.**  (near)
> **That is a ___.**  (far)

**Say (word-for-word):**
> *"Two sentences today. When a thing is **near**: **This is a ___.** When a thing is **far**: **That is a ___.** The blank is our naming word."*
> *(Point at a near pen.)* *"Near, one thing. **This is a ___** — pen. **This is a pen.**"*
> *(Point at a far tree.)* *"Far, one thing. **That is a ___** — tree. **That is a tree.**"*

**Worked examples — fill the blank with different held nouns, class reads each finished sentence aloud:**
- near book → *"This is a book."*
- far house → *"That is a house."*
- near bag → *"This is a bag."*
- far door → *"That is a door."*
- near pencil → *"This is a pencil."*

**One article note, lightly (do not over-teach — this is a demonstrative block, not an article block):**
> *(Point at a near umbrella.)* *"This is **an** umbrella — 'an', because umbrella starts with a soft open sound. We just say it the easy way."* Keep it to a passing correction; article choice is not graded here.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child writes/says *"This a book"* (drops *is*): tap the board — *"This **is** a book. We need 'is'."*
- Child mixes the frame: *"That is this a tree."* Slow it down, one word at a time from the board.
- Child says *"This is book"* (drops *a*): *"This is **a** book — one book, so we say 'a'."*

**Step 5 — CW-1 done in class (23–33 min)**

Distribute **CW-1** (this/that only — one object, near vs far).

**Model item 1 aloud before they start (word-for-word):**
> *"Look at picture 1. Is the thing **big and close** — near? Or **small and far away** — far? … It is near. Near, one thing — **This is a ___.** What is it? A book. Write: **This is a book.**"*
> *"Picture 2 — big or small? Small, far away. So — **That is a ___.** A tree. **That is a tree.**"*

Then let them work. Move around and prompt with the **two questions only**, never the answer:
> *"Near or far?" … "One or many?"* *(today: always one)* *… "So which word — this or that?"*

Six picture items — students complete **CW-1 in class** in the ~10 min. **HW-1** goes home as separate independent practice. *(The CW-1 picture set is `C1B05-CW1`; see the worksheet extract and the Image Prompts section.)*

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child reads the picture size wrong (calls a small/far object "near"): point to the size — *"Is it big on the page, or small? Small means far. Far is **that**."*
- Child writes the right demonstrative but the wrong noun: fine for the demonstrative mark; the demonstrative is what's graded, and the picture shows the object — help them read it if stuck.
- Child finishes fast: have them whisper-read all six finished sentences back to check each starts with a capital and ends with a full stop.

**Step 6 — Exit Check (33–35 min)**

One prompt per student (roll = 7); teacher points at or names an object as near/far (one thing), child says the full sentence. Prompts: *near book · far tree · near pen · far house · near bag · far door · near pencil.* Answer alone suffices.

# Monday — one/many · these/those (all four)

**Vocabulary today:** *these* / *those* (many); revise *this/that*. All four demonstratives now in play.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine |
| 2 | 5–9 | Revise near/far · this/that |
| 3 | 9–18 | Introduce **one/many** → these/those; teacher points at one vs a group |
| 4 | 18–25 | Model all four frames |
| 5 | 25–33 | **CW-2 done in class** (~8 min) |
| 6 | 33–35 | Exit Check |

**Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)**

The fixed oral opener (never cut).

**Step 2 — Revise near/far · this/that (5–9 min)**

Quick recall of Sunday before adding one/many. Confirm near/far is secure first.

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *(Hold a book near.)* *"Near or far? … Near. So — **This is a book.**"*
> *(Point at a far door.)* *"Near or far? … Far. So — **That is a door.**"*
> *(Hold a pen near, then move it far.)* *"Near — **This is a pen.** Now far — **That is a pen.** The word changes with the distance."*

Two or three rounds, class answering aloud. If most of the class is shaky on this/that, spend the extra minute here before moving on — one/many builds directly on it.

**Step 3 — Introduce one/many · these/those (9–18 min)**

Today's new idea: **one → this/that · many → these/those.** The class already has near/far; now they add one/many on top of it.

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *(Hold up one pen.)* *"One pen, near. **This is a pen.**"*
> *(Hold up three pens together, near.)* *"Now **many** pens, near. Not 'this' — **These are pens.** Many and near → **these**."*
> *(Point at one far tree.)* *"One tree, far. **That is a tree.**"*
> *(Point at a far row of trees.)* *"Many trees, far. **Those are trees.** Many and far → **those**."*

**Build the four-cell chart on the board as you go:**

> | | one (একটি) | many (অনেক) |
> |---|---|---|
> | **near (কাছে)** | **this** | **these** |
> | **far (দূরে)** | **that** | **those** |

**Worked examples — do four to six, class calls the word before you say it:**
- one near book → *this* → *"This is a book."*
- many near books → *these* → *"These are books."*
- one far house → *that* → *"That is a house."*
- many far houses → *those* → *"Those are houses."*
- many near eggs (a tray) → *these* → *"These are eggs."*
- many far houses (a row) → *those* → *"Those are houses."*

**Note the verb change (say it plainly, don't over-drill):**
> *"One thing — **is a**. Many things — **are**. This **is a** pen. These **are** pens."*

*Bangla support (oral only):* একটি হলে this/that, অনেক হলে these/those। *(one → this/that, many → these/those)*

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child says *this* for a group (*"This is pens"*): *"How many? Many. Many and near is **these**. **These are pens.**"*
- Child keeps *is a* for many (*"These is a pens"*): *"Many things — we say **are**, not 'is a'. **These are pens.**"*
- Child confuses these/those (near vs far group): back to the hand — *"Is the group near you or far? Near group → these. Far group → those."*
- Child over-generalises and makes everything plural: hold up one object — *"How many? One. One is **this** — This is a book."*

**Step 4 — Model all four frames (18–25 min)**

Now all four sentence shapes go on the board together.

Write and read:
> **This is a ___.** (near, one) · **That is a ___.** (far, one)
> **These are ___.** (near, many) · **Those are ___.** (far, many)

**Say (word-for-word), pointing to each line:**
> *"Four sentences now. Two we know — this, that — for **one** thing. Two are new — these, those — for **many** things."*
> *"Near one → **This is a ___.** Far one → **That is a ___.**"*
> *"Near many → **These are ___.** Far many → **Those are ___.**"*
> *"Watch the little words: **is a** for one, **are** for many."*

**Worked examples — one of each kind, class reads the finished sentence:**
- near one umbrella → *"This is an umbrella."*
- far one door → *"That is a door."*
- near many pens → *"These are pens."*
- far many houses → *"Those are houses."*

**Two-step check the class can say with you every time:**
> *"Ask two questions. **Near or far?** **One or many?** Then choose. Near + one = this. Near + many = these. Far + one = that. Far + many = those."*

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child gets near/far right but one/many wrong: isolate the second question — *"Good, it's near. Now — one or many? Many. Near + many = **these**."*
- Child writes *"Those is houses"*: *"Many → **are**. Those **are** houses."*
- Child asks about *"an"* vs *"a"*: answer briefly (*"'an' before umbrella, egg — a soft open sound"*) and move on; not graded here.

**Step 5 — CW-2 done in class (25–33 min)**

Distribute **CW-2** (all four demonstratives).

**Model item 1 aloud (word-for-word):**
> *"Picture 1 — near or far? … Near. One or many? … One. Near + one = **this**. **This is a book.**"*
> *"Picture 3 — near or far? Near. One or many? Many — look, there are three. Near + many = **these**. **These are pens.**"*

Then they work. Prompt only with the two questions — *"Near or far? One or many?"* — never the answer. Six picture items — students complete **CW-2 in class**. **HW-2** goes home as separate independent practice.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child defaults every group to *these* (forgets far): *"Is the group near or far? Far group → those."*
- Child writes singular for a pictured group: point at the picture — *"Count them. More than one? Then **are**, and these or those."*
- Fast finishers: whisper-read all six back, checking *is a* / *are* matches one/many in each.

**Step 6 — Exit Check (33–35 min)**

One prompt per student (roll = 7); teacher points at one/many, near/far, child gives the full sentence. Prompts: *one near book · many near pens · one far house · many far trees · one near umbrella · many near bananas · one far door.* Answer alone suffices.

# Tuesday — full four-way picture practice

**No new forms.** All four demonstratives; the week's fullest practice.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Track A oral routine |
| 2 | 5–10 | Revise the four-cell chart |
| 3 | 10–18 | Whole-class picture reading — point, ask near/far + one/many, class calls the word |

**Step 1 — Track A oral routine (0–5 min)**

The fixed oral opener (never cut).

**Step 2 — Revise the four-cell chart (5–10 min)**

Rebuild the near/far × one/many chart with the class calling each word. No new forms today — this is fluency practice.

**Say and do (word-for-word):**
> *(Draw the empty four-cell grid.)* *"Help me fill it. Near, one — which word? … **this**. Near, many? … **these**. Far, one? … **that**. Far, many? … **those**."*
> *(Then reverse it.)* *"I say a word, you tell me near/far and one/many. **Those** — ? … far, many. **This** — ? … near, one."*

Confirm all four are secure before the fullest practice sheet. If a cell is shaky, do two quick pointing examples for it.

**Step 3 — Whole-class picture reading (10–18 min)**

Point at objects, or draw quick board sketches at size (one near, one far, a near group, a far row), and have the class call the demonstrative aloud **before** anyone writes. This rehearses the exact skill CW-3 then tests independently.

**Worked examples — run six to eight quickly, mixing all four types:**
- big near book (sketch large, low) → *these? no — one* → *"This is a book."*
- small far house (sketch tiny, high) → *"That is a house."*
- near group of eggs → *"These are eggs."*
- far row of houses → *"Those are houses."*
- near one umbrella → *"This is an umbrella."*
- far row of trees → *"Those are trees."*
- near group of books → *"These are books."*
- far one bag → *"That is a bag."*

Each time, make the class say the **two questions** first — *"Near or far? One or many?"* — then the sentence.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Class is quick on near/far but slow on one/many, or vice versa: separate the two questions and drill the weak one for a minute.
- A child calls out only the demonstrative, not the sentence: *"Good — now the whole sentence: These are…?"*

**Step 4 — CW-3 done in class (18–30 min)**

Distribute **CW-3** — the week's fullest sheet, all four demonstratives mixed.

**Model item 1 aloud, then let them work slowly:**
> *"Picture 1 — near or far? … Near. One or many? … Many. Near + many = **these**. **These are books.**"*

The children **read each picture** (near/far? one/many?), choose the demonstrative, and complete the frame. Teacher moves around and **guides where needed** — pointing back to the four-cell chart, asking the two questions — but does **not** give the answer. **HW-3** goes home.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Child rushes and writes the first word that comes: slow them — *"Two questions first. Point to the chart. Near or far? One or many?"*
- Child gets stuck between these/those on a group: *"Is the group big and low (near) or small and high (far)? Near group → these. Far group → those."*
- Child's noun doesn't match the picture: fine for the demonstrative mark; the demonstrative is graded, not the noun spelling — help them name the pictured object if stuck.

**Step 5 — Exit Check (30–35 min)**

One prompt per student (roll = 7); prompts differ from the worksheet items. Teacher shows a quick board sketch at size (big/near, small/far, one/group), child gives the full sentence. Prompts: *many near eggs · one far house · one near umbrella · many far pencils · many near books · one far tree · one near bag.*

The fixed oral opener (never cut).

**Step 2 — Mixed revision (5–12 min)**

Quick oral picture-reading across all four demonstratives — no new forms. Mix near/far and one/many freely so the class practises deciding **both** cues together before the consolidation sheet.

**Say and do (word-for-word), fast rounds:**
> *(Sketch or point, at size, changing type each time.)*

> *"Near, one — **This is a ___.** … book."*
> *"Far, many — **Those are ___.** … trees."*

Keep the pace brisk — the aim is automatic recall of the two-question routine, not new teaching. If one type still wobbles, give it two extra examples.

**Step 3 — CW-4 out (12–24 min)**

Distribute **CW-4** (mixed four-way — the consolidation sheet).

> *"Picture 1 — near or far? Far. One or many? Many. Far + many = **those**. **Those are houses.**"*

By now the class should need less guiding. Prompt only where a child stalls, always with the two questions. Six picture items — students complete **CW-4 in class**. **HW-4** goes home as separate independent practice.

**Anticipated errors & correction:**
- Lingering these/those or this/that confusion: send the child back to the wall chart to self-check rather than giving the answer.
- Child finishes early: have them read all six sentences back and check each *is a*/*are* matches one/many — self-correction is the goal on consolidation day.

**Step 4 — Self-try rehearsal (24–31 min) — **ungraded****

Frame on the board: **___ is a ___.** / **___ are ___.**

**Say (word-for-word):**
> *"Now you make your own sentence. Look around — pick something **near or far**, **one or many**. Choose your own naming word. Write one sentence."*
> *(Model one so they see the shape, then wipe it.)* *"Watch me: I see one book near me — **This is a book.** Now your turn — a different thing."*

Students write in silence, ~4 min. 2–3 volunteers read aloud; teacher gives feedback on **accuracy only** — **no marks**. This is the rehearsal; Thursday's graded self-try uses **different prompts** (§6.5).

**Remind the class this is practice for tomorrow** — the graded self-try on the PT asks the same kind of sentence with a fresh prompt.

**Anticipated errors & correction (verbal, gentle — nothing recorded):**
- Child copies the modelled sentence exactly: *"Good shape — now change the thing. What else is near you?"*
- Child writes a free sentence beyond the frame (*"I like my book"*): steer back to the pointing frame — *"Start with a pointing word — This, That, These, or Those."* *(Keeps within the naming-frame cap; no free composition.)*
- Child mismatches one/many with is/are: note it aloud for the class generally, don't single the child out — *"Remember: one → is a, many → are."*

**Step 5 — Collect; remind about Thursday PT (31–33 min)**

Collect the CW-4 sheets and books. Tell the class that Thursday's Performance Test covers spelling and meanings plus a pointing sentence of their own — a quick heads-up, no new content.

**Step 6 — Exit Check (33–35 min)**

One prompt per student (roll = 7); read/point a mixed cue, child gives the full sentence. Prompts differ from the worksheet (§6.5 spirit): *many far houses · one near book · many near bananas · one far door · one near umbrella · many near pens · one far pencil.*
