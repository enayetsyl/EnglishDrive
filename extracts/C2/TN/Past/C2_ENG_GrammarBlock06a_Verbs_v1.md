## The Clue Card · `C2B06a-CC`

*A one-page card for the student's desk. Re-show the naming-word card from earlier blocks, then add this. (Teacher note: describing words come in Block 7 — do not present them yet.)*

> ### My Doing-Words Card  *(আমার কাজ-শব্দের কার্ড)*
>
> **A doing word tells what someone does.**  *(কাজ-শব্দ বোঝায় কেউ কী করে।)*
> I **read**.  We **play**.  She **writes**.  They **look**.
>
> **One doer or many doers?**  *(এক কর্তা না অনেক কর্তা?)*
> — One doer (he, she, the boy) → add **s** to the doing word → he **reads**, she **looks**   *(একজন কর্তা হলে কাজ-শব্দের শেষে **s** বসে।)*
> — Many doers, or I / we / you / they → keep it plain → they **read**, we **look**   *(অনেক হলে, বা I / we / you / they-এর সাথে কাজ-শব্দ একই থাকে।)*
> *This **s** is on the **doing word** (one doer). It is **not** the plural **s** that makes a naming word many (book → book**s**).*  *(এই **s** আলাদা — নাম-শব্দের **s** মানে অনেক; কাজ-শব্দের **s** মানে একজন কর্তা।)*
>
> **am · is · are** — the little joining words  *(ছোট্ট যোগ-শব্দ)*
> — **I am** …   *(আমি → am)*
> — he / she / the boy **is** …   *(একজন → is)*
> — we / you / they **are** …   *(অনেক → are)*
> *Example:* I **am** at school.   The bird **is** in the tree.   We **are** friends.

# W5 · Sunday — Doing words in action: simple present + the **-s** change (one vs many)

**Day 1 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout · Clue Card issued today**
**Verbs in play today (all known from Block 1 as doing words; graded-eligible from today):** ask, answer, listen, look, say
**New skill today:** using a doing word in a sentence, and changing it for **one** (adds **s**) vs **many** (stays plain).

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Fast reactivation: students already know doing words (Block 1) |
| 2 | 4–8 | The new question — *does the word ever change?* |
| 3 | 8–16 | Discovery: **one** adds **s**; **many** stays plain (rich examples) |
| 4 | 16–22 | Rule box + guided "one or many?" drill |
| 5 | 22–30 | Guided practice — CW1 out |
| 6 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Fast reactivation (0–4 min)
*Students met doing words in Block 1 — do not re-teach recognition slowly. Reactivate and move on.*
"Assalamu alaikum. In Block 1 you already learned two word-jobs: the **naming word** and the **doing word**. Quick — call out a **doing word**." *(Take 4–5 fast: read, eat, play, walk, pray, go — all from Block 1.)* "Good, you know these well. A doing word tells an **action** — something we *do*. Today we do something **new** with them."

### Step 2 — The new question (4–8 min)
Write two sentences on the board, side by side, and read them:
> The boy **reads**.    The boys **read**.
"Look carefully. Same doing word — but is it written the **same way**? … No. One has an extra **s**: *read**s***. The other is plain: *read*. Today we learn **when** a doing word takes that **s**, and when it stays plain." *(This is the day's new learning — flag it as important.)*

### Step 3 — Discovery: one doer vs many doers (8–16 min)
Act it out with real students so the rule is felt, not just told. Build two columns on the board headed **One doer → doing word takes s** and **Many doers → doing word stays plain**.

*Head off the plurals trap before it starts.* Last week (Block 5) the class learned that adding **s** makes a naming word **many** — book → book**s**. Today's **s** is different, so name the difference out loud:
> "Careful — this is a **new** kind of **s**. Last week, **s** on a **naming word** meant **many** — one book, many book**s**. Today's **s** goes on the **doing word**, and it means the **opposite**: it tells us there is just **one doer**. *The boy read**s*** — one boy. *The boy**s** read* — many boys, so the **doing word** has **no s**."

Show the contrast on the board so they see both s's at once:
> **book → books**  ← s on the **naming word** = **many** *(last week)*
> **the boy reads** ← s on the **doing word** = **one doer** *(today)*

Call one student to the front:
"Aisha is **one** doer. Aisha **looks** at the board — *look**s***, with an **s** on the **doing word**." *(write under One doer)*
Now call three students:
"These are **many** doers. They **look** at the board — *look*, plain, no s." *(write under Many doers)*

Repeat with each of today's verbs, always one child then a group, so they hear both forms every time. *(Point out the second row: "boy**s** has the plural s — that is the naming word being many — so the doing word *ask* stays plain. Two different jobs for s.")*
| One doer → doing word takes **s** | Many doers → doing word stays plain |
|---|---|
| The boy **asks** a question. | The boy**s** **ask** a question. |
| Yusuf **answers** well. | The friends **answer** well. |
| My sister **listens** to her mother. | We **listen** to our mother. |
| Aisha **says** her name. | They **say** their names. |
| He **looks** at the clock. | You **look** at the clock. |

Then plant the pronoun cases explicitly (these trip children up):
"With **I**, **we**, **you**, **they** — always **plain**. *I look. We ask. You listen. They say.*
With **he**, **she**, **it**, or **one naming word** — the doing word takes **s**. *He looks. She asks. The boy listens.*"

*(Optional Bangla support: এই **s** আলাদা — গত সপ্তাহে নাম-শব্দে **s** মানে ছিল **অনেক** (book→books); আজকের **s** বসে **কাজ-শব্দে**, আর মানে **একজন কর্তা**। একজন কর্তা হলে কাজ-শব্দের শেষে **s**; অনেক কর্তা হলে কাজ-শব্দ একই থাকে। Keep main delivery in English.)*

### Step 4 — Rule box + guided drill (16–22 min)
**Rule box (write on the board, read together twice):**
> **One doer** (he, she, the boy) → doing word **+ s** → look**s**, ask**s**, say**s**
> **Many doers**, or **I / we / you / they** → **plain** doing word → look, ask, say
> *Remember: this **s** is on the **doing word** and means **one doer** — it is not the plural **s** that makes a naming word **many**.*

Now a fast oral drill — say the subject, class supplies the form and the reason. Do all ten; keep the pace up:
1. the boy … (asks — one) · 2. we … (ask — many) · 3. Aisha … (looks — one) · 4. they … (look — many) · 5. I … (listen — I is plain) · 6. my father … (listens — one) · 7. the friends … (answer — many) · 8. she … (says — one) · 9. you … (say — many) · 10. the boys … (look — many)

### Step 5 — Guided practice (22–30 min, CW1)
Hand out CW1. **Part A** is a quick warm-up (underline the doing word — recognition they already own, one minute). **Part B** is the new work: choose the correct form for one vs many — model B1 together: "*The boy ___ his friend* — one boy, so **asks**." **Part C** has a word box (ask · answer · listen · look · say) and **no options** — the child picks the doing word that fits *and* gives it the right form: model C1 — "*Yusuf ___ 'Assalamu alaikum'* — you **say** a greeting, and Yusuf is one, so **says**." Then students complete all three parts.

### Step 6 — Exit Check — Sunday (14 prompts, one per student, oral, 2–5 min)
Give each student a **subject**; they say the sentence with the correctly-formed verb and state "one" or "many":
1. the boy / look → looks (one) · 2. we / ask → ask (many) · 3. Aisha / listen → listens (one) · 4. they / answer → answer (many) · 5. I / say → say (I, plain) · 6. my father / ask → asks (one) · 7. the friends / look → look (many) · 8. she / answer → answers (one) · 9. you / listen → listen (many) · 10. the boys / say → say (many) · 11. the bird / listen → listens (one) · 12. we / look → look (many) · 13. my sister / ask → asks (one) · 14. they / listen → listen (many)
*Target: correct one/many form + reason. Correct on the spot; nothing printed.*

# W5 · Monday — The **-s** change, extended: five new doing words + trickier subjects

**Day 2 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout**
**New verbs today (graded-eligible from today):** see, live, read, write, make
**Today's work:** the same **one/many** rule from Sunday, now with **five new doing words** and **harder subjects** (a naming word instead of just he/she; two-part subjects).

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall the -s rule (fast) |
| 2 | 4–9 | Five new doing words: see, live, read, write, make |
| 3 | 9–17 | Apply the rule to the new verbs — rich examples |
| 4 | 17–23 | Extend: naming-word subjects (the bird → lives; the birds → live) |
| 5 | 23–30 | Guided practice — CW2 out |
| 6 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall the rule (0–4 min)
*The -s rule was introduced Sunday — recall it, don't re-teach it.*
"Say Sunday's rule with me: **one doer** adds **s** to the doing word, **many doers** keep it **plain**. Quick check — *the boy … look?*" *(class: looks)* "*the boy**s** … look?*" *(class: look)* "Notice — *boys* has the plural **s** because they are **many**, but the **doing word** *look* stays plain. Two different s's. Today the **same rule**, with **new** doing words."

### Step 2 — Five new doing words (4–9 min)
Read-and-say each, with meaning and one oral sentence. Keep subjects mixed (one/many) so the rule stays live:
- **see** — "We **see** the birds. The boy **sees** the birds."
- **live** — "The bird **lives** in the tree. The birds **live** in the tree."
- **read** — "I **read** my book. Aisha **reads** her book."
- **write** — "They **write** their names. He **writes** his name."
- **make** — "We **make** a shape. The boy **makes** a shape."

### Step 3 — Apply the rule (9–17 min)
Two columns again — **One (add s)** / **Many (stay plain)** — but now the class supplies the form. Do all of these, alternating:
| One (add **s**) | Many (stay plain) |
|---|---|
| The bird **lives** in the tree. | The birds **live** in the tree. |
| Aisha **reads** her book. | The friends **read** their books. |
| Yusuf **writes** his name. | We **write** our names. |
| My father **sees** the birds. | They **see** the birds. |
| The cat **lives** near the pond. | The cats **live** near the pond. |

Pause on the pronoun reminder once more: "*I, we, you, they* → **plain**. *I see, we read, you write, they live.*"

### Step 4 — Extend: naming-word subjects (17–23 min)
This is the harder step. Sunday used he/she/the boy; today stress that **any single naming word** counts as "one":
"*The **frog*** — is that one or many? One. So — *the frog **lives**.* *The **frogs*** — many — *the frogs **live**.*"
Do four quick ones, class deciding one/many first, then the form:
1. the hen … (one → lives) · 2. the boys … (many → read) · 3. my sister … (one → writes) · 4. the friends … (many → see)

### Step 5 — Guided practice (23–30 min, CW2)
Hand out CW2. **Part A** — choose the correct form. **Part B** — write the correct form of the verb in ( ). Model A1 ("*The bird ___ in the tree* — one bird, so **lives**"); students complete.

### Step 6 — Exit Check — Monday (14 prompts, answer + "one/many")
1. the boy / read → reads (one) · 2. we / see → see (many) · 3. Aisha / write → writes (one) · 4. the boys / live → live (many) · 5. my father / read → reads (one) · 6. they / see → see (many) · 7. the bird / live → lives (one) · 8. I / write → write (I, plain) · 9. the friends / read → read (many) · 10. Yusuf / see → sees (one) · 11. the cat / live → lives (one) · 12. we / read → read (many) · 13. my sister / write → writes (one) · 14. the hens / live → live (many)

# W5 · Tuesday — am / is / are (introduce)

**Day 3 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout**
**New verbs today:** count, draw, match, trace, play *(the graded target today is am/is/are)*

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Recall doing words + agreement |
| 2 | 5–14 | Discovery: I **am** · one **is** · many **are** |
| 3 | 14–20 | Rule box + worked examples |
| 4 | 20–24 | New verbs: count, draw, match, trace, play |
| 5 | 24–30 | Guided practice — CW3 out |
| 6 | 30–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–5 min)
"Say Monday's rule: one doer → doing word **+s**; many doers → plain doing word. Today we learn three little words: **am, is, are.** We use them to say what someone or something **is**. Listen: *I **am** here. Yusuf **is** my friend. The boys **are** at school.* Today we learn which one to use — **am**, **is**, or **are**."

### Step 2 — Discovery (5–14 min)
Point to yourself, one student, a group:
"**I am** your teacher — with *I*, we use **am**. Yusuf **is** here; the bird **is** in the tree — with **one**, we use **is**. The boys **are** in the classroom; we **are** at school — with **many**, or *we/you/they*, we use **are**."

**Rule box:**
> **I → am · one (he / she / it / the boy) → is · many, or we / you / they → are**

### Step 3 — Worked examples (14–20 min)
Do these on the board, each time asking the class **"I, one, or many?"** *before* filling the blank, so the choice is reasoned, not guessed:
1. I ___ at home. → **am** *(with I → am)*
2. Yusuf ___ my friend. → **is** *(one → is)*
3. We ___ in the classroom. → **are** *(many → are)*
4. The bird ___ in the tree. → **is** *(one naming word → is)*
5. They ___ near the pond. → **are** *(they → are)*
6. My father ___ at home. → **is** *(one → is)*
7. The boys ___ at school. → **are** *(many → are)*
8. I ___ your friend. → **am** *(with I → am)*

Keep the board split into three little columns — **am** / **is** / **are** — and write each answer under its heading, so the class sees the three groups building up.

### Step 4 — New verbs (20–24 min)
Read-and-say each of today's five doing words, with meaning and one oral sentence. These are the last five verbs of the block, so use them in **am/is/are** carrier sentences where you can — that keeps today's target live while introducing the word:
- **count** — "We **count** the birds." *(show 1-2-3 on fingers)* "The boy **counts** the eggs."
- **draw** — "I **draw** a triangle." "Aisha **draws** a square." *(point to shapes on the board)*
- **match** — "We **match** the words." "She **matches** the shapes." *(mime joining two cards)*
- **trace** — "You **trace** the letter." "He **traces** his name." *(mime following a line with a finger)*
- **play** — "The boys **play** in the classroom." "The boy **plays** with his friend."

*(Note the -s on the "one doer" versions — counts, draws, matches, traces, plays — quietly keeps Monday's rule alive without re-teaching it.)*

### Step 5 — Guided practice (24–30 min, CW3)
Hand out CW3. Both parts fill **am / is / are.** Model the first item of each part aloud, thinking through the choice so students hear the reasoning:
- A1 — "*I ___ at school.* Start with the subject: **I**. With **I** we always use **am**. So — *I **am** at school.*"
- Then a "one" item and a "many" item so both other forms are modelled: "*The bird ___ in the tree* — one bird, so **is**. *The boys ___ in the classroom* — many, so **are**."

Then students complete both parts on their own. **Watch for two common errors as you circulate:**
1. **"I is"** — children often reach for *is* with *I*. Remind: "**I** takes **am**, only **am**."
2. **Compound coming Wednesday** — if a child asks about "the cat and the dog," tell them we meet that tomorrow; today keep to single subjects.

### Step 6 — Exit Check — Tuesday (14 prompts, am/is/are + reason)
1. I → am · 2. the boy → is · 3. we → are · 4. Aisha → is · 5. they → are · 6. the birds → are · 7. I → am · 8. my father → is · 9. you → are · 10. the cat → is · 11. the boys → are · 12. she → is · 13. I → am · 14. the friends → are

# W5 · Wednesday — am/is/are consolidation + subject-number + Self-try

**Day 4 of 4 · 35 minutes · English throughout · No new rule**

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Recall am/is/are |
| 2 | 5–12 | Mixed practice, incl. compound subjects (the cat **and** the dog → are) |
| 3 | 12–22 | Guided practice — CW4 out |
| 4 | 22–30 | **Self-try** (4-mark rubric) |
| 5 | 30–35 | Exit Check (mixed) |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–5 min)
"Say it: I → **am**, one → **is**, many → **are**. Today we practise all together, and meet a new case: two things joined by **and** make **many** — so we use **are**."

### Step 2 — Mixed practice + the "and" case (5–12 min)
Today's one new idea: **two subjects joined by "and" make "many" → are.** Teach it, then mix all three forms so nothing is forgotten.

**Teach the "and" case first.** Call two students to the front:
"Here is Yusuf. Yusuf **is** one — *Yusuf **is** here.* Here is Abdullah. Abdullah **is** one — *Abdullah **is** here.* Now — **Yusuf and Abdullah**. That is **two**, and two is **many**. So — *Yusuf and Abdullah **are** here.* When we join with **and**, we have **many**, and many takes **are**."

Write the pattern on the board:
> one **and** one → **many** → **are**

**Now mix all three forms** — ask "I, one, or many?" before each, and keep the pace up:
1. The cat **and** the dog ___ near the tree. → **are** *(two → many)*
2. She ___ near the pond. → **is** *(one)*
3. You **and** I ___ friends. → **are** *(two → many)*
4. The birds ___ in the tree. → **are** *(many)*
5. I ___ at home. → **am** *(with I)*
6. My brother **and** my sister ___ at home. → **are** *(two → many)*
7. The bird ___ in the tree. → **is** *(one)*
8. We ___ at school. → **are** *(we)*

*(Watch the trap: a child may see one nearby word and say "is" — e.g. "the cat and the dog **is**." Point back to **and**: "two things — so **are**.")*

### Step 3 — Guided practice (12–22 min, CW4)
Hand out CW4. **Part A** is single subjects (am/is/are); **Part B** mixes in the **compound "and" subjects** from today; **Part C** comes back to Monday's **doing-word -s rule** (one doer → add s; many doers → plain), so the week's two ideas — *being* words and *doing* words — sit side by side. Model one of each:
- A1 (be) — "*The boys ___ near the board* — many, so **are**."
- B1 (compound be) — "*The cat **and** the dog ___ near the tree* — two things joined by **and**, so **many**, so **are**."
- C1 (doing-word agreement) — "*The bird ___ in the tree (sit/sits)* — one bird, one doer, so **sits**."

Students complete all three parts. As you circulate, watch two things: in **Part B**, every "and" subject takes **are** (if a child writes "is", point to **and** and ask "one or two?"); in **Part C**, remind them this **s** is the *doing-word* s (one doer) — not am/is/are, and not the plural s on a naming word.

### Step 4 — Self-try (22–30 min) — 4-mark rubric
The block's mandatory self-construction close (Charter §I.1). **Anchored, not open. Separate from the W5 Performance Test self-try.**

> **Self-try prompt (demo/rehearsal anchor — doing word: *play*; subject given: *the boys*):**
> "Write your own **one** sentence about your family or your class using a **doing word** you learned. First **name its job** (doing word). Then **say why** in one sentence (Bangla, English, or mixed). Then **circle the doing word.**"

**How to run it (do not give away a gradable sentence):**
1. **Model with the anchor only.** On the board, work the *rehearsal* anchor — *the boys / play* — all the way through, so students see the shape without copying a sentence they will be graded on:
 > *The boys **play** in the classroom.* → job: **doing word** (**play**). Why: it tells what the boys **do**. → circle **play**.
2. **Then release them** to write their **own** sentence with a **different** subject and doing word. Remind: one sentence, real, about their family or class.
3. **Circulate and prompt, don't supply.** If a child is stuck, ask "what does someone in your family **do**?" — let the verb come from them.

**The 4 marks (1 each — PD-010; value alignment not scored):**
- **Content accuracy** — the sentence is correct and uses a real doing word.
- **Reasoning** — the "why" correctly explains that it is a doing word (an action).
- **Organisation** — named the job, gave the why, circled the word (all three parts present).
- **Word-job placement** — the circled word is genuinely the doing word, not a naming word.
