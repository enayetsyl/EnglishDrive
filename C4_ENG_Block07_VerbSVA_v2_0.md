## Verb & Agreement Clue Card

> ### THE VERB — **ক্রিয়া** · and its match with the **SUBJECT — কর্তা**
>
> A **verb** is the word that tells what someone or something **does** or **is**.
> *(ক্রিয়া হলো সেই শব্দ যা বোঝায় কেউ বা কিছু কী **করে** বা কী **অবস্থায় আছে**।)*
>
> The **subject** is the one who does it — ask **“Who or what does the action?”**
> *(কর্তা হলো যে কাজটি করে — প্রশ্ন করো: **কে** বা **কী** কাজটি করছে?)*
>
> **The one question that runs this whole block:**
> ### *Is the subject **one**, or **more than one**?*
> *(কর্তা কি **এক**, নাকি **একের বেশি**?)*
>
> ---
>
> **① One subject → the verb takes -s** · **More than one → the verb stays plain**
> *(কর্তা এক হলে verb-এ **-s** বসে; একের বেশি হলে verb **অপরিবর্তিত** থাকে।)*
>
> | Subject | Verb | Example |
> |---|---|---|
> | one (একবচন) | **+ -s** | The child **plays**. · My sister **writes**. |
> | more than one (বহুবচন) | plain | The children **play**. · My sisters **write**. |
>
> **⚑ The trap:** the **-s** moves. One sister**—** write**s**. Two sister**s** — write. *(noun-এ -s থাকলে verb-এ -s থাকে না।)*
>
> **Teacher note.** Some naming words show *more than one* without an **-s** — **children · people · clothes · leaves**. The rule still holds; only the clue is missing. *The children **play**.* Ask the meaning, not the letter.
>
> ---
>
> **② Spelling — when the verb needs -es or -ies**
>
> | Verb ends in | Add | Held examples |
> |---|---|---|
> | **-sh · -ss · -x** (and -ch, -o) | **-es** | wash → **washes** · finish → **finishes** · discuss → **discusses** · mix → **mixes** |
> | **consonant + y** | **-ies** | carry → **carr**~~y~~**ies** |
>
> *(sh · ss · x শেষে **-es**; consonant + y হলে y উঠে গিয়ে **-ies**।)*
>
> ---
>
> **③ is / are · has / have**
>
> | Subject | be | have | Example |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | one | **is** | **has** | The park **is** green. · My aunt **has** a ticket. |
> | more than one | **are** | **have** | The lakes **are** cold. · My uncles **have** tickets. |
>
> **Two groups to remember:**
> - **people · clothes** — always **more than one**: *The people **are** healthy.* · *The clothes **are** new.*
> - **family · team** — **one group, so one**: *My family **is** small.* · *The team **has** a new card.*
> *(family / team একটি দল বোঝায় — তাই একবচন।)*
>
> ---
>
> **④ each + one thing → one verb**
>
> **each** is always followed by **one** thing, so the verb is the **one**-verb.
> *(each-এর পরে সবসময় একবচন — তাই verb-ও একবচন।)*
>
> *Each doctor **has** a form.* · *Each cousin **carries** the jar.* · *Each sister **collects** the leaves.*
>
> ---
>
> **⚑ Remember — মনে রেখো:**
> - Find the verb first. Then ask: **one, or more than one?**
> - **One → -s. Many → plain.** *(এক → -s; বহু → plain।)*
> - **people / clothes → are, have.** **family / team → is, has.**
> - **each → one.**

# Class 1 (Sunday) — Find the verb · one or more than one · the **-s** rule

**Objective.** Students find the verb in a simple present sentence, decide whether the subject is one or more than one, and add **-s** to the verb when the subject is one.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Re-walk the classes we know; what a verb is |
| 2 | 5–12 | Find the verb |
| 3 | 12–19 | Find the subject — one, or more than one? |
| 4 | 19–27 | The rule: one subject → verb + **-s** |
| 5 | 27–29 | Clue Card rows ① |
| 6 | 29–32 | CW-1 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Re-walk the classes we know (0–5 min)

Books closed. Write on the board: `worker · digs · strong · quickly`

> **Say:** “Our Clue Card first. *Worker* — does it name someone, or act?” → **“Names — noun.”** “*Strong* — what does it do to a noun?” → **“Describes it — adjective.”** “*Quickly* — what does it tell us?” → **“How — adverb.”** “And *digs*?” → **“Action — verb.”**

> **Say:** “This week the **verb** is our word. We have known it since Block 1 — but today we learn something new about it. The verb **changes its shape** depending on who is doing it. That is our whole week.”

**Teacher note — say both terms in class.** *“The **role** of a word is what the exam calls its **part of speech**.”* Repeat through the week so no child meets “part of speech” first in the exam paper.

### Step 2 — Find the verb (5–12 min)

> **Say:** “To find the verb, ask: **what is happening?** What does someone do?”

Write, one under the other, and work through aloud:

```
My uncle pours the water.
The tourists enjoy the mountain.
Aisha collects the stamps.
The workers collect the stones.
```

> **Say (item 1):** “*My uncle pours the water.* What is happening here?” → **“Pours.”** → “Good. *Pours* is the verb. *Uncle* names a person — noun. *Water* names a thing — noun. Only **pours** is an action.”

> **Say (one more word today):** “A **worker** is the one who does the **work**. *Worker* names the person; *work* names what they do. Both are naming words — nouns.” *(`work` is a **PD-011 override** — a W7 word from today, spelled and tested like any other.)*

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student names the noun after the verb** (says *water*): “*Water* is a thing — you can point at it. Can you point at *pours*? No — you can only **do** it. If you can **do** it, it is the verb.”

### Step 3 — Find the subject: one, or more than one? (12–19 min)

> **Say:** “Now the second question. Once you have the verb, ask: **who or what does it?** That one is called the **subject** — *কর্তা*.”

Return to the same four sentences and ask only for the subject:

| Sentence | Verb | Subject | One or more? |
|---|---|---|---|
| My uncle pours the water. | pours | my uncle | **one** |
| The tourists enjoy the mountain. | enjoy | the tourists | **more than one** |
| Aisha collects the stamps. | collects | Aisha | **one** |
| The workers collect the stones. | collect | the workers | **more than one** |

> **Say:** “Careful — the subject is the one who **does**, not just any noun in the sentence. In *The workers **collect** the stones*, who **collects**? **The workers.** *Stones* are not doing anything; they are being **collected**.”

### Step 4 — The rule: one subject → verb + **-s** (19–27 min)

Write the two columns side by side and let the class read across:

```
ONE                          MORE THAN ONE
My sister writes.            My sisters write.
The child plays.             The children play.
The bird drinks.             The birds drink.
The worker digs.             The workers dig.
```

> **Say:** “Look only at the verbs. On the left: *writes, plays, drinks, digs* — every one has **-s**. On the right: *write, play, drink, dig* — no **-s** anywhere. So: **one → -s. More than one → plain.**”

**The trap — teach it deliberately, it is the commonest mistake.**
> **Say:** “Now watch where the **-s** goes. *My sister**—** write**s**.* One sister, and the **-s** is on the **verb**. *My sister**s** write.* More than one, and the **-s** has moved onto the naming word — there is none left on the verb. The **-s** moves. When the naming word takes the -s, the verb gives it up.”

Ask the class to say it back: **“এক হলে verb-এ -s; একের বেশি হলে verb-এ -s নেই।”**

**Quick oral round.** Read the subject; the class supplies the verb form.

| Prompt | Class answers |
|---|---|
| The doctor … (help) | help**s** |
| The doctors … (help) | help |
| My cousin … (visit) | visit**s** |
| My cousins … (visit) | visit |
| The flower … (bloom) | bloom**s** |
| The flowers … (bloom) | bloom |

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student adds -s to a plural subject** (*the doctors helps*): “You have put the -s twice — once on *doctors*, once on *helps*. English only allows it once. The naming word already told us there are many. Take the -s off the verb: *the doctors **help***.”

**Attribution note for the teacher.** Natural-world sentences are kept **descriptive** all week — *the rain falls*, *the river flows*, *the plant grows*. Never *the rain gives us water*: what is given is given by Allah. Correct it as a values matter if a child says it; never mark it down as a language error.

### Step 5 — Clue Card, row ① (27–29 min)

Pin the Verb & Agreement Clue Card. Read row ① only.

> **Say:** “Two lines to keep. **One → -s. More than one → plain.** Tomorrow we learn what to do when the verb ends in a difficult letter.”

### Step 6 — CW-1 (29–32 min)

Distribute CW-1. Model item 1 of each part:

> **Say (Part A):** “Underline the verb — the action word.”
> **Say (Part B):** “Write **one** or **more than one** for the subject. Do not write the verb here — only count the subject.”
> **Say (Part C):** “Two forms in brackets. Choose the one that matches the subject, and write only that word. *The child ___ (play / plays)* — is the child one?” → **“One.”** → “So?” → **“Plays.”**

“What you do not finish goes home as HW-1.”

### Step 7 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

Teacher reads the subject and the plain verb; one student per prompt gives the correct form.

# Class 2 (Monday) — **-es / -ies** spellings · the second exam format · first find-and-fix

**Objective.** Students add **-es** to verbs ending in *sh, ss, x* and **-ies** to a verb ending in consonant + *y*; recognise **both** ways the exam asks the question; and correct a wrong `-s`/`-es` form.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–3 | Re-walk Day 1: one → -s |
| 2 | 3–11 | **-es** after sh · ss · x |
| **2b** | **11–15** | **Two words for the last two endings: `teach` · `go`** (§N.1) |
| 3 | 15–20 | **-ies** after consonant + y |
| 4 | 20–24 | **Two ways the exam asks** — slash pair and single base verb |
| 5 | 24–30 | Find and fix (light) |
| 6 | 30–32 | CW-2 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

**⚑ Day 2 is still the heaviest day of the block** — three spelling patterns, two new words, a second item format and a first find-and-fix in 35 minutes. Step 2b was compressed to 4 minutes and the time given to Step 5, so find-and-fix is no longer the step at risk. **Step 2b cannot be dropped**: PD-011 requires `go` to be taught explicitly.

### Step 1 — Re-walk (0–3 min)

> **Say:** “Yesterday’s question — say it with me.” → **“Is the subject one, or more than one?”** “And the rule?” → **“One → -s. More than one → plain.”**

Two on the board: `The son helps.` / `The sons help.` Ask which is which and why.

### Step 2 — **-es** after *sh · ss · x* (3–11 min)

> **Say:** “Now a small difficulty. Some verbs cannot take a plain **-s** — your mouth cannot say it. Try: *wash* + *s*. *Washs*. Can you say it?” *(Let them try; the failure is the teaching.)* “No. So we help the mouth with an **e**: **wash → washes**.”

Write and read across:

| Verb | Ends in | One-subject form |
|---|---|---|
| wash | **-sh** | wash**es** |
| finish | **-sh** | finish**es** |
| discuss | **-ss** | discuss**es** |
| mix | **-x** | mix**es** |

> **Say:** “Four verbs, one pattern. If the verb ends in **sh, ss or x**, add **-es**, not just -s. Say them: *washes · finishes · discusses · mixes*. There are two more endings that do this, and today we learn a word for each.”

Put each into a sentence on the board and ask for the form:

```
My mother ___ (wash) the spoon.        → washes
Nusair ___ (finish) the picture.       → finishes
The teacher ___ (discuss) the news.    → discusses
My aunt ___ (mix) the seeds.           → mixes
```

> **Say (the check that matters):** “And if the subject is **more than one**? *The teachers ___ the news.*” → **“Discuss — plain.”** “Correct. The **-es** difficulty only ever appears when the subject is **one**. More than one, and the verb goes back to plain — no -s, no -es.”

### Step 2b — Two words for the last two endings (11–15 min)

**Do not skip this step.** Both binding papers set an `-es` item on an ending our word list cannot supply — **HY25 Q5(c)** *The Quran ___ (teach / teaches) us to live a good life* is **-ch**, and **Annual Q7(c)** *My little brother ___ (go/goes) to the mosque* is **-o**. Without these two words the class meets those endings for the first time in the exam.

The class already knows both words. **What is new is the spelling**, so go straight there — §N.1 Meaning → Class → Use, run briskly.

**① teach** *(শেখানো)*

> **Say:** “**Teach** — to help someone learn. Your teacher **teaches** you English. Name or do?” → **“Do — verb.”**
> **Spell it on the board:** `t-e-a-c-h`. “It ends in **-ch**. Like *sh*, *ss* and *x*, **-ch** cannot take a plain -s. So: **teach → teaches**.” Write both.
> **Use:** *My mother **teaches** the child.* / *My uncles **teach** the child.* Ask which subject is one and which is more than one.

**② go** *(যাওয়া)*

> **Say:** “**Go** — to move from here to another place. We **go** to the mosque. Name or do?” → **“Do — verb.”**
> **Pronounce:** *go → goes*. Say the pair once together — the **sound** changes, not just the spelling.
> **Spell it on the board:** `g-o`. “Two letters, ending in **-o**. And **-o** also cannot take a plain -s. So: **go → goes**.” Write both — the spelling is the part to watch.
> **Use:** *My cousin **goes** to the fair.* / *The friends **go** to the fair.*

**⚑ The two words are not treated the same, and the teacher needs to know why.**

- **`go` is a proper new word this week.** It joins the W7 list, it is **spelled and tested like any other W7 word**, and it may appear in **Thursday's dictation**. It stays with the class — Block 8 needs it for *go → went*. *(PD-011 override.)*
- **`teach` is borrowed for this block only.** It is taught, and it is graded **on this block's worksheets and Performance Test** — but it is **never a dictation or spelling item**, and it is **not** on the W7 word list. *(PD-012 block-local.)*

Five endings now on the board, together: **-sh · -ss · -x · -ch · -o → add -es.**

> **Say (close the step):** “Five endings, one rule. If the verb ends in **sh, ss, x, ch** or **o**, and the subject is **one**, add **-es**.”

### Step 3 — **-ies** after consonant + y (15–20 min)

> **Say:** “One more. When the verb ends in **y**, and a consonant comes just before the y, the **y** disappears and we write **-ies**.”

Write it as a transformation, slowly:

```
carr y   →   carr ies        My uncle carries the poster.
```

> **Say:** “*Carry* — take the **y** away, put **-ies** in its place: **carries**. Say it: *carries*.”

**Teacher note.** Five verbs show this pattern — `carry · study · try · cry · fly`. **Four of them are borrowed for this block only**: they are taught and they appear on the sheets and in the test, but they are **never spelling or dictation words** and they do not join the W7 list. The rule matters more than the words — the exam may use a verb we have not held, and the spelling rule travels even when the word does not.

Write the five on the board and run the change across all of them:

```
carr y → carr ies      stud y → stud ies      tr y → tr ies
cr y  → cr ies         fl y  → fl ies
```

> **Say:** “Take the **y** away, put **-ies** in its place. Same move, five times. And remember — this only happens when the subject is **one**. *The children **study**.* Many, so the verb stays plain.”

Oral round, mixing all three spellings:

### Step 4 — **Two ways the exam asks** (20–24 min)

**Do not skip this step.** Without it the single-base items on the sheet will look to the class like a printing mistake.

Write both shapes on the board, one under the other:

```
①  The child ____ (play / plays) in the park.
②  The child ____ (play) in the park.
```

> **Say:** “Both ask the same question. In ① the exam gives you **two forms** and you **choose** one. In ② it gives you **one** form — the plain verb — and you must **make** the right form yourself. Same rule, same answer: **plays**. Only the help is different.”

**Objective.** Students choose **is/are** and **has/have** to match the subject; treat **people** and **clothes** as plural; and treat **family** and **team** as singular.

Board: `My uncle carries the poster.` / `My uncles carry the posters.`

> **Say:** “Which verb has the -ies, and why?” → one subject. “Today the same rule, but with two special verbs the exam loves.”

### Step 2 — **is / are** (4–12 min)

> **Say:** “These two do not add -s. They **change into a different word**. But the question is exactly the same one: **is the subject one, or more than one?**”

```
ONE                       MORE THAN ONE
The park is green.        The parks are green.
My cousin is strong.      My cousins are strong.
The meal is fresh.        The meals are fresh.
```

> **Say:** “One → **is**. More than one → **are**. Nothing else to learn.”

Then give them the exception before they trip on it: **`I` is one, but `I` never takes *is*. `I` takes *am*.** *I **am** happy.* Not *I is happy.*

**Teach it, do not test it.** No worksheet or Performance Test item in this block uses `am` or a first-person subject — every graded sentence is third person. The note exists to stop a bright pupil applying *one → is* to *I* and getting it wrong, not to add a form to the week's load.

# Class 4 (Wednesday) — **each + singular** · find and fix · mixed review · self-try demonstration

**Objective.** Students apply **each + singular**; find and correct a wrong agreement of any of the four types; and construct their own correct sentences.

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Re-walk all three rules |
| 2 | 4–11 | **each +** singular |
| 3 | 11–20 | Find and fix — all four types |
| 4 | 20–26 | Mixed review |
| 5 | 26–30 | Self-try demonstration (not collected) |
| 6 | 30–32 | CW-4 distribution |
| 7 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Re-walk (0–4 min)

Three questions, fast, whole class:

> “One subject — what happens to the verb?” → **“-s.”**
> “Verb ends in *sh*, *ss* or *x*?” → **“-es.”**
> “*People*?” → **“Are.”** “*Family*?” → **“Is.”**

### Step 2 — **each + singular** (4–11 min)

> **Say:** “A new word today: **each**. *Each* means we take them **one at a time**. So however many there are, *each* points at **one** — and the verb is the **one**-verb.”

```
The doctors have a form.         →   Each of the doctors has a form.
The cousins carry the jars.      →   Each of the cousins carries a jar.
The sisters collect the leaves.  →   Each sister collects the leaves.
```

> **Say:** “Look at the second one. *The cousins carry* — plain verb, many cousins. *Each of the cousins **carries*** — one at a time, so **-ies**. And look what stays plural: **cousins**. The naming word next to the verb is *many*, and the verb is still the **one**-verb. ***Each* decides, not the word beside it.**”

**Anticipated error & correction.**
> **If a student writes *each children have*:** “Two mistakes from one word. *Each* takes **one** thing after it — so *each **child***, not *each children*. And one child → **has**. *Each child has.*”

**Teacher note.** Last year’s half-yearly paper asked *Each of the students ____ (has / have) a new notebook.* Our version uses our own words — *Each of the **children** ____ (has / have)* — but the shape is identical, and the answer is **has**: *each* still points at one.

### Step 5 — Self-try demonstration (26–30 min)

> **Say:** “Now you make the sentences yourself. I give you a subject; you give me a whole sentence, correct.”
