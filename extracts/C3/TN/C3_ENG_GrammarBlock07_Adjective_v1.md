**Build notes (for future builders):**
- **Attributive only.** Every taught and graded adjective sits *before* its noun. Predicate position (*the road is wet*) is deliberately **not** taught this year — it exceeds the §2 cap and no C3 paper tests it. This differs from C4 Block 5, which teaches predicate position (C4's band + paired-pronoun scope carry it). Do not "add" predicate adjectives to C3.
- **The test is the job, not the place.** Adjective = describes a naming word (what kind / how many / which / whose). Position (before the noun) is taught as a *clue*, not the definition.
- The possessive/demonstrative/number strands are **taught in-cap but not tested by any binding paper** — Annual Q8 tests descriptive adjectives only. The 55/25/20 weighting is deliberate; do not reconcile it toward an exam that does not test them.
- *best* and *same* are held vocabulary but **excluded from graded adjective items** (comparative/superlative guardrail).
- **PT order convention:** this PT leads with **dictation (Part A)**, then grammar (Parts B–E), a single continuous A–E run. This reverses the binding-paper order (which ends on dictation) — the reversal is a deliberate house convention, not an error.
- **PT dictation = 10 words** (house standard, all three binding papers score dictation [10]) — not the ~45-item weekly-vocab figure from Drive Plan §4.

## Adjective Clue Card — `C3B07-CC`

*Built with the class across the week; on the wall by Thursday. English delivery; Bangla gloss retained as learning content (Run Book §3.14).*

| An adjective… | Ask… | Example (adjective **bold**) | বাংলা |
|---|---|---|---|
| **describes a naming word** | — | a **clean** hand · a **bright** morning | বিশেষ্যকে বর্ণনা করে |
| tells **what kind** | What kind of ___? | a **wet** road · a **cold** day | কেমন |
| tells **how many** | How many ___? | **two** books · **many** friends | কতগুলো |
| tells **which one** | Which ___? | **this** book · **that** boy | কোনটি |
| tells **whose** | Whose ___? | **my** bag · **her** book | কার |

> **The test is the job, not the place.** To find an adjective, ask: *does this word describe the noun — tell us what kind, how many, which, or whose?*

**Dual-job reminder:** a word's role comes from its **job here, in this sentence**. *clean* describing a noun = adjective (*a clean hand*); *clean* telling someone to do = verb (*clean the room*).

# Teaching Days

## Class 1 (Sunday) — What is a describing word? (*what kind / how many*)

*Oral-first. Understand level. Flow stays 5 steps — the example banks are for the teacher to pick from, not to drill in full.*

| Step | Min | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–8 | What is an adjective? |
| 2 | 8–16 | *What kind?* and *How many?* |
| 3 | 16–24 | Find the describing word |
| 4 | 24–30 | CW-1 |
| 5 | 30–35 | Exit Check (17) |

**Step 1 — What is an adjective? (0–8 min)**

Hold up a book. *"This is a book. Just a book. Now watch — I can tell you more about it. What kind of book? A new book. An old book."* Hold up a second one if you have it. *"This book is new. That one is old. The word that tells us new or old — that word describes the book."*

Write on the board and underline the describing word:
> **new** book · **old** book

*"These describing words — new, old — have an important role. They tell us what the book is like. A word that describes a naming word is called an **adjective**."* Say the word slowly, twice, and have the class say it back once (choral — no rhyme or tune, §H.2). *"An adjective tells us about a naming word. It can tell what kind, how many, which one, or whose."*

Keep it concrete — don't define "adjective" in the abstract. Anchor it on the object in your hand.

> **Teacher example bank — objects you can hold up or point to (all held nouns, natural what-kind pairings):**
> a **new** pen · an **old** bag · a **red** hat · a **clean** hand · a **wooden** mat · a **good** book · a **wet** towel · a **cold** tap · a **bright** morning (point to the window) · a **dirty** shoe
> *Pick 2–3 that match objects actually in the room. The point is: a plain naming word, then a describing word that tells what kind.*

**Step 2 — *What kind?* and *How many?* (8–16 min)**

**First, *what kind* (about 4 minutes).** Ask the class real questions about things they know, and let them answer. Each time, write the pair and underline the adjective:

*"What kind of morning is it today — bright or dark? … A bright morning."* → **bright** morning
*"After the rain, what kind of road do we walk on? … A wet road."* → **wet** road
*"When you wash well, what kind of hands do you have? … Clean hands."* → **clean** hands
*"In winter, what kind of day is it? … A cold day."* → **cold** day

**Teacher example bank — *what kind* (held W5–W6 describing words):**

| Describing word | Natural pairing (held nouns) |
|---|---|
| bright | a bright morning |
| wet | a wet road |
| clean | clean hands |
| cold | a cold day |
| red | a red bag |
| old | an old man |
| new | a new shoe |
| healthy | a healthy body |
| cozy | a cozy home |
| grassy | a grassy garden |
| polluted | a polluted river |
| hard | a hard bed |
| beautiful | a beautiful river |
| favourite | a favourite book |
| big | a big school |

*Draw on 3–4. Keep them things a child in Sylhet actually sees. (**big** and **table** are taught this block — **block-local, PD-047** — so a **big table**, a **big bag**, a **wooden table** are all fair game.)*

**Then extend to *how many* (about 4 minutes).** *"Adjectives don't only tell what kind. They can also tell **how many**. Listen: how many books are on my table? Two books. How many friends do you have at school? Many friends."* Write and underline the number word:

> **two** books · **many** friends · **some** days · **few** shoes · **all** students

*"Two, many, some, few, all — these describe the naming word too. They don't tell what kind — they tell **how many**. So an adjective can tell what kind, **or** how many."*

**Teacher example bank — *how many* (number words + held countable nouns):**

| Number word | Natural pairing (plural nouns) |
|---|---|
| two | two books |
| three | three pens |
| four | four shoes |
| many | many friends |
| some | some hens |
| few | few bags |
| all | all students |

*Use plural naming words with number words (two book**s**, many friend**s**) — quietly revisits Block 4 plurals.*

**Start the Adjective Clue Card on the wall:** add the first two rows — **what kind** (a bright morning) and **how many** (two books). Tell the class you'll add more this week.

**Step 3 — Find the describing word (16–24 min)**

Now turn it around — the children find the adjective themselves. The key move here is teaching the **test**, not a position rule:

*"To find the describing word, don't just ask 'which word comes first?' Ask the real question: **does this word describe the naming word — does it tell what kind, or how many?** If it describes, it is an adjective."*

Do a few together, pointing at the board pairs from Step 2. *"In* bright morning *— which word names? Morning. Which word describes it? Bright. Bright is the adjective."*

Then round-the-class turns — say a naming word, a child adds a describing word before it:
*"…book?"* → *"a good book"* · *"…friends?"* → *"many friends"* · *"…road?"* → *"a wet road"* · *"…day?"* → *"a cold day"*

Accept **any** sensible held describing word — there's no single right answer, that's the point. Correct only if the added word doesn't actually describe (e.g. a child says a verb like "run book" — gently: *"does 'run' tell us what the book is like? No — so it's not describing. Try again: what kind of book?"*).

> **Teacher example bank — naming words to prompt with (held):**
> book, pen, bag, shoe, hat, mat, hand, face, morning, day, road, garden, river, house, school, home, friend, brother, mother, story, hen, cat
> *Mix what-kind prompts and how-many prompts: "how many pens?" → "three pens"; "what kind of garden?" → "a grassy garden".*

**Step 4 — CW-1 (24–30 min)**

Distribute CW-1. Model **Part A item 1** on the board — read it aloud, show how to underline the describing word, do that one *with* the class. Then they work as far as they can on their own; whatever isn't finished goes home as HW-1. Move around the room; watch for children underlining the naming word instead of the describing word.

**Step 5 — Exit Check (30–35 min)**

One prompt per child (17 children). Teacher says a naming word; the child answers with a describing word before it — either a *what kind* or a *how many* word. De-pattern the prompts so answers don't fall into a rhythm.

> **Exit Check prompts (17, held nouns):**
> road · morning · hand · book · bag · boy · girl · day · garden · shoe · river · house · friend · food · mother · brother · sister
> *Accept any sensible held adjective. Note any child who hesitates or gives a non-describing word — they may need a quick recap tomorrow.*

## Class 2 (Monday) — *which* and *whose*: this/that, my/his/her/our/their before a noun

**Objective.** Students recognise that *my/his/her/our/their* and *this/that* **before a noun** describe *whose* or *which* — so they are adjectives — and find them in a passage.

| Step | Min | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall Day 1 |
| 2 | 4–12 | *whose* (কার) and *which* (কোনটি) still describe |
| 3 | 12–20 | Find them in sentences |
| 4 | 20–26 | Passage identify |
| 5 | 26–30 | CW-2 |
| 6 | 30–35 | Exit Check (17) |

**Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)**

*"Yesterday we learned what an adjective is. Who can tell me — what does an adjective do?"* → *it describes a naming word.* *"Good. And what two questions did we learn it can answer?"* → *what kind, how many.* *"Right — a bright morning, two books. Today we meet some new describing words. They might surprise you — you may not think they are adjectives at first. But they are, for the same reason: they describe the naming word."*

**Step 2 — *whose* and *which* still describe (4–12 min)**

Write three columns on the board, one line at a time, reading across:
```
a book      my book      this book
a bag       her bag      that bag
a shoe      his shoe     this shoe
```

*"Read across the first line with me. A book. My book. This book. Now — in* my book *— whose book is it?"* → *"My — it's mine."* *"So* my *tells us **whose**. And in* this book *— which book do I mean, all the books, or one?"* → *"This one."* *"So* this *tells us **which one**. Both of these words sit before the naming word, and both tell us something about it — whose, or which. That is exactly what a describing word does. So* my, your, his, her, our, their *— and* this, that *— when they come before a naming word, they are **adjectives**."*

Tie it back to the four questions: *"Remember our questions — what kind, how many, which, whose? Words like* bright *and* wet *answer* what kind. *Words like* two *and* many *answer* how many. *And now — words like* my *and* her *answer* whose, *and* this *and* that *answer* which. *All four kinds describe. All four are adjectives."*

> **Teacher example bank — *whose* (possessive adjectives, held nouns):** my bag · his pen · her book · our school · their garden · my shoe · his hand · her towel · our home · their house
> **Teacher example bank — *which* (demonstratives, held nouns):** this book · that boy · this river · that mat · this shoe · that garden · this pen · that hen
> *Say each aloud, ask "whose?" or "which?" each time. Use 4–5 from each — don't grind through the whole list.*

**Anticipated error & correction:**
> **When a child says "*my* is a pronoun":** *"I understand — it feels like one, and later, in another block, you'll meet words that really are pronouns. But look where* my *sits. Is there a naming word right after it?"* → *"Yes — bag."* → *"Then* my *is doing an adjective's job here: sitting before the naming word, telling us whose. Before a noun and describing it → adjective."*

**Teacher note.** Possessives *my/his/her/our/their* only sit before the noun (we say *my book*, not *the book is my*). Do **not** teach the *mine/hers/ours* form, or the after-*is* position — those belong to the Pronoun block. Keep the children on *my/his* **before a naming word**. *this/that* they will meet again in Block 8 standing alone (*This is mine*) — but that is not taught here.

**Add two more rows to the Clue Card:** **whose** (my bag) and **which** (this book). Now all four describing questions are on the wall.

**Step 3 — Find them in sentences (12–20 min)**

Read each sentence aloud; the class names the describing word and says whether it tells *whose* or *which*. All attributive, held nouns, house names (§H.5), single subject (no non-mahram pairing):

> my uncle *(whose)* · our school *(whose)* · this river *(which)* · her bag *(whose)* · that boy *(which)* · their garden *(whose)* · his shoe *(whose)* · this book *(which)*

*"Some of these have no* bright *or* clean *in them — but there is still a describing word. In* my uncle *— what sits before* uncle *and tells us whose? →* my. *That's today's kind of adjective."*

> **Teacher example bank — extra sentences to draw on:** my hat · her story · our city · their home · this garden · that river · his bag · this hand
> *Mix whose and which so children don't fall into "it's always my."*

**Step 4 — Passage identify (20–26 min)**

Write this short passage on the board (attributive only, both kinds mixed, §H-compliant):

> *My friend Aisha has a new book. She has a small clean room. This kind girl helps her tired mother in the green garden.*

Read it aloud once. Then: *"Find every describing word — the plain ones like* new, small*, and the whose/which ones like* My, This, her."* Underline as the class calls them out:

*My* (whose), *new* (what kind), *her* (whose), *small* (what kind), *clean* (what kind), *This* (which), *kind* (what kind), *her* (whose), *tired* (what kind), *green* (what kind).

*"Ten describing words in three little sentences. That is how many adjectives we use without even noticing."*

**Step 5 — CW-2 (26–30 min)** — Distribute CW-2, model item 1, rest → HW-2.

**Step 6 — Exit Check (30–35 min)**

One prompt per child (17). Say a short phrase; the child names the describing word and whether it tells *what kind / how many / which / whose*. De-pattern across the four answer types.

> **Exit Check prompts (17):** my bag · this road · a cold day · her shoe · that boy · a clean hand · our house · their garden · a wet road · his mat · this river · a bright morning · my brother · a hard bed · that girl · four pens · a new book

## Class 3 (Tuesday) — Mixed identify (all four kinds) + the *clean* dual-job

**Objective.** Students find the adjective in mixed sentences (what kind / how many / which / whose), and meet the idea that one word can be an adjective *or* a verb depending on its job — anchored on *clean*.

| Step | Min | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Recall — the four describing questions |
| 2 | 5–13 | Mixed identify (all four kinds) |
| 3 | 13–22 | The *clean* dual-job (adjective ↔ verb) |
| 4 | 22–30 | CW-3 |
| 5 | 30–35 | Exit Check (17) |

**Step 1 — Recall (0–5 min)**

Clue Card visible. *"Our four describing questions — an adjective can tell us…?"* → *what kind, how many, which, whose.* Point to each Clue Card row as they say it. *"And the real test — how do we know a word is a describing word? Do we look at where it sits?"* → *"No — we ask if it describes the naming word."* *"Exactly. The job it does, not the place it sits."*

**Step 2 — Mixed identify (5–13 min)**

Now all four kinds are mixed together. Write six sentences; the class finds the describing word(s) and says which question each answers. All attributive, held nouns, house names:

```
1. Aisha has a new book.             → new (what kind)
2. Two boys clean the garden.        → two (how many)
3. This road is by our school.       → This (which), our (whose)
4. My brother has many friends.      → My (whose), many (how many)
5. Her shoe is on the old mat.       → Her (whose), old (what kind)
6. Some students help their mother.  → Some (how many), their (whose)
```

*"Each sentence has one or two describing words. Find them, and tell me which question each one answers — what kind, how many, which, or whose."* Take the sentences with two adjectives slowly — *"this one has two describing words. This — which road. Our — whose school. Both are adjectives."*

> **Teacher example bank — extra mixed sentences to draw on (all held, attributive):**
> Yusuf has a red bag. *(red — what kind)* · Three hens are in the garden. *(three — how many)* · That book is on my bed. *(That — which; my — whose)* · Our garden has some flowers. *(Our — whose; some — how many)* · Abdullah has a cozy home. *(cozy — what kind)* · This pen is on the table. *(This — which)*
> *Note for teacher: keep the describing word **before** the naming word. Don't use "the road is wet" style here — that comes in a later class.*

**Step 3 — The *clean* dual-job (13–22 min)**

This is the block's key idea: **the same word can do two different jobs.** Anchor it on *clean*. Write the pair:

```
Aisha has clean hands.        Please clean the room.
```

*"Look at both sentences. The same word — clean — is in each one. But it is doing a **different job** each time. In the first: clean hands. Does* clean *describe the hands? What kind of hands? Clean hands. Yes — so here* clean *is a describing word, an **adjective**. In the second: Please clean the room. Is* clean *describing the room? No — here it tells someone to **do** something. Clean the room! It's an action. So here* clean *is a doing word, a **verb**."*

The framing sentence (from the Clue Card): *"A word's role comes from its **job here, in this sentence**. Ask: is it describing the naming word, or telling an action? Describing → adjective. Doing → verb."*

One more pair to secure it, then stop — do **not** turn this into a long drill (§N: Class 3 *introduces* dual-job, doesn't grind it):

```
Yusuf has a clean towel.   →  clean describes towel → adjective
We clean the school.      →  clean is the action → verb
```

> **If a child says *clean* is "always an adjective":** *"Let's test it. In* We clean the school *— is* clean *describing the school? Or is it what we **do** to the school?"* → *"What we do."* → *"Then here it's a doing word — a verb. The same word can do two jobs. The job decides."*

> **Teacher note — keep dual-job clean-only.** Do not extend to other words today. The dual-job idea is *introduced* through one clear anchor word (*clean*); other dual-job words are not this block's target. Graded items keep the position unambiguous (*clean* before a noun = adjective; *clean* heading an action = verb).

**Step 4 — CW-3 (22–30 min)** — Distribute CW-3, model item 1, rest → HW-3.

**Step 5 — Exit Check (30–35 min)**

One prompt per child (17). Most prompts: name the describing word and its question. Include 3–4 dual-job prompts. De-pattern so adjective/verb answers don't run.

> **Exit Check prompts (17):** a new pen *(what kind)* · many friends *(how many)* · my bag *(whose)* · this river *(which)* · a cold day *(what kind)* · three shoes *(how many)* · her hand *(whose)* · that boy *(which)* · **"In *Aisha has clean hands* — adjective or verb?"** *(adjective)* · a wet road *(what kind)* · our school *(whose)* · **"In *Clean the mat* — adjective or verb?"** *(verb)* · some hens *(how many)* · a bright morning *(what kind)* · his shoe *(whose)* · **"In *We clean the garden* — adjective or verb?"** *(verb)* · a red bag *(what kind)*

## Class 4 (Wednesday) — Find-and-correct + Self-try (demonstration)

**Objective.** Students find a wrong describing word and fix it, then rehearse the graded Self-try format.

| Step | Min | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall the four kinds + dual-job |
| 2 | 4–13 | Find the wrong / missing describing word |
| 3 | 13–24 | Self-try — demonstration |
| 4 | 24–30 | CW-4 |
| 5 | 30–35 | Exit Check (17) |

**Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)**

*"An adjective describes a naming word — it can tell what kind, how many, which, or whose. And yesterday we learned something special about one word — clean. What were its two jobs?"* → *clean hands (adjective) / clean the room (verb).* *"Right — the job decides the role."*

**Step 2 — Find the wrong / missing describing word (4–13 min)**

**First — the describing word doesn't fit.** Read a sentence where the adjective makes no sense; the class spots it and gives a sensible one:

```
1. The wet sun is very hot.      → wet doesn't fit the sun → bright / hot
2. My friend has a sad book.     → a book isn't sad → new / old / good
3. Two of the boy play football. → two needs a plural naming word → boys
```

*"Listen carefully — something is wrong in each sentence. In the first: the wet sun. Can the sun be wet? No. What describing word fits the sun? →* bright, hot."

> **Teacher example bank — "doesn't fit" sentences to draw on:**
> The grassy pen is on the table. → *grassy doesn't fit a pen → red / new* · Aisha has a polluted bag. → *polluted doesn't fit a bag → dirty / old* · The cozy river runs fast. → *cozy doesn't fit a river → wet / cold*
> *Number-agreement type: Many cat sat on the mat → cat**s**; Three hen are here → hen**s**. Ties how-many back to Block 4 plurals.*

**Then — the describing word is missing.** The class adds one (keep it **before** the naming word):

```
4. Porshi has a ___ room.            → clean / small / cozy
5. We saw ___ birds in the garden. → many / two / some
```

**Step 3 — Self-try demonstration (13–24 min)**

This is the block's *create* moment — demonstrated today, graded Thursday. Model the **exact** PT format on the board so Thursday holds no surprises.

> **Self-try (demonstration).** *Choose a describing word you know. (1) Write the word and its role. (2) Write one sentence using it before a naming word. (3) Write why it is an adjective.*

Think aloud, slowly, writing each part:
*"Let me choose* bright*. Step one — the word is* bright*, and its role is* **adjective**. *Step two — a sentence with* bright *before a naming word:* The bright morning is cool*. Step three — why is it an adjective? Because* bright *describes the morning — it tells us what kind of morning."*

Then a quick second one using a *how many* word, so children see the new category counts:
*"Now* many*. Its role — adjective. A sentence:* Many friends came to our school*. Why — it tells us **how many** friends."*

Point at the 4-mark rubric on the board (content · reasoning · organisation · word-class placement) so they know what earns marks. **Don't collect** — this is rehearsal.

> **Teacher example bank — words a child might pick (all held, all four kinds):**
> what kind: new, old, clean, red, cold, bright, beautiful, cozy · how many: two, three, many, some · whose: my, her, our · which: this, that
> *If a child picks a whose/which word, that's fine — remind them to put a naming word right after it (my bag, this book) and to say what it tells (whose / which).*

**Step 4 — CW-4 (24–30 min)** — Distribute CW-4, model item 1, rest → HW-4.

**Step 5 — Exit Check (30–35 min)**

One prompt per child (17): a mix of find-the-adjective, name-its-question, and 2–3 "fix the describing word." De-pattern.

> **Exit Check prompts (17):** a bright day · my shoe · **"fix: the wet sun"** *(→ bright/hot)* · three pens · her bag · this garden · a cold hand · many stories · **"fix: a sad book"** *(→ new/old)* · our home · that river · a new hat · some friends · **"fix: Two of the boy"** *(→ boys)* · his mat · a clean face · this pen

## Self-Construction (Self-Try) Task — graded on Thursday PT — `C3B07` Part E

*Charter §I.1–I.4 · 4 marks · PD-010 (value alignment not scored).*

> **Write your own.**
> 1. Choose a describing word you know. Write the word and its **role**.
> 2. Write **one** sentence using it **before** a naming word.
> 3. Write **why** it is an adjective (what does it describe?).

**Rubric (4 marks, 1 each — uniform, Charter §I.3):**

| Criterion | 1 mark if… |
|---|---|
| Content accuracy | The sentence is correct and the word is genuinely an adjective |
| Reasoning | The "why" correctly states what the word describes / which question it answers (Class 3: evaluated, not just present) |
| Organisation | The three parts are given in order; sentence well-formed |
| Correct word-class placement | The word is named "adjective" **and** used before a noun (judged against the role the student named — §I.4) |

*Value alignment not scored; a §H conflict is corrected as a values matter, never marked down (§I.3 · Run Book §13.1).*
