## Teacher checklist

- [ ] **English throughout.** Bangla only as a marked note or a wall-chart gloss.
- [ ] Teach only to the §2 cap. **The correlative *"the more… the more…"* is shown once, Wednesday, as a whole sentence — never a blank, never on a worksheet, never in the PT.**
- [ ] **No abstract zero-article rule-naming.** Students produce **x** correctly; they never explain *why* x applies. Do not ask "why is there no article here?"
- [ ] Sunday re-walks **Block 5 countability** and **Block 4 proper nouns** — both carry the whole zero-article layer on Tuesday. Do not skip.
- [ ] **Say every exemplar aloud before writing it.** The ear leads, the eye follows. This is the block's single method.
- [ ] Affirm the a-e-i-o-u **letter** rule the class already holds; present sound as the sharper tool, **not** as a correction of what they were taught.
- [ ] Expect over-application: a child who learns *an hour* will write *an hero*. Correct by ear every time — "Do you hear the h? Then **a**."
- [ ] Graded items use **held words (W1–W5) or declared exemplars** only.
- [ ] **Sacred-word guard (§H.3): Allah, Quran, Ramadan, Hajj and Zakat never appear as an article blank** — in any worksheet, the PT, or the Assignment. They may appear in teacher prose only.

- [ ] **Do not use** `team`, `temple` (Block 5 exclusions) in any authored item. **`festival` is Islamic-calendar use only** (Principal ruling 01.08.26) — Eid and Ramadan contexts, never other-faith festivals.
- [ ] Non-mahram screen: Yusuf and Abdullah never paired with Aisha in any sentence.
- [ ] Each day closes with its Exit Check — **8 prompts, one per student** (Run Book §9.4).
- [ ] Unfinished classwork goes home with the homework (§9.3) — do not rush a lesson to finish CW in class.
- [ ] Wednesday's self-try is a **rehearsal**, not collected; the graded self-try is Thursday's PT Part E.

## The Article Clue Card — `C5B06-CC`

Built with the class across the week — rows 1–4 by Tuesday, row 5 Wednesday. Bangla glosses permitted on a wall reference (§3.14).

> ### WHICH ARTICLE?
>
> **1. Is it a name? → x (no article)**
> *নামের আগে সাধারণত কোনো article বসে না।*
> Bangladesh · Kishoreganj · Indonesia · Yusuf
> ⚠ **But river names and some country short-forms take *the*** — see row 3.
>
> ---
>
> **2. Can you count it? No → x (no article)**
> *গোনা যায় না, আর কোনটা বলিনি — তাই article বসে না।*
> water · honey · money · rice · patience · mercy · kindness · science
> *(From Block 5 — if you cannot ask "How many?", you cannot put **a** in front of it.)*
>
> ---
>
> **3. Do we know which one? → the**
> *আমরা কোনটা বুঝি — তাই **the** বসে।*
>
> | Three ways we know | Example |
> |---|---|
> | We already said it | *Yusuf bought a storybook. **The** storybook was about a hero.* |
> | We say which one | *the reporter from Kishoreganj* · *the wall of the garden* |
> | There is only one of it | *the sun · the moon · the earth* |
>
> **Also take *the*:** river names — *the Padma · the Meghna · the Buriganga* · these country names — *the UAE · the UK · the USA*
>
> ---
>
> **4. Any one of many? → a or an**
> **Listen to the first SOUND, not the letter.** — *প্রথম **শব্দ** শোনো, অক্ষর নয়।*
>
> | You HEAR a vowel sound → **an** | You HEAR a consonant sound → **a** |
> |---|---|
> | **an** island, **an** email, **an** animal | **a** library, **a** garden, **a** tourist |
> | **an** announcement, **an** election | **a** museum, **a** river, **a** prize |
> | **an** hour *(h is silent)* | **a** hero *(we hear the h)* |
> | **an** honest reporter *(h is silent)* | **a** historical fort *(we hear the h)* |
> | **an** heir *(h is silent)* | **a** university *(sounds like "yu")* |
> | **an** M.B.B.S. *(sounds like "em")* | **a** European country *(sounds like "yu")* |
> | | **a** one-eyed deer · **a** one-taka coin *(both sound like "wan")* |
>
> **Say the word aloud. Your ears decide, not your eyes.**
> *জোরে বলো। চোখ দিয়ে নয়, কানে শুনে সিদ্ধান্ত নাও।*
>
> ---
>
> **5. One person with two jobs? → *the* only once**
> *একজন মানুষের দুটি পদ — **the** একবারই বসে।*
>
> **Look at the verb first.** — *আগে verb দেখো।*
>
> | You see | It means | So write |
> |---|---|---|
> | **is** | **ONE** person doing two jobs | ***The*** Headmaster and Secretary **is** coming. → **one** *the* |
> | **are** | **TWO** people | ***The*** Headmaster and ***the*** Secretary **are** coming. → **two** *the*s |
>
> **One person → one *the*. Two people → two *the*s.**
> *একজন → একটি the · দুজন → দুটি the*

# Class 1 (Sunday) — What an article is, and *a / an* by sound

**Objective.** Students know what an article is, name the three, and choose between *a* and *an* by listening to the first **sound** of the word that follows — including the words where the eye and the ear disagree.

**Exemplars today:** `university · European · one-eyed · one-taka · heir`
**Held words leaned on today:** `hour · honest · hero · historical` (the h-pair contrast comes free from the held set)

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–5 | Re-walk: countable, uncountable, names |
| 2 | 5–10 | What an article is — the three words |
| 3 | 10–14 | The letter rule you already hold |
| 4 | 14–18 | Ears before eyes |
| 5 | 18–27 | The words where eye and ear disagree |
| 6 | 27–28 | Listening drill |
| 7 | 28–32 | CW-1 |
| 8 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

**Note on staging.** Today teaches **a** and **an** only. *The* is named in Step 2 so the class knows the full set, but it is **not** taught and **not** graded until Monday. **x** is not mentioned at all today. CW-1 and HW-1 require *a* or *an* only.

### Step 1 — Re-walk (0–5 min)

**Books closed.** Write on the board:

```
flower   water   Bangladesh   library   money   Kishoreganj   honey   museum
```

> "Three questions, quickly. Which of these can we count — one, two, three?"

Expected: *flower, library, museum*.

> "Which cannot be counted?"

Expected: *water, money, honey* — last week's work.

> "And which two are names?"

Expected: *Bangladesh, Kishoreganj*.

> "Good. Hold those three ideas — **countable**, **uncountable**, and **names**. You will need all three this week, and Tuesday's whole lesson is built on them."

**Teacher note.** This re-walk is not decoration. Tuesday's zero-article work is entirely built on it: a child who cannot tell *money* from *museum* cannot decide between **x** and **a**. If the class is shaky here, spend the extra two minutes now and take them from Step 6.

### Step 2 — What an article is (5–10 min)

**(a) The idea (5–8).** Write one sentence on the board, complete:

```
Yusuf borrowed a storybook from the library.
                ─                ───
```

Underline **a** and **the** as you say:

> "Read this with me." Class reads the sentence aloud.

> "Now look at the two words I have underlined — **a** and **the**. They are small. You have written them a thousand times. But they have a name, and today you learn it."

> "Words like these are called **articles**. An article is a small word that goes in front of a noun."

> "Point to the nouns in this sentence." → *storybook, library.*

> "Good. **a** goes in front of *storybook*. **the** goes in front of *library*. That is the job of an article — it stands in front of a noun and tells us something about it."

> "And there are only **three** articles in the whole of English. Only three."

Write them large:

```
a        an        the
```

> "You have already met two of them in one short sentence. The third is **an**, and you will meet it before the lesson ends."

> "Three little words. But they are in almost every sentence you will ever write, and the exam asks about them every single year. This week is about knowing exactly which one to use."

**(b) The split (8–10).** Draw a line down the board.

> "**a** and **an** mean *any one of many*. **The** means *the one we mean* — the particular one."

```
Yusuf borrowed a storybook.     → any storybook, we do not know which
Yusuf borrowed the storybook.   → the particular one, we both know which
```

> "Say the difference back to me in your own words."

> "Today we work on **a** and **an** only. *The* is Monday's work. But now you know all three exist."

**Teacher note.** Do not let *the* leak into today's practice, even in answers. If a student uses it, say "that is Monday's word — today, *a* or *an*." Say it warmly; the instinct is correct, it is only early.

### Step 3 — The letter rule you already hold (10–14 min)

> "Now — when do we write **a**, and when do we write **an**? You already know a rule. Tell me."

Someone will say the vowel rule. Draw it out.

> "Yes. Say the five vowels for me." → **a, e, i, o, u**

Write:

```
vowels:  a  e  i  o  u          →  an
everything else (consonants)    →  a
```

> "That rule is correct. It has served you well and we are not throwing it away."

Work through eight held words on the board, class answering chorally:

```
______ island          →  an        ______ animal          →  an
______ garden          →  a         ______ museum          →  a
______ email           →  an        ______ announcement    →  an
______ tourist         →  a         ______ election        →  an
```

> "All eight correct. Now say them aloud with me." Class reads the list.

> "Notice something — they **sound** right too. Your eyes said *an island*, and your ears agree."

**Teacher note.** Four minutes is enough because Step 2 has already had the class handling **a** and **the** in a real sentence and naming the nouns they sit in front of. What matters here is not new learning but *confidence* — every child answering, the old rule affirmed rather than corrected. A child who feels corrected will stop volunteering, and Step 4 only lands on a class that is still putting hands up.

### Step 4 — Ears before eyes (14–18 min)

> "So the letter rule works. Almost always. Today I am going to give you a **sharper** tool — one that works *every* time, including the few places the letter rule slips."

Write on the board, and leave it there all week:

```
LISTEN TO THE FIRST SOUND, NOT THE FIRST LETTER.
```

> "Watch. I am going to say a word, and I want you to tell me only one thing — is the **first sound** a vowel sound or a consonant sound? Do not look at spelling. Close your eyes if it helps."

Say each aloud clearly; class answers before anything is written:

- **"Island."** → *eye*- → vowel sound → **an island** ✓
- **"Museum."** → *myu*- → consonant sound → **a museum** ✓
- **"Hero."** → *h*- → consonant sound → **a hero** ✓
- **"Hour."** → *ow*- → **vowel sound** → **an hour**

Stop on *hour*. Write it up.

> "Look at the spelling. **H-O-U-R.** *H* is not a vowel letter. But we do not pronounce the **h** in *hour*. The word begins with the sound *ow*. Your **ear** heard a vowel. So — **an hour**."

> "This is why the ear is the sharper tool. When the eye and the ear disagree, **the ear wins**."

Write the contrast pair and have the class read it twice:

```
an hour        a hero
```

> "Same letter at the front. Different sound. Different article. In *hour* the h is silent. In *hero* you hear it clearly — *heh-ro*. Your ear knows."

### Step 5 — The words where eye and ear disagree (18–27 min)

> "There are only a small number of words like this, and you will meet them in your exam. We will learn them by ear, four today, more on Tuesday."

Each one: **say it first, discuss, write it after.**

**(a) *honest* — held word (18–20).**

**Say** *honest*.

> "What sound comes out first?" → **on-** → vowel.

> "Yes — the **h** is silent again, like in *hour*. So: **an honest reporter**. **an honest neighbour**."

```
an honest reporter      an honest neighbour      an honest promise
```

> "*Honest* means truthful — a person who does not lie. It is a quality Allah loves in a believer."

**(b) *heir* — exemplar (20–22).**

**Say** *heir* — pronounced *air*.

> "Listen. **Air.** Do you hear an *h*?" → **No.**

> "Silent again. Third one. **an heir**."

> "*An heir* is the person who will receive what a parent leaves behind — property, or a responsibility, or a trust. In our exam paper you will see: *He is **an** heir of a respected Islamic scholar.*"

Write the silent-h family together and read it chorally:

```
an hour        an honest man        an heir
```

> "Three words, one habit — the h hides. **an** every time."

**(c) *university* and *European* — exemplars (22–25).**

**Say** *university*.

> "What sound comes out first?" — let them try. Someone will say *u*, a vowel.

> "Say it again slowly. **Yu-ni-ver-si-ty.** What is that first sound? *Yu.* Is *yu* a vowel sound or a consonant sound?"

Let them work it out. It is a consonant sound — the *y*-sound.

> "So even though the letter **U** is a vowel letter, the *sound* is a consonant. **a university.** Say it with me — *a university.*"

> "*A university* is a place where students study after college."

**Say** *European*.

> "**Yu-ro-pean.** The same *yu* sound. So — **a European country**."

```
a university        a European country
```

> "*European* means from Europe — a part of the world far to the west of us. Your paper asks: *She visited **a** European country to attend an Islamic conference.*"

**(d) *one-eyed* and *one-taka* — exemplars (25–27).**

**Say** *one*.

> "**Wan.** What is that first sound?" → *w* → consonant.

> "Even though it is spelled with **O**, a vowel letter, we say *wan*. Consonant sound. So — **a one-eyed deer**."

```
a one-eyed deer        a one-taka coin        a one-minute wait
```

> "*One-eyed* means having only one eye. *A one-taka coin* is a coin worth one taka — you have all held one."

> "Say all three. Every word that begins with *one* starts with the **wan** sound, so every one of them takes **a**."

**Now the full contrast**, written together, class reading it twice aloud:

```
SILENT h  →  an     hour (ow) · honest (on) · heir (air)
HEARD  h  →  a      hero (heh)
"yu"      →  a      university · European
"wan"     →  a      one-eyed · one-taka
```

> "Eight words. In every one, the letter at the front says one thing and the sound says another — and **the ear wins every time**."

**Teacher note.** *Hero* is the trap in reverse and it is in the held vocabulary, so students will meet it constantly. Once a child learns *an hour*, they will start writing *an hero*, *an historical fort*. Correct it by ear, never by rule: "Say it. Do you hear the h? Then **a**."

### Step 6 — Listening drill (27–28 min)

Rapid-fire, no writing. Teacher says the noun; class calls out *a* or *an*. One round, brisk.

```
library · hour · island · hero · university · email · honest reporter · museum
animal · heir · garden · one-eyed deer · announcement · European country · tourist · historical fort
```

> "Last one — *historical fort*. Say it. Do you hear the h? **Yes.** So?" → **a historical fort.**

### Step 7 — CW-1 (28–32 min)

Distribute CW-1. Model item 1 on the board, saying the word aloud before writing the article.

> "Every item today is *a* or *an*. Nothing else. When you are unsure, **say the word out loud under your breath** — then write."

### Step 8 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

One prompt per student, briskly. Reasons in two or three words, not sentences.

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ___ hour | an | silent *h* |
| 2 | ___ library | a | *l* sound |
| 3 | ___ honest reporter | an | silent *h* |
| 4 | ___ university | a | *yu* sound |
| 5 | ___ island | an | vowel sound |
| 6 | ___ hero | a | we hear the *h* |
| 7 | ___ European country | a | *yu* sound |
| 8 | ___ email | an | vowel sound |

Spread: **an** ×4 · **a** ×4. No run beyond 2.

**If Step 8 runs short**, trim the Step 6 drill to the first line only — not Step 5(d), and never Step 1.

# Class 2 (Monday) — *the*: the one we mean

**Objective.** Students use **the** for a noun both speaker and listener can identify — by three routes: it was already mentioned, we say which one, or there is only one of it. They also learn that river names and three country names take **the**.

**Exemplars today:** `the sun · the moon · the earth · the Padma · the Meghna · the Buriganga · the UAE · the UK · the USA`

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall *a / an* by sound |
| 2 | 4–9 | What *the* means |
| 3 | 9–18 | Three ways we know which one |
| 4 | 18–22 | Only one of it — sun, moon, earth |
| 5 | 22–27 | Names that take *the* — rivers and three country names |
| 6 | 27–28 | Clue Card rows 3–4 |
| 7 | 28–32 | CW-2 |
| 8 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

**Note on staging.** Today teaches **the**, and mixes it with *a/an* from Sunday. **x is still not mentioned.** CW-2 and HW-2 require *a*, *an* or *the* only.

### Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)

Clue Card row 4 visible. Rapid oral round, no writing:

```
hour · library · honest neighbour · university · island · hero · European country · one-eyed deer · email · historical fort
```

> "Good. One rule, and it never changes: **say the word, then decide**. The ear beats the eye."

> "Yesterday you learned two of the three articles. Today you learn the third — and it is the one the exam asks about most."

### Step 2 — What *the* means (4–9 min)

**(a) The pair (4–7).** Write two sentences, one under the other:

```
I read a book.
I read the book Yusuf gave me.
```

> "Read both. One word is different. But look further — the second sentence tells you something the first does not. What?"

Draw it out: *Yusuf gave me.*

> "In the first — **a book** — you do not know which book. Any book. One of many. In the second — **the book** — you know exactly which one, because the sentence says so: *the book Yusuf gave me*."

Write the definition large and leave it up:

```
a / an  =  any one of many
the     =  the one we mean  ·  the particular one
```

> "That is what **the** does. It means **the one we mean** — the particular one. Sometimes the sentence tells you which one. Sometimes we said it earlier. Sometimes there is only one of it in the whole world. We will meet all three ways in a moment."

**(b) The test (7–9).**

> "Here is how you check. Ask yourself: *can my listener point to it?* If yes — **the**. If they would have to ask 'which one?' — **a** or **an**."

```
I saw a tiger at the zoo.           →  which tiger? we do not know. → a
The tiger was large and quiet.      →  now we know — the one just mentioned. → the
```

> "Say that pair with me." Class reads both aloud.

### Step 3 — Three ways we know which one (9–18 min)

> "There are exactly three ways you and I can both know which one. Only three. Learn these and you will never be lost."

Build the list on the board as you go, one route at a time.

**(a) Route one — we already said it (9–13).**

```
Yusuf borrowed a storybook from the library.
The storybook was about a brave champion.
```

> "First sentence — **a storybook**. Which one? You do not know yet. Second sentence — **the storybook**. Now you know: the one Yusuf just borrowed."

> "This is the commonest pattern in all of English. **First time: a. After that: the.**"

Two more, class supplying the second article:

```
Abdullah wrote a poem for the competition.  ______ poem was about kindness to a neighbour.
A swarm of bees flew across the garden.     ______ bees settled on a flower.
```

Answers: **The** poem · **The** bees.

> "Notice — the second time, you are not learning about a new poem. You are being told more about the same poem."

**(b) Route two — we say which one (13–16).**

> "You already met this one, in our very first pair — *the book Yusuf gave me*. We did not need to mention the book before. **We said which one in the same sentence**."

```
the wall of the garden
the reporter from Kishoreganj
the map on the board
the direction of the mosque
the winner of the race
```

> "Look at each. *The wall* — which wall? **Of the garden.** The sentence tells you. *The reporter* — which reporter? **From Kishoreganj.** The words after the noun say which one."

Have the class complete three:

```
______ prize for the champion
______ station near the beach
______ meal my parents cook on Eid
```

All three: **the**.

> "In each one, the words that follow point at exactly one thing. That is enough for **the**."

**Teacher note.** This route is the source of the commonest article error in an exam — a student writes *a* where the following phrase has already fixed which one. Keep saying: **"read to the end of the sentence before you choose."**

**(c) Route three — there is only one of it (16–18).**

> "And the third way — sometimes there is only **one** of the thing in the whole world. Then we never need to explain which one."

Write the three routes as a permanent list:

```
WE KNOW WHICH ONE BECAUSE...
1. we already said it        →  a storybook ... the storybook
2. we say which one          →  the wall of the garden
3. there is only one of it   →  (next)
```

### Step 4 — Only one of it (18–22 min)

**(a) The three (18–21).** Write them one at a time, saying each aloud.

```
the sun        the moon        the earth
```

> "How many suns are there?" → **One.**
> "How many moons do we see in our sky?" → **One.**
> "How many earths do we live on?" → **One.**

> "There is only one of each. So we never say *a sun* — nobody would ask 'which sun?' We always say **the sun**."

> "In your exam paper you will find: *______ sun rises in the east.* And now you know the answer without thinking. **The sun.**"

Sentences, read aloud by the class:

```
The sun rises in the east.
The moon shines at night.
Allah created the earth and everything on it.
The sun gives us light by Allah's Will.
```

**(b) Mixed practice (21–22).** Oral, whole class:

```
Yusuf saw ______ tiger at the zoo.             →  a
______ tiger was blue-eyed and quiet.          →  The
______ moon shines at night.                   →  The
```

### Step 5 — Names that take *the* (22–27 min)

**(a) River names (22–25).**

> "You know that names of people and places are special. Most of them behave one way — and we will deal with that tomorrow. But **river names** do something you must learn today."

Write:

```
a river          →  any river
the Padma        →  the name of one river
the Meghna       →  the name of one river
the Buriganga    →  the name of one river
```

> "*A river* — which river? Any. But *the Padma* — there is only one Padma. River names always take **the**. Always."

> "Say them with me." Class reads: *the Padma, the Meghna, the Buriganga.*

Sentences:

```
The Padma is a large river.
The Meghna flows through many districts.
The Buriganga flows past Dhaka.
The Meghna is wider than a small river near our school.
```

> "In your exam you will see: *______ Meghna is a big river.* And: *______ Buriganga flows through Dhaka.* Both take **the**."

**(b) Country names that take *the* (25–27).**

> "One more group. Most country names take nothing in front — and we will see that tomorrow. But a few take **the**. They are a short list, and you learn them the way you learned the river names."

```
the UAE   =   the United Arab Emirates
the UK    =   the United Kingdom
the USA   =   the United States of America
```

> "Three names. Each one can be written the long way or as letters, and **both ways take the**. *The United Kingdom* — or *the UK*. *The United States of America* — or *the USA*."

> "The UAE is a Muslim country in the Middle East. The UK and the USA are far to the west of us. Many people from Bangladesh live and work in all three."

```
The UAE is a Muslim country.
Many people from Bangladesh work in the UAE.
My uncle studies in the UK.
The USA is very far from Bangladesh.
```

> "Say them — *the UAE, the UK, the USA*. Never *a UAE*. Never just *UK* on its own."

> "Plain country names — Bangladesh, Indonesia — behave differently, and that is tomorrow's lesson. Today: **river names take the, and these three country names take the**."

### Step 6 — Clue Card rows 3 and 4 (27–28 min)

Add rows 3 and 4 to the wall card with the class, reading each line aloud as it goes up. Row 3's three routes are copied from the board list; the "also take *the*" line records the rivers and the three country names.

### Step 7 — CW-2 (28–32 min)

Distribute CW-2. Model item 1.

> "Today's sheet uses all three: **a**, **an**, **the**. Two rules together — say the word for *a* or *an*, and read to the end of the sentence for *the*."

### Step 8 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |

| 1 | ___ sun rises in the east | The | only one |
| 2 | Aisha planted ___ flower | a | any one |
| 3 | ___ Padma is a large river | The | river name |
| 4 | ___ hour of quiet study | an | silent *h* |
| 5 | ___ map on the board | The | we say which one |
| 6 | ___ UAE is a Muslim country | The | country name |
| 7 | Yusuf borrowed ___ storybook | a | first time |

# Class 3 (Tuesday) — When no article is needed: **x**

**Objective.** Students write **x** where English uses no article — before plain names and before uncountable nouns used in general — and complete the sound-exception set with the letter-name cases.

**Exemplars today:** `M.B.B.S. · S.M.S. · star` + carried from Block 5: `water · honey · money · rice`

| Step | Time | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–4 | Recall *a / an / the* |
| 2 | 4–9 | Discovery: some nouns take nothing |
| 3 | 9–14 | Plain names take **x** |
| 4 | 14–21 | Uncountables take **x** |
| 5 | 21–26 | The last sound exceptions — *an M.B.B.S.* · *an S.M.S.* |
| 6 | 26–28 | The four-question path, whole |
| 7 | 28–32 | CW-3 |
| 8 | 32–35 | Exit Check |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–4 min)

Clue Card rows 3–4 visible. Oral round:

```
___ hour · ___ sun · ___ university · ___ Padma · ___ island · ___ wall of the garden · ___ hero · ___ UAE
```

Answers: an · the · a · the · an · the · a · the.

### Step 2 — Discovery: some nouns take nothing (4–9 min)

**Books closed.** Write six sentences, articles missing, and ask the class to fill them aloud:

```
1. Yusuf visited ______ museum in Dhaka.
2. Yusuf visited ______ Bangladesh last year.
3. Aisha drank ______ glass of water.
4. Aisha drank ______ water before her prayer.
5. Abdullah bought ______ storybook.
6. Abdullah showed ______ kindness to a neighbour.
```

Let them try 2, 4 and 6. They will struggle, or produce something that sounds wrong.

> "Say number two out loud. *A Bangladesh?* *The Bangladesh?* Do either of those sound right?"

Let them hear it fail.

> "They do not. And here is why: **sometimes English uses no article at all.** The blank stays empty."

> "When that happens, we write a small **x** in the blank. It means *no article needed here*. Your exam paper will tell you to do exactly that."

Fill them in:

```
1. a museum          2. x Bangladesh
3. a glass of water  4. x water
5. a storybook       6. x kindness
```

> "So now you have **four** answers to choose from, not three: **a**, **an**, **the**, and **x**. Today is about knowing when the answer is x."

> "There are two groups. Names, and things you cannot count. That is all. Two groups."

### Step 3 — Plain names take **x** (9–14 min)

**(a) The pattern (9–12).** Write:

```
x Bangladesh      x Kishoreganj      x Indonesia      x Yusuf      x Aisha
```

> "Plain names of people, countries, districts and towns — nothing in front. Not *a*, not *the*. **x**."

Sentences, class reading aloud:

```
Yusuf lives in x Bangladesh.
The reporter from x Kishoreganj wrote a message.
x Abdullah won the competition.
```

> "Say each one without any little word before the name. It sounds right, because that is how English works."

**(b) The contrast that matters (12–14).**

> "But yesterday I taught you two names that **do** take *the*. Who remembers?"

Draw out: river names, and the three country names.

Write the contrast side by side:

```
NO ARTICLE (x)              TAKES the
x Bangladesh                the Padma
x Kishoreganj               the Meghna
x Indonesia                 the Buriganga
x Dhaka                     the UAE · the UK · the USA
```

> "Plain names — **x**. River names — **the**. These country names — **the**. Learn the right-hand column as a short list; everything else on the left."

> "Say the whole right column." Class reads: *the Padma, the Meghna, the Buriganga, the UAE, the UK, the USA.*

**Teacher note.** Do not try to explain *why* rivers take *the*. There is no child-accessible reason and the cap forbids the theory. It is a short learned list — treat it exactly like the silent-*h* words.

### Step 4 — Uncountables take **x** (14–21 min)

**(a) The bridge from Block 5 (14–17).**

> "Last week you learned that some nouns cannot be counted. Give me four."

Expected from Block 5: *water, honey, money, rice* — plus held *patience, mercy, kindness, science, energy, gratitude, happiness*.

> "Good. Now here is the link. Remember the test we used — **can I put *a* in front of it?** *A water?* No. *A honey?* No. If you cannot put *a* in front of it, and you are not saying which one — the answer is **x**."

Write:

```
x water     x honey     x money     x rice
x patience  x mercy     x kindness  x science    x energy
```

> "Say each one. *Yusuf drank water.* *Aisha showed kindness.* No little word. **x**."

Sentences read aloud:

```
Aisha drank x water before her prayer.
Abdullah collected x money for a poor neighbour.
Yusuf showed x patience when his sister was noisy.
A believer is grateful for x mercy from Allah.
We eat x rice with x vegetable at every meal.
x Science is an interesting subject.
```

**(b) The sharp edge (17–21).** This is the part that decides marks.

> "But careful — an uncountable noun takes **the** when we **say which one**. Watch."

```
Aisha drank x water before her prayer.        →  water in general
Aisha drank the water in the glass.           →  which water? in the glass.
```

> "Read them both. In the first, water in general. In the second, we said **which** water — *in the glass*. So it is **the**."

Three more pairs, class deciding aloud:

```
Abdullah collected x money for the poor.
Abdullah counted the money in the box.

We keep x honey at home.
The honey my mother bought is fresh.

Yusuf needs x energy to climb the wall.
The energy of the champion was pleasant to watch.
```

> "So the question is never just *can I count it*. It is: **can I count it, and have we said which one?**"

**Teacher note.** This is where students lose exam marks — they learn "uncountable → x" as a slogan and then write x where the sentence has already said which one. Always finish reading the sentence. Practise this pattern more than any other today.

### Step 5 — The last sound exceptions: *an M.B.B.S.* · *an S.M.S.* (21–26 min)

> "One more word where the eye and the ear disagree. Then your exception list is complete."

**Say** *M.B.B.S.* — *em-bee-bee-ess*.

> "Listen to the very first sound. **Em.** Is *em* a vowel sound or a consonant sound?"

Let them work it out — it is a vowel sound.

> "Yes. The letter **M** is not a vowel letter. But we do not say *muh* — we say the letter's **name**, *em*. And *em* begins with a vowel sound. So — **an M.B.B.S.**"

```
He is an M.B.B.S.
```

> "*M.B.B.S.* is the degree a person earns to become a doctor. When we say someone *is an M.B.B.S.*, we mean they have completed that study."

> "Your exam paper asks exactly this: *He is ______ M.B.B.S.* You now know: **an**."

**A second one, to prove it is the letter's name (24–25).**

**Say** *S.M.S.* — *ess-em-ess*.

> "Another one. What is the very first sound?" → **ess** → a vowel sound.

> "So — **an S.M.S.** An *S.M.S.* is a short message you send on a phone."

```
an M.B.B.S.        an S.M.S.
```

> "Now look at both. One begins with **M**, one begins with **S** — different letters. But we do not say *muh* or *suh*. We say the letter's **name** — *em*, *ess* — and both names begin with a vowel sound. So both take **an**."

> "That is the rule. With letters, **say the letter's name, then listen to it**."

**The full exception list, complete.** Write it and have the class read it twice:

```
SILENT h  →  an        an hour · an honest reporter · an heir
HEARD h   →  a         a hero · a historical fort
"yu"      →  a         a university · a European country
"wan"     →  a         a one-eyed deer · a one-taka coin
LETTER    →  an        an M.B.B.S. · an S.M.S.
```

> "Eleven words. That is the whole list. It does not grow."

**And the star sentence (25–26).** Write the paper's own sentence:

```
The moon is not a star; it reflects the light of the sun.
```

> "Three articles in one sentence. Find them."

Draw out: *the moon* — only one · *a star* — one of many, there are countless stars · *the light of the sun* — we say which light, and there is only one sun.

> "This one sentence uses everything you have learned in three days. Read it aloud together."

> "And notice — *it reflects the light of the sun*. The moon does not make light. Allah gave the sun its light, and the moon only throws it back to us."

### Step 6 — The four-question path, whole (26–28 min)

Build the decision path on the board, then run the class down it.

```
1. Is it a plain name?              →  x       (but rivers and the UAE/UK/USA take the)
2. Can you count it?      No        →  x       (unless we say which one → the)
3. Do we know which one?  Yes       →  the
4. Any one of many?                 →  a / an  — by SOUND
```

Rapid oral round, students naming the answer **and** the step number:

```
___ Bangladesh (1 → x)          ___ hour (4 → an)
___ money in the box (2 → the)  ___ Padma (1 → the)
___ kindness (2 → x)            ___ M.B.B.S. (4 → an)
```

> "Read the exam instruction with me: *Fill in the blanks with appropriate articles.* And on another paper: *use x if no article is needed.* Both mean the same thing. When nothing is needed — write **x**. Do not leave it blank."

### Step 7 — CW-3 (28–32 min)

Distribute CW-3.

> "Four answers now: **a**, **an**, **the**, **x**. Walk down the four questions in order every time."

### Step 8 — Exit Check (32–35 min)

| # | Prompt | Answer | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ___ Bangladesh | x | plain name |
| 2 | ___ M.B.B.S. | an | *em* sound |
| 3 | ___ water in the glass | the | we said which one |
| 4 | ___ kindness | x | cannot count it |
| 5 | ___ Meghna | the | river name |
| 6 | ___ hero of the story | a | we hear the *h* |

# Class 4 (Wednesday) — Two titles, one person · error-find · self-try rehearsal

**Objective.** Students apply all four answers in the exam's own format, use the verb as the clue when two titles name one person, find and correct article errors, and rehearse the self-try they will be graded on Thursday.

| Step | Time | Focus |

| 2 | 3–11 | Two titles, one person |
| 3 | 11–14 | One sentence to recognise |
| 4 | 14–20 | CW-4 Part A — error-find |
| 5 | 20–26 | CW-4 Part B — mixed exam format |
| 6 | 26–32 | Self-try rehearsal |
| 7 | 32–33 | HW-4 distribution |
| 8 | 33–35 | Exit Check + PT preview |

### Step 1 — Recall (0–3 min)

Clue Card fully visible.

```
___ Indonesia · ___ hour · ___ Buriganga · ___ mercy · ___ prize for the champion · ___ European country · ___ moon · ___ honey in the jar
```

Answers: x · an · the · x · the · a · the · the.

> "Four questions, in order, every time. Name? Countable? Which one? Any one of many? You have the whole system."

### Step 2 — Two titles, one person (3–11 min)

**(a) The puzzle (3–6).** Write one sentence and stop.

```
The Headmaster and Secretary is coming.
```

> "Read it. Something looks strange. What?"

Someone will notice *is* — two people, but a singular verb.

> "Good eyes. It says *is*, not *are*. Why would that be?"

Let them wonder. Then:

> "Because it is **one person**. One man who holds **two jobs** — he is the Headmaster of the school, and he is also the Secretary. One person, two titles."

**(b) The rule (6–9).** Write the pair, one under the other:

```
The Headmaster and Secretary is coming.        →  ONE person, two jobs
The Headmaster and the Secretary are coming.   →  TWO people
```

> "Look only at the articles. In the first, **the** appears **once**. In the second, **the** appears **twice** — once before each title."

> "That is the whole rule. **One *the* = one person. Two *the*s = two people.**"

> "And the verb tells you which is which. *Is* — one. *Are* — two. If the sentence gives you the verb, you already have the answer."

**(c) Practice (9–11).** Class decides aloud, using the verb as the clue:

```
______ reporter and candidate is speaking today.        →  The   (one person)
______ reporter and ______ candidate are speaking.      →  The ... the   (two people)
______ champion and captain of the team is my brother.  →  The   (one person)
______ Headmaster and ______ Secretary were both busy.  →  The ... the   (two people)
```

> "Read to the verb. Then decide."

**Teacher note.** In the exam the paper wrote *(one person)* in brackets to help. Teach the class to look for two clues — the bracket note if it is there, and the verb always. Do not teach any coordination theory beyond this single contrast; the cap allows this pair and nothing more.

### Step 3 — One sentence to recognise (11–14 min)

> "One last sentence. I am not going to teach you a rule for this one — I only want you to have seen it, so it does not surprise you in an exam."

Write it whole, articles already in place:

```
The more you read the Qur'an, the more peaceful you feel.
```

> "Read it aloud with me." Class reads once.

> "It is a pattern English uses to say *when one thing grows, another grows with it*. The more you read, the more peaceful you become. Notice it uses **the** twice, in a special way that is not one of our four questions."

Move on immediately.

### Step 4 — CW-4 Part A, error-find (14–20 min)

Distribute CW-4. Model item 1 on the board.

> "One article in each sentence is wrong. Find it, write the correction, and write **why** in two or three words."

Students work; teacher circulates and corrects at the desk. Take answers aloud at the end, asking "why?" for each.

**Teacher note.** Finding another person's error is harder than filling a blank — this is the block's Analyze work. Expect it to be slower than Part B. If time is short, take fewer answers aloud rather than cutting students' working time.

### Step 5 — CW-4 Part B, mixed exam format (20–26 min)

> "This is exactly how the question comes in your exam."

Read both paper instructions aloud:

> "*Fill in the blanks with appropriate articles.* And on another paper: *Fill in the blanks with a, an or the.* One of your papers also says: *use x if no article is needed.* Whichever wording you meet — the work is the same."

Students work on the sheet; teacher circulates. Take answers aloud with reasons.

### Step 6 — Self-try rehearsal (26–32 min)

**Not collected. Not marked.** This is the rehearsal for Thursday's graded self-try.

**(a) Teacher models one (26–28).** Use *university* — the exemplar students most often get wrong.

Write all four lines on the board while saying them:

> **My word:** university
> **My article:** a
> **Why:** it begins with a *yu* sound
> **My sentence:** *My brother studies at a university in Dhaka.*

> "Four lines. Word, article, reason, sentence. That is the shape."

**(b) Students try one (28–31).** Each student picks a word from the CW-4 word box and fills all four lines.

Three or four read aloud. Give feedback on the **reason** line, not just the sentence — that is the line that earns the reasoning mark.

**(c) The warning (31–32).**

> "Tomorrow you will do this for marks — but **I will give you the word.** You will not choose it. So do not memorise today's sentence; practise the *method*."

> "And one rule: your sentence must be **your own**. Not copied from the worksheet, not copied from the board."

### Step 7 — HW-4 (32–33 min)

Distribute HW-4 — the task line is on the sheet.

| 5 | ___ gratitude | x | cannot count it |

| Reasoning | 1 | line (c) gives a correct reason in the block's own terms |
| Organisation | 1 | complete, readable sentence — the assigned word carries a real meaning in it, not a token slotted in |
| Correct word-class placement | 1 | the article sits immediately before the noun, or before an adjective + noun, that it belongs to — *an honest reporter*, not *honest an reporter* |
| **Total** | **4** | |

Each criterion scores **1 or 0** — no partial credit at this total. A wrong article in line (b) with a correct-sounding sentence still forfeits content accuracy. A copied sentence forfeits content accuracy but may still earn organisation and placement.

**Note for rolls 2 and 6 (x).** Where the assigned word takes **x**, the "article" in line (b) is **x**, and line (d) must show the noun with no article before it. Do not penalise a student for not explaining *why* — the §2 guardrail bars abstract zero-article rule-naming, so *"plain name"*, *"cannot count it"* or *"no article needed"* all earn the reasoning mark.

**Islamic/value alignment is not scored** (Charter §I.3, Run Book §13.1). Charter §H binds every item, prompt and sentence in this block in full — but a student's own sentence is marked on the four criteria above. A religiously neutral, on-topic sentence earns full marks. A sentence that conflicts with §H is flagged and corrected as a values matter, never marked down as a language error.
